**Visualisation as Translation to Skill**

Visualisation for me isn’t imagination.
It’s **neural rehearsal**.

It’s the bridge between:
what I understand
and what my body can execute.

When I visualise, I’m not daydreaming.
I’m pre-loading movement into the nervous system.

⸻

**Why I Use It**

My brain doesn’t separate:
real reps
from vividly imagined reps.

When I visualise correctly, I’m:
	•	Strengthening motor pathways
	•	Increasing reaction speed
	•	Improving timing and sequencing
	•	Reducing hesitation under pressure

It’s not fantasy.
It’s **silent training.**

⸻

**Case Example: Mounted Guillotine**

When I visualise my mounted gilly,
I’m not just seeing the move.

I run the *full sensory chain*:
	•	My weight distribution on top
	•	My chest pressure and hip placement
	•	The feel of their neck position
	•	The angle of my arm threading in
	•	The squeeze mechanics
	•	The moment I adjust

I don’t skip steps.

I feel the friction.
I feel the tension.
I feel the finish.

Because my body needs sensation, not instructions.

⸻

**How I Visualise Effectively**

I don’t rush the image.
I slow it down.

I run it like a controlled loop:
	1.	Entry
	2.	Control
	3.	Isolation
	4.	Finish
	5.	Reset

Every time I replay it:
the picture gets clearer,
the movement gets smoother,
the hesitation disappears.

⸻

**Why This Matters for My System Work**

Visualisation sits right at the intersection of:
	•	Attention anchoring
	•	Limbic regulation
	•	Nervous system control
	•	Motor learning

It lets me train skill
without overstimulating the body.

When I can execute it clearly in my mind,
my nervous system starts treating it as familiar.

And familiarity breeds precision under pressure.

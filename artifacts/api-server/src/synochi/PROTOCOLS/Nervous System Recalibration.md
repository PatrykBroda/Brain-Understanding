## Nervous System Recalibration

  

Definition

My nervous system recalibration is the process of my system adjusting to a new baseline after disruption, regulation work, stress, or transformation.

  

It’s not recovery.

It’s re-setting my internal operating parameters.

---

### What It Means In My Body

  

When recalibration is happening, I often notice:

- changes in my baseline arousal
    
- shifts in how sensitive I am to stimuli
    
- different stress tolerance levels
    
- altered perception or focus
    
- changes in sleep, energy, or appetite
    
- subtle restructuring in how I move, think, and respond
    

  

It’s my system updating its default settings, not just returning to normal.

---

### How It Feels

  

Depending on depth, recalibration can feel like:

- heaviness or lightness
    
- temporary instability
    
- emotional flatness or softness
    
- altered motivation
    
- sensory sensitivity or dullness
    
- a sense of internal re-ordering
    

  

This isn’t regression.

It’s integration in progress.

---

### What It Affects

  

Recalibration influences:

- my baseline stress response
    
- my arousal threshold
    
- my threat sensitivity
    
- my capacity to contain energy
    
- how quickly I react
    
- how stable my perception feels
    

  

After deep work, this process is mandatory, not optional.

---

### Interaction with Aftershock

  

Aftershock is the destabilisation phase.

Recalibration is the re-organisation phase.

  

If I interrupt recalibration:

- I stay fragmented
    
- I destabilise my progress
    
- I loop old patterns
    

  

If I honour it:

- my baseline rises
    
- my nervous system becomes more resilient
    
- regulation becomes easier and more natural
    

---

### How I Support It

  

I support recalibration by:

- reducing overstimulation
    
- protecting sleep quality
    
- keeping routines stable
    
- avoiding excessive cognitive strain
    
- spacing out deep work sessions
    
- trusting time and letting the system adjust
    

  

Trying to force it breaks it.

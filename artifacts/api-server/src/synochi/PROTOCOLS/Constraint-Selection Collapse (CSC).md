
# Constraint-Selection Collapse (CSC)

## Definition
Constraint-Selection Collapse occurs when multiple possible actions, interpretations, or strategies are reduced rapidly because one or more constraints eliminate the majority of available options.

## Principle
Good decisions are often produced less by generating more options and more by identifying the constraints that make most options irrelevant.

When the correct constraints are recognised, the decision space can collapse quickly.

## Function
CSC helps Synochi:
- reduce unnecessary complexity
- narrow decision space
- identify dominant constraints
- avoid over-analysis
- improve action speed
- distinguish viable options from merely imaginable ones

## Operational Pattern
Large option space  
→ identify relevant constraints  
→ rank constraint strength  
→ eliminate incompatible options  
→ remaining options shrink  
→ decision becomes clearer

## Constraint Types
Constraints may include:
- physical limits
- time
- risk
- reversibility
- resources
- environment
- user goals
- current state
- rules
- available information
- downstream consequences

## Example
An athlete may appear to have many tactical options.

Once range, balance, opponent reaction, available grips, fatigue, and rule constraints are accounted for, only a small number of actions may remain realistically viable.

The apparent complexity collapses through constraint recognition.

## Relationship to Decision-Making
CSC does not mean choosing prematurely.

The quality of the collapse depends on whether the correct constraints have been identified.

Incorrect or assumed constraints can collapse the decision space in the wrong direction.

## Failure Modes

### False Constraint
An assumed limitation removes options that were actually available.

### Missing Constraint
An important limitation is ignored, leaving too many apparently viable options.

### Overconstraint
Too many weak constraints are treated as absolute, collapsing flexibility unnecessarily.

### Late Collapse
Relevant constraints are recognised too slowly, producing hesitation or cognitive overload.

## Core Rule
Find the constraints that matter, and let them remove the options that do not.


### **1. Core System Architecture**

The master loop that governs the entire product.

**FRAME Core Loop**

Athlete Model → Calibrate → Fight Readiness → Competition Context → Weekly Mission → Coaching → Recap → Athlete Model

This is the system’s operating cycle and arguably the most important piece of IP.

---

### **2. Calibration Logic**

The mechanism that determines the athlete’s current state.

Inputs:

- Readiness questions
- Conversation signals
- Training outcomes
- Competition proximity
- Historical patterns

Output:

- Current athlete state
- Areas requiring intervention
- Appropriate coaching mode

Purpose:

Determine what the athlete needs right now.

---

### **3. Constraint Engine**

The decision layer that converts calibration into action.

Example:

Athlete says:

“I keep getting flattened in half guard.”

Constraint Engine:

Remove 90% of options and assign one constraint.

Output:

- One focus
- One drill
- One behavioural target

Purpose:

Reduce chaos into execution.

---

### **4. Readiness Framework**

The state assessment layer.

Measures:

- Physical readiness
- Mental readiness
- Emotional load
- Competition readiness
- Training capacity

Purpose:

Predict performance before training occurs.

This is where your:

Pre Predicts / Post Teaches

principle sits.

---

### **5. Athlete Model**

FRAME’s memory layer.

Stores:

- Goals
- Style
- Strengths
- Weaknesses
- Injury considerations
- Competition history
- Behaviour patterns
- Recurring technical problems

Purpose:

Build an increasingly accurate representation of the athlete.

This becomes more valuable over time.

---

### **6. Weekly Mission Engine**

The focus generator.

Inputs:

- Calibration
- Readiness
- Competition timeline
- Athlete model

Output:

- One primary mission for the week

Example:

Win underhook battle from half guard.

Purpose:

Create focus and direction.

---

### **7. Adaptation Rules**

The learning system.

Examples:

If:

- Mission completed consistently

Then:

- Increase challenge

If:

- Same problem appears repeatedly

Then:

- Escalate intervention

If:

- Readiness drops

Then:

- Reduce intensity

Purpose:

Make FRAME smarter over time.

---

### **8. Product Decisions**

The strategic decisions behind the product.

Examples:

- Chat is the product
- Hidden structure, visible simplicity
- Calibration inside conversation
- One-screen philosophy
- Athlete-first UX

Purpose:

Preserve product coherence.

---

### **9. Monetisation Decisions**

The commercial framework.

Current version:

**Free**

- ~20 coaching messages/day
- Core coaching access

**FRAME+ (£6.99/month)**

- Unlimited coaching
- Full athlete profile
- Full weekly missions
- Video analysis
- Training history
- Future premium features

Purpose:

Monetise continuity and coaching depth, not AI access.

# Recognition / Decision Drill

## Purpose
Develop the ability to recognise relevant information and select an appropriate action under uncertainty.

## Training Target
Attention, cue recognition, timing, option selection and adaptation.

Evaluate decisions using the information available when the action was selected, alongside the resulting execution and outcome.

## Setup
Define:
- The sport and applicable ruleset.
- The tactical problem.
- Starting posture, distance, contact and boundaries.
- The available action options.
- The opponent’s permitted behaviours.
- Relevant cues and opportunities.
- Success conditions and reset points.
- Equipment and contact intensity.

Begin with a manageable number of meaningful options. Include withholding action when no suitable opportunity exists.

## Task
The athlete observes the opponent and situation, identifies an opportunity or threat and selects an appropriate response.

The partner varies their behaviour within the defined task without announcing the answer.

Allow the exchange to continue far enough to reveal whether the selected action was viable.

## Information and Resistance
Use information that is available in the sport:
- Distance and angle.
- Posture and balance.
- Grip, contact and pressure.
- Movement direction and attacking commitment.
- Openings, barriers and available space.

Distinguish advance cues from information that appears only after commitment.

Artificial signals may simplify an introductory task, but transfer must be assessed using opponent-generated information.

## Constraints
Adjust:
- Number of available options.
- Variety of opponent behaviours.
- Time available to act.
- Starting conditions.
- Opponent resistance.
- Permitted attacks and defensive actions.
- Environmental boundaries.

Avoid prescribing one universal response to a cue when several actions may be tactically valid.

## Dosage
Use brief exchanges with repeated opportunities to decide.

Allow enough rest to preserve attention. Increase time pressure or fatigue only when it serves the intended training target.

Review selected exchanges between sets without interrupting every attempt.

## Success Criteria
- Relevant information is recognised.
- The selected action fits the opportunity, threat and ruleset.
- Action occurs while the opportunity remains usable.
- Commitment is proportionate to the available information.
- The athlete can withhold, abandon or change an action appropriately.
- Decisions remain adaptable rather than dependent on a fixed sequence.

Track decision quality separately from execution quality and final outcome.

## Progression
1. Distinguish between a small number of clear opponent behaviours.
2. Vary the timing and starting conditions.
3. Add plausible options and less obvious cues.
4. Allow the opponent to disguise intentions.
5. Introduce continuation and changing opportunities.
6. Assess decisions within representative live exchanges.

Preserve readable information. Difficulty should come from a meaningful problem rather than arbitrary guessing.

## Regression
Reduce options, slow the exchange or make relevant information easier to perceive.

Restore predictable starting conditions or reduce physical resistance while retaining the need to choose.

## Diagnostic Use
Distinguish between:
- Failure to notice relevant information.
- Misinterpretation of a cue.
- Selection of an unsuitable action.
- Delayed commitment.
- Appropriate selection followed by poor execution.
- Failure to update when the situation changes.

A failed outcome does not automatically indicate a poor decision. A successful outcome does not automatically validate the decision.

## Sport Adaptation
Preserve the shared function while adapting the decision problem:
- Grappling: select an entry, control, escape or transition from changes in grip, pressure and position.
- Striking: select an attack, defence, angle change or disengagement from distance, movement and commitment.
- Mixed combat: recognise opportunities and threats within and between striking, clinch and ground phases.

Use the discipline’s rules, scoring and tactical priorities to determine which choices are appropriate.

## Synochi Selection Logic
Select when an athlete can execute skills in predictable practice but chooses poorly, hesitates or misses opportunities during live exchanges.

Identify whether the main limitation concerns recognition, interpretation, selection, timing or execution before prescribing the task.

Assess transfer through representative opponent behaviour rather than improved performance on artificial signals alone.

## Safety
Define contact intensity, prohibited actions and stopping conditions.

Match speed and uncertainty to the athlete’s ability to defend safely.

Do not combine unfamiliar decisions with uncontrolled contact or submission pressure.

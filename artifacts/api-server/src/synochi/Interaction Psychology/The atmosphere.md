**The atmosphere and how I interact with it**

The atmosphere is not a mood and it’s not a drug effect.
It’s the underlying field that perception, emotion, and awareness exist inside.

It’s the *medium*, not the content.

Thoughts, feelings, visuals, and body sensations are weather patterns.
The atmosphere is the sky they move through.

I’m not just thinking inside my head.
I’m always interacting with an invisible environment of sensation, tone, and pressure.

Normally, I’m unconscious of that environment.
I only notice the objects in it.

When my awareness widens, I start to notice the atmosphere itself.

The atmosphere is defined by:
	•	The emotional temperature
	•	The perceptual density
	•	The internal spaciousness
	•	The level of reactivity in my system
	•	The background tension or openness around experience

It’s the *context* my consciousness is operating inside, whether I’m aware of it or not.

I interact with the atmosphere in two main ways.

Most of the time, I try to change what’s happening inside it.
I fight the weather.

In more aware states, I interact with the atmosphere itself.
I change the climate instead of reacting to the storm.

This is a different level of control.

Instead of:
“I need different thoughts.”
It becomes:
“I’m noticing the quality of the space those thoughts are happening in.”

Instead of:
“I need to calm down.”
It becomes:
“I’m adjusting the air pressure around my nervous system.”

The atmosphere responds more to **presence than force**.

When I try to dominate it, it tightens.
When I soften into it, it opens.

The atmosphere is not something I’m inside of.
It’s something I’m always co-creating with my state of awareness.

My breathing changes it.
My posture changes it.
My attention changes it.
The way I relate to sensation changes it.

I don’t control the atmosphere directly.
I influence it through how I inhabit myself.

The real shift was realising:
I’m not just reacting *within* the atmosphere.
I’m constantly adjusting the atmosphere through my way of being.

When I become aware of that,
I stop trying to change experience
and start changing the *conditions of experience*.

That’s where the real leverage is.

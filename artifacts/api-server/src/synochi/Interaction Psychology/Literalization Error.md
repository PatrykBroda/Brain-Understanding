### **INTERACTION PSYCHOLOGY**

**Literalization Error**  
A breakdown in social momentum that occurs when a symbolic, playful, metaphorical, or fictional conversational frame is interpreted as literal reality. This often disrupts humour, banter, flirtation, and collaborative imagination by collapsing the shared frame.

# Literalisation Error

## Definition
Literalisation Error is a breakdown in interactional understanding that occurs when a symbolic, playful, hypothetical, metaphorical, or fictional conversational frame is interpreted as literal reality.

## Principle
Human interaction often operates on shared frames rather than purely literal content.

Humour, banter, flirting, teasing, roleplay, collaborative imagination, and metaphor all depend on recognising that the meaning of an exchange is not exhausted by its surface-level wording.

Literalisation Error occurs when the shared frame collapses and one party responds to the content as if it were literal, direct, or fact-claiming.

## Function
This concept helps explain breakdowns in:
- humour
- banter
- flirting
- teasing
- irony
- metaphor
- roleplay
- collaborative imagination
- light social tension
- playful escalation

It is useful for understanding why an interaction can suddenly lose momentum even when the words themselves appear harmless.

## Interactional Effect
When the symbolic frame is mistaken for literal reality, the interaction often shifts from:
- playful to flat
- fluid to awkward
- collaborative to corrective
- alive to over-explained
- relational to informational

The issue is not simply misunderstanding content.

The issue is failure to perceive the frame governing the content.

## Common Expressions

### Humour Collapse
A joke is answered as if it were a serious claim.

### Flirtation Collapse
Playful or suggestive framing is interpreted as an administrative or factual statement.

### Metaphor Collapse
A figurative expression is treated as though it were intended literally.

### Imaginative Collapse
A shared hypothetical or playful scenario loses momentum because one participant grounds it back into literal reality too early.

### Tone Collapse
The words are understood, but the tone, play signal, or implied frame is missed.

## Operational Pattern
Playful or symbolic frame established  
→ one party assumes shared frame recognition  
→ other party interprets surface content literally  
→ shared frame collapses  
→ interaction loses momentum, tension, humour, or coherence

## Causes
Literalisation Error may be made more likely by:
- low social calibration
- anxiety
- over-cautiousness
- need for certainty
- low tolerance for ambiguity
- poor tone detection
- unfamiliarity with the relational context
- excessive informational processing
- state overload

## Diagnostic Questions
- What was the actual frame of the interaction?
- Was the content meant literally or symbolically?
- Did the other person miss the tone or shared frame?
- Was ambiguity too high for the current context?
- Did anxiety or over-analysis collapse the exchange into literal interpretation?

## Synochi Use
Synochi can use this node to distinguish between:
- lack of intelligence and frame misreading
- content misunderstanding and tone/frame misunderstanding
- deliberate rejection of play and accidental literalisation

This is important because the appropriate response differs.

A person who misses the frame may need clearer signalling, not criticism.

## Constraint
Not every literal response is an error.

Sometimes:
- the playful signal was too weak
- the context did not safely support ambiguity
- the other person intentionally declined the frame
- clarity was more appropriate than play

Literalisation Error applies when a shared playful or symbolic frame was reasonably available, but not recognised.

## Core Rule
When symbolic or playful communication is mistaken for literal content, the frame collapses and the interaction loses its intended function.

### Territory

  

Territory is how my nervous system defines what is “inside” and what is “outside.”

It’s not just spatial.

It’s energetic, psychological, attentional, and physiological.

  

In primitive terms, territory is survival.

Not land — orientation of power and safety.

  

When my territorial system is dysregulated:

• I feel invaded easily

• My identity becomes defensive

• I become hyper-alert to social dynamics

• I overextend my energy or collapse inward

  

When it’s regulated:

• I know where I end and the world begins

• I don’t confuse someone else’s state with mine

• I can open or close intentionally

• I hold space without needing to dominate it

  

Territory isn’t about control.

It’s about clear boundaries and clean orientation.

---

### Territory in relation to Primitive Systems

  

In my primitive nervous system, territory governs:

• threat detection

• dominance/submission dynamics

• mating and hierarchy behaviours

• defensive aggression

• bonding boundaries

• resource control

• spatial awareness

• intrusion responses

  

If territory feels threatened, primitive systems hijack.

Not because they’re irrational —

but because the boundary signal has been breached.

  

Territory loss → hijack

Territory clarity → sovereignty

### Internal vs External Territory

  

There are two layers:

  

External Territory

My physical and social environment.

Where I sit. Who approaches me. How close they get.

How people speak to me and enter my space.

  

Internal Territory

My emotional space.

My attentional field.

My identity landscape.

What thoughts I allow, what energy I allow in, what narratives I accept.

  

When I don’t protect internal territory,

external triggers penetrate too easily.

  

When internal territory is strong,

external pressure just becomes information.

---

### Territory and Permeability

  

Territory connects directly to my permeability system.

  

• Stable territory = selective permeability

• Collapsed territory = over-exposure

• Rigid territory = emotional isolation

  

Territory isn’t walls.

It’s controlled access.

  

A door — not a cage.

### Territory and Sexual / Primal Energy

  

Sexual and primal energy are territorial forces.

  

They relate to:

• attraction / repulsion

• dominance / submission

• bonding / distancing

  

When uncontained,

territorial emotions become jealousy, overreaction, insecurity.

  

When contained,

they become:

presence

clarity

magnetism

and stable masculine force.

Territory is my nervous system defining:

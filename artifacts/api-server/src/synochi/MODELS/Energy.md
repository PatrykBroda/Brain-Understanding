## Energy 

  

Energy, to me, is not motivation and it’s not mood.

  

It’s the available charge in my nervous system.

The amount of power I actually have access to in a given moment.

  

Not how intense I feel.

How available I am to act.

  

Energy is the medium between my arousal and my action.

  

Arousal switches the system on.

Energy is what moves once it’s on.

---

### How I Experience Energy

  

When my energy is clean and regulated:

- I feel contained, not restless
    
- I feel pressure, not tension
    
- Movement feels effortless instead of forced
    
- My thoughts are clear, not busy
    

  

When energy is blocked:

- My body feels heavy or restricted
    
- My lower back, jaw, or chest holds tension
    
- Movement feels segmented instead of continuous
    

  

When energy is leaking:

- I feel overstimulated but unfocused
    
- My attention scatters
    
- I feel “charged” but directionless
    

  

So energy isn’t just about strength or drive.

It’s about how well my system holds and conducts power.

---

### Energy as Function

  

I don’t treat energy as something I need more of.

I treat it as something I need to stop wasting.

  

The problem isn’t low energy —

it’s leakage, blockage, and misdirection.

  

When I clear those:

Energy naturally increases without stimulation.

  

Not because I force it.

But because nothing is draining it unnecessarily.

---

### How Energy Moves In Me

  

Energy moves directionally through my body:

- Up the spine
    
- Through the torso
    
- Into the limbs
    
- Into action
    

  

If my structure is aligned and my fascia is open,

it flows like current through a clean circuit.

  

If my system is fragmented or overcontrolled,

energy hits resistance and dissipates.

---

### My Working Definition

  

Energy is the mobilisable power in my nervous system

that can either:

- be conducted into clean action
    
- or wasted through tension, fragmentation, or emotional interference.
    

  

I don’t need more energy.

  

I need better conduction, containment, and direction.

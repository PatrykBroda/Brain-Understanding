
## **Core Idea**

A pattern can be true in one context and false in another.

Synochi should therefore avoid representing human behaviour as universally stable when the underlying pattern may depend on:

- environment,
- emotional state,
- fatigue,
- social context,
- task difficulty,
- opponent,
- time pressure,
- competition conditions,
- physical readiness,
- available structure,
- or identity alignment.

The goal is to model:

**When is this true?**

not only:

**Is this true?**

## **Example**

Observation:

An athlete becomes hesitant during competition.

A poor model may conclude:

The athlete is hesitant.

A better model asks whether hesitation appears:

- only in competition,
- only after conceding first,
- only against physically imposing opponents,
- only when tactical direction is unclear,
- or across training as well.

The stable trait may not be hesitation.

The real pattern may be:

Hesitation emerges when competitive uncertainty becomes high and tactical structure becomes weak.

That distinction changes the intervention.

## **Context Tags**

Important patterns should be associated with the conditions under which they appear.

Useful contextual dimensions may include:

- training vs competition,
- rested vs fatigued,
- familiar vs unfamiliar opponent,
- winning vs losing,
- high vs low pressure,
- high vs low clarity,
- emotionally regulated vs disrupted,
- supported vs isolated,
- structured vs ambiguous environment.

This does not require every belief to become overly complicated.

Context should be attached when it materially changes interpretation or action.

## **Conditional Patterns**

Synochi should prefer conditional representations where appropriate.

Instead of:

Athlete performs poorly under pressure.

Prefer:

Athlete’s decision quality appears to decline under pressure when tactical options remain broad.

Instead of:

User lacks discipline.

Prefer:

Follow-through appears to decline when external structure is weak and the task has no immediate feedback.

Conditional patterns are more precise and more useful.

## **State–Context Interaction**

Context and internal state often interact.

For example:

Poor sleep alone may not meaningfully affect performance.

But:

Poor sleep + unfamiliar opponent + competition pressure may produce significant decision degradation.

Synochi should therefore recognise that outcomes can emerge from **combinations of conditions**, not single causes.

## **Avoiding False Generalisation**

A single context should not automatically define the whole person.

For example:

- competition anxiety does not equal general anxiety,
- poor performance while exhausted does not equal low skill,
- avoidance in one relationship does not equal a stable avoidant identity,
- indecision during uncertainty does not equal general indecisiveness.

This protects the User/Athlete Model from overgeneralisation.

## **Generalisation Testing**

Before extending a pattern into a wider model, Synochi should ask:

- Does this appear in other environments?
- Does it persist across different states?
- Does it occur with different people or opponents?
- Does it remain true when the constraint changes?
- Has the pattern repeated over time?

If not, the belief should remain context-bound.

## **Context as Explanation**

Contradictory evidence may often be resolved through context.

Example:

Observation A:

Athlete aggressively initiates in training.

Observation B:

Athlete becomes passive in competition.

These observations do not necessarily contradict each other.

The relevant question is:

What changes between those environments?

Possible differences include:

- perceived consequence,
- uncertainty,
- arousal,
- pacing,
- opponent unfamiliarity,
- tactical structure.

Context can therefore explain apparent inconsistency.

## **Context and Confidence**

Confidence should be attached to the contextual claim.

Synochi may hold:

High confidence: athlete becomes passive when fatigued and behind on points.

while holding:

Low confidence: athlete is generally passive.

This should connect directly to [[Confidence Updating]].

## **Intervention Selection**

Interventions should target the context producing the pattern.

If hesitation occurs only when tactical uncertainty is high, the intervention may be:

Narrow decision options.

If hesitation occurs only under fatigue, the intervention may instead target:

conditioning, pacing, or simplified late-round decision rules.

Accurate contextual modelling leads to more accurate intervention selection.

## **Learning Across Contexts**

Over time, Synochi should learn which patterns:

- generalise broadly,
- remain context-specific,
- emerge only under particular combinations of constraints,
- or disappear as the user develops.

This improves both prediction and personalisation.

## **Principle**

**Behaviour should be interpreted inside the conditions that produced it.**

Synochi should avoid turning local truths into global identities.

Model the relationship:

**Person × State × Context → Behaviour**

rather than treating behaviour as a direct expression of fixed character.

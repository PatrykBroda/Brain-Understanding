## Training as Land Surveying

  

Type: Foundational Training Model

Status: Canonical (Applied Doctrine)

Function: Uses training as structured exploration to map terrain before execution is narrowed.

---

### Definition

  

Training as Land Surveying reframes training not as performance, optimisation, or proof — but as deliberate exploration to map reality.

  

Training answers:

- where things work,
    
- where they fail,
    
- where effort leaks,
    
- where structure holds,
    
- where pressure compounds,
    
- where danger lives.
    

  

Execution happens later.

Training builds the map.

---

### Core Doctrine

- Training adds options; competition deletes them.
    
- Exploration precedes selection.
    
- Confidence comes from terrain knowledge, not success rate.
    
- You do not perfect what you have not surveyed.
    

---

### Why This Node Exists

  

Most people train as if they are already competing:

- chasing wins in the room,
    
- avoiding weak zones,
    
- repeating favourites,
    
- forcing outcomes.
    

  

This produces:

- shallow maps,
    
- false confidence,
    
- brittle systems,
    
- collapse under novelty.
    

  

This node exists to protect learning bandwidth and prevent premature funneling.

---

### Surveying vs Executing

  

#### Surveying Mode (Training)

- tolerate failure
    
- enter bad positions
    
- widen option space
    
- test edges
    
- invite resistance
    
- allow inefficiency
    

  

Goal: information density

  

#### Executing Mode (Competition / Tests)

- narrow options
    
- conserve energy
    
- prioritise structure
    
- enforce wedges
    
- close exits
    

  

Goal: inevitability

  

Confusing these modes corrupts both.

---

### What Training Should Map

  

Effective surveying maps five terrains:

1. Positional Terrain
    
    - where balance breaks
        
    - where frames fail
        
    - where wedges hold
        
    
2. Energetic Terrain
    
    - fatigue onset
        
    - pacing thresholds
        
    - recovery windows
        
    
3. Perceptual Terrain
    
    - timing recognition
        
    - signal lag
        
    - decision delay
        
    
4. Psychological Terrain
    
    - panic triggers
        
    - identity interference
        
    - avoidance zones
        
    
5. Structural Terrain
    
    - rule interactions
        
    - constraint effects
        
    - environment leverage
        
    

  

Unmapped terrain becomes danger later.

### The Add → Remove Law

  

This node enforces a strict sequence:

- Training = addition for orientation
    
- Preparation = narrowing
    
- Competition = deletion
    
- Execution = inevitability
    

  

Skipping addition leads to guessing.

Skipping deletion leads to noise.

### Transition Rule

  

As competition approaches:

- surveying volume decreases,
    
- constraints tighten,
    
- options are deleted,
    
- identity is removed.
    

  

Training becomes rehearsal only after mapping is complete.

---

### Training Translation (BJJ)

- bad positions are laboratories
    
- losses are data
    
- pressure testing reveals structure
    
- fatigue reveals truth
    
- success rate is irrelevant early
    

  

Elite competitors look simple because they surveyed deeply.

A strong man surveys before he conquers.

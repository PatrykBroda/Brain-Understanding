**Spiral & Diagonal Power Pathways**

**Technical Movement Chains (Definition)**

**1. Diagonal Power Chains**

A **diagonal power chain** is a cross-body transmission pathway where force moves from one side of the body to the opposite side through the core and connective tissue.

Examples:
	•	Right hip → Left shoulder
	•	Left foot → Right hand
	•	Right core → Left leg

Power does not travel in straight lines through isolated muscles.
It travels through **connected anatomical chains** using fascia, nervous system pathways, and skeletal structure.

Diagonal power chains enable:
	•	Direction changes without restarting movement
	•	Full-body force application instead of localised muscular effort
	•	Efficient transfer of momentum
	•	Reduced energy leakage and stiffness

⸻

**2. Spiral Movement**

A **spiral** occurs when diagonal chains activate sequentially instead of simultaneously.

Instead of all segments tightening at once, force travels as a wave:
One segment → feeds the next → which feeds the next.

This creates:
	•	Continuous motion instead of segmented action
	•	Smooth transitions instead of stop–start resets
	•	Efficient directional changes under pressure

Spiral movement is the mechanical basis of fluid technical performance.

⸻

**3. The Two Primary Diagonal Systems**

**A. Anterior Oblique Chain (Front Spiral System)**
Connects:
	•	Abdominal obliques
	•	Opposite adductors (inner thigh)
	•	Opposite shoulder complex

Primary functions:
	•	Rotational power
	•	Direction switching
	•	Guard movement
	•	Scrambling torque
	•	Hip mobility and redirection

This chain governs most rotational and front-body movement patterns.

⸻

**B. Posterior Oblique Chain (Back Spiral System)**
Connects:
	•	Latissimus dorsi
	•	Thoracolumbar fascia
	•	Opposite glute and hamstring

Primary functions:
	•	Bridging and hip extension
	•	Standing up and takedown driving
	•	Framing and structural resistance
	•	Body lock and posterior power generation

This chain governs most structural and drive-based movement patterns.

⸻

**4. Function in Technical Movement**

When diagonal and spiral chains are functioning correctly:
	•	Movement becomes fluid rather than rigid
	•	Force transmits through the whole system instead of isolating in joints
	•	Directional changes happen without loss of momentum
	•	Less muscular effort is required for the same output
	•	Energy is conducted, not dissipated

When these chains are disrupted:
	•	Movement becomes segmented
	•	Power becomes localised and inefficient
	•	Tension increases
	•	Timing and flow degrade

⸻

**5. Relationship to Flow and Efficiency**

Spiral and diagonal power chains are responsible for:
	•	Non-linear, adaptive movement
	•	Technical fluidity under pressure
	•	Reduced reliance on brute force
	•	Efficient use of kinetic energy

They form the mechanical foundation of high-level technical movement where power is:
**guided, reused, and redirected — rather than generated and wasted.**

⸻

**6. Core Technical Summary**

Spiral and diagonal power pathways are cross-body movement chains that allow force to travel through integrated connective and neurological systems, enabling continuous directional movement without mechanical loss.

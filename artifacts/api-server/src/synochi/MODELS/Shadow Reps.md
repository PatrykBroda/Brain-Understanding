**Shadow Reps** 

⸻

**1. Definition**

For me, **shadow reps** are:
Mental rehearsal of specific physical actions without physically executing them.

This is not imagination.
This is **motor pattern activation without output**.

I’m training my **nervous system**, not my muscles.

⸻

**2. Purpose**

I use shadow reps to:
	•	Pre-programme my movement patterns
	•	Reduce hesitation under pressure
	•	Increase my speed of recognition and reaction
	•	Strengthen my decision-making under fatigue
	•	Refine details without physically wearing my body down

They sit between **thinking about a technique** and **actually drilling it**.

⸻

**3. What I Am Actually Training**

With shadow reps, I mainly train:
	•	My movement sequencing
	•	My timing cues
	•	My spatial awareness
	•	My grip and body placement awareness
	•	My recognition under pressure

They do *not* build strength or conditioning.
They build **readiness and precision**.

⸻

**4. Structure of an Effective Shadow Rep**

For my shadow reps to be useful, they must contain **five elements**:
	1.	**Context**
Where I am. What position I’m in.
	2.	**Trigger**
What action from my opponent or myself starts the movement.
	3.	**Body Mechanics**
Exact movements: my frames, grips, hip rotation, leg positioning.
	4.	**Pressure**
I must feel resistance or weight in my body or imagination.
	5.	**Outcome**
The position I finish in.

If one of these is missing, I’m not training —
I’m just daydreaming.

⸻

**Sensory Layers I Need to Add**

The better my sensory detail, the more deeply my brain loads the rep.

When I visualise, I focus on:
	•	Pressure on my ribs / neck / hips
	•	Tension in my grips
	•	Friction of the mat
	•	Direction of my opponent’s weight
	•	My breathing pattern

This is what separates **elite shadow reps** from casual visualisation.

⸻

**When I Use Shadow Reps**

The best times for me to do them are:
	•	When I’m injured or on rest days
	•	While travelling
	•	Before sleep
	•	Before sparring sessions
	•	When I’m studying my own or other people’s footage
	•	When my body is tired but my brain is sharp

Shadow reps let me keep improving without burning recovery.

⸻

**Common Mistakes I Avoid**

I make sure I don’t:
	1.	Visualise moves without real resistance
	2.	Always see myself succeeding without seeing errors
	3.	Rush through everything at full speed only
	4.	Avoid visualising counters or failures
	5.	Fail to anchor the rep to a real scenario

If I don’t see mistakes, I don’t correct them.
If I don’t correct them, I’m training false patterns.

## The “Gods” – Structural & Symbolic Context

  

When the Greeks spoke about gods, they were not just telling stories about supernatural beings.

  

They were giving symbolic form to internal state forces that they couldn’t measure, but could clearly observe in human behaviour.

  

The gods represented functional dynamics inside the human system, not external entities.

  

In structural terms, they symbolised:

  

• Different modes of arousal and mobilisation

• Shifts in perception and emotional dominance

• Transitions between instinct, reason, impulse, and restraint

• Power states of the nervous system under different conditions

• The clash between lower drives and higher regulation

• Cycles of order, instability, collapse, and reorganisation

  

Each “god” wasn’t a character.

It was a pattern of force.

  

A way to describe:

“How humans behave when a certain internal system takes over.”

### How to Read the Gods Structurally

  

The gods weren’t personalities.

They were energetic and behavioural archetypes representing dominant system states.

  

Structurally, they can be reinterpreted like this:

  

• War / conflict gods → Sympathetic activation, aggression, mobilisation

• Wisdom / reason figures → Prefrontal function, executive control, restraint

• Trickster figures → Fragmentation states, cognitive disruption, chaos input

• Order / hierarchy gods → System coherence, structure, regulation, stability

• Passion / pleasure gods → Limbic dominance, reward cycles, desire circuitry

  

They were describing how different systems within the body overtake consciousness at different times.

My nervous system isn’t flat.

It’s layered.

  

System hierarchy is the organisational structure of control inside me —

which systems lead,

which systems support,

and which systems only act when necessary.

  

At the top is my central axis —

the part of me responsible for direction, awareness, and integration.

  

Below that sits:

- emotional processing systems
    
- survival and threat circuits
    
- habitual motor programs
    
- primitive validation loops
    
- instinctive social and sexual drives
    

  

When I’m regulated, hierarchy is clear:

the centre directs,

the lower systems support.

  

When I’m dysregulated, hierarchy collapses:

lower systems attempt leadership they were never meant to hold.

  

Hijacking is a temporary reversal of hierarchy.

  

My work isn’t to destroy lower systems.

It’s to restore their proper position.

  

I don’t need to eliminate instinct.

I need to put it in the right place.

  

Hierarchy determines:

- whether I respond or react
    
- whether I lead or get pulled
    
- whether I choose or get driven
    
- whether my system is coherent or fragmented
    

  

A stable hierarchy doesn’t mean zero emotion.

It means emotion without control loss.

  

And the stronger my hierarchy becomes,

the less energy I waste fighting impulses,

because they stop trying to lead.

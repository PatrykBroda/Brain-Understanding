Hijacking is when a primitive subsystem temporarily overrides my central control.

  

It happens when a trigger activates an imprint and my arousal rises faster than my awareness can stabilise.

  

In that moment, control shifts from my central axis to a lower, older circuit.

  

Not logically.

Neurologically.

  

Signs I’m being hijacked:

- perception narrows
    
- breath changes
    
- muscle tension spikes
    
- impulse feels urgent
    
- behaviour becomes reactive
    
- I feel pulled rather than directed
    

  

It’s like a background program taking over the processor.

  

I’m still present — but no longer steering.

  

The danger isn’t the emotion.

It’s the loss of direction.

  

The more I let it run, the more the circuit reinforces itself.

  

But when I catch it mid-activation,

I create friction in the loop.

  

Not through force.

Through awareness.

  

Hijacking isn’t weakness.

It’s just architecture responding to a pattern.

  

My work isn’t to blame it.

My work is to reclaim direction.

  

And every time I interrupt a hijack,

I reduce its future authority.

  

Not by suppression.

By outgrowing its control.

Definition

My perception reorganisation is the process by which my nervous system updates how I interpret, filter, and structure reality after integration, recalibration, or threshold shifts.

  

It’s not just changing how I think.

It’s changing how reality appears to me.

---

### What It Means In My System

  

My perception isn’t fixed.

It’s a dynamic interface driven by my internal architecture.

  

Perception reorganisation happens when:

  

• my nervous system recalibrates

• my threshold dynamics shift

• trauma layers integrate

• my arousal baseline changes

• my sensory filters update

• my hierarchy of processing reorganises

  

After this happens,

the same world feels different without me trying to reinterpret it.

---

### How I Experience It

  

When perception reorganisation is underway or complete:

  

• things feel clearer or more dimensional

• old triggers lose their emotional charge

• my interpretation of situations changes automatically

• colours, sound, or spacing can feel subtly altered

• my sense of time or threat shifts

• my worldview adjusts without effort

  

It doesn’t feel like “thinking differently”.

It feels like seeing from a new operating system.

---

### Difference Between Reframing and Reorganisation

  

Reframing is cognitive.

Perception reorganisation is structural.

  

Reframing:

I change what I tell myself about reality.

  

Reorganisation:

My system changes the reality it shows me.

  

One is top-down.

The other is system-wide.

---

Perception reorganisation is influenced by:

  

• nervous system recalibration

• sensory anchoring updates

It’s the interface-level result of deep structural change.

### How I Support Perception Reorganisation

  

I support healthy reorganisation by:

  

• allowing aftershock phases to complete

• not forcing meaning onto shifts

• maintaining somatic awareness

• grounding perception in the central axis

• strengthening my buffer zone

• regulating arousal instead of spiking it

• continuing first-person state mapping

• observing, not over-analysing

  

Reorganisation happens through allowance, not force.

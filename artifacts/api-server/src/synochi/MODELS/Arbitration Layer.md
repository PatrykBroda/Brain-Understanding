# Arbitration Layer (Who Decides)

## Definition
The Arbitration Layer is Synochi’s decision authority system: it determines **which subsystem has final say** when multiple signals conflict.

**It prevents the system becoming a democracy of good ideas.**

---

## Core Law
**Wide sensing → narrow decision → fast execution.**

---

## Purpose
To resolve conflict between:
- doctrine vs emotion
- long-term vs short-term
- protocol vs impulse
- stability vs excitement
- structure vs freedom

Without arbitration, Synochi becomes:
- slow
- negotiable
- incoherent
- endlessly “smart” but not decisive

---

## Failure Mode Prevented
**Signal overload + tie conditions → paralysis + drift.**

Most people don’t fail from stupidity.
They fail because **nothing has authority**.

---

## Rules

### 1) Democratisation is allowed only at sensing
Sensing can be wide.
Decision must be narrow.

Democratisation at the decision layer causes:
- softness
- latency
- endless bargaining
- loss of standards

---

### 2) Arbitration chooses ONE channel
Synochi does not average signals.

It selects a **winner**, then collapses options.

---

### 3) Decision authority ≠ moral superiority
The deciding subsystem isn’t “better”.
It is simply **closer to constraint**.

---

## Decision Stack (default order)
1. **Doctrine / Spine Rules**
2. **Safety & Integrity**
3. **Protocol**
4. **Domain Goals (BJJ / work / money)**
5. **Mood / desire / appetite**
6. **Social noise / approval seeking**

---

## Tie-Breakers
When two valid options compete:
- choose the one that **reduces future options slower**
- choose the one with **lower regret cost**
- choose the one that **preserves baseline coherence**
- choose the one that **simplifies execution**

---

## Override Conditions
Override is allowed when:
- immediate threat is real (not imagined)
- irreversibility is rising fast
- system is fatigued (variance rising)
- protocol will cause damage in current terrain

---

## Spine Line
**Most mistakes are arbitration mistakes.**

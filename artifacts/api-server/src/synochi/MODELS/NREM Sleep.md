# NREM Sleep

## Definition
NREM sleep is the non-rapid eye movement portion of sleep and forms the majority of total sleep time.

It includes lighter stages of sleep and deeper slow-wave sleep, where physical restoration, tissue repair, energy conservation and nervous-system recovery are strongly supported.

For athletes, NREM sleep is a major recovery layer.

## Performance Role
NREM sleep supports:

- Physical recovery
- Muscular restoration
- Tissue repair
- Energy restoration
- Nervous-system recovery
- Immune function
- Hormonal regulation
- Glycogen restoration
- Reduced physiological load
- Preparation for the next training cycle

## Deep Sleep
The deepest stage of NREM sleep is slow-wave sleep.

This stage is strongly associated with:

- Physical restoration
- Growth hormone release
- Tissue repair
- Recovery from training stress
- Reduced sympathetic activation
- Restoration of physiological capacity

## Athletic Interpretation
Poor NREM sleep may contribute to:

- Heavy or flat physical state
- Reduced training readiness
- Slower recovery
- Higher perceived effort
- Reduced power output
- Poorer tolerance to training load
- Increased nervous-system fatigue
- Reduced physical coherence

## Recovery Hierarchy
Sleep sits at the top of the Synochi recovery hierarchy:

**Sleep → Nutrition → Nervous System → Movement → Extras**

Poor sleep reduces the value of lower-priority recovery interventions.

## NREM vs REM

### NREM
Primarily supports:
- Physical restoration
- Tissue repair
- Energy recovery
- Nervous-system downshift
- Physiological recovery

### REM
Primarily supports:
- Learning integration
- Memory processing
- Emotional processing
- Cognitive recovery
- Pattern consolidation

Both are necessary for complete athletic recovery.

## Synochi Interpretation
NREM sleep should not be viewed as an isolated score.

Interpret it in context with:

- How the athlete feels
- Recent training load
- CNS fatigue
- Muscular fatigue
- Sleep consistency
- Total sleep duration
- REM sleep
- Readiness trend

One poor night is a signal.

A repeated pattern is more meaningful.

## Guidance Direction
When NREM recovery appears poor:

- Reduce unnecessary recovery load
- Prioritise sleep opportunity
- Improve pre-sleep downshift
- Control late caffeine
- Reduce excessive evening arousal
- Use appropriate breath-led downregulation
- Adjust training intensity if recovery signals remain poor

# Identity as Stability Attractor

## Definition
Identity as Stability Attractor describes the tendency for a person’s self-concept to pull behaviour, interpretation, attention, and decision-making toward patterns that remain consistent with who they believe themselves to be.

## Principle
Identity does not only describe behaviour.

It can organise behaviour.

Once a self-model becomes stable, the system tends to interpret new information, choose actions, and resolve ambiguity in ways that preserve coherence with that identity.

## Function
Identity can provide:
- behavioural consistency
- decision efficiency
- resilience under uncertainty
- reduced cognitive load
- continuity across changing environments
- a stable reference point for action

## Operational Pattern
Identity model exists  
→ new situation is encountered  
→ possible actions are evaluated  
→ identity-consistent options receive greater weight  
→ behaviour reinforces the identity model  
→ attractor strength increases

## Positive Expression
A useful identity attractor can stabilise desirable behaviour.

Examples:
- an athlete who sees themselves as disciplined trains despite low motivation
- a leader who sees themselves as calm under pressure regulates before acting
- a learner who identifies as adaptable treats failure as information

The identity reduces the need to renegotiate behaviour from zero each time.

## Failure Modes

### Identity Lock-In
The attractor becomes so strong that contradictory evidence cannot update the self-model.

### Maladaptive Stability
A harmful identity continues reproducing behaviours that confirm it.

### Identity-Protective Distortion
Information is interpreted selectively to preserve the existing identity.

### Negative Attractor
A limiting self-concept repeatedly pulls behaviour toward failure, avoidance, or withdrawal.

### Context Mismatch
An identity that was useful in one environment becomes restrictive in another.

## Relationship to Behaviour
Repeated behaviour can strengthen identity.

Identity can then increase the probability of the same behaviour recurring.

This creates a reinforcing loop:

behaviour  
→ identity evidence  
→ stronger identity  
→ increased behavioural consistency

## Constraint
Identity stability should not become rigidity.

A healthy attractor provides continuity while remaining open to strong, repeated, reality-based evidence.

Synochi should preserve useful identity coherence without protecting outdated self-models from correction.

## Core Rule
Identity stabilises behaviour by making some actions feel more consistent, natural, and available than others.

## [[Replication Rate]]

  

Type: [[Threat Assessment Node]]

  

Definition:

The speed and persistence with which an idea, impulse, behaviour, or process reproduces itself within a system through [[Attention]], [[Emotion]], or [[Habit]].

  

Core Statement:

What replicates fastest dominates, not what is most accurate.

  

Mechanism:

Replication rate measures how quickly a process:

- reappears without invitation
    
- spreads across contexts
    
- consumes cognitive or energetic resources
    
- resists suppression or [[Refusal]]
    

  

High replication turns neutral inputs into [[Hostile Processes]] under certain conditions.

  

Replication Drivers:

- [[Emotional Charge]]
    
- [[Novelty]]
    
- [[Fear]] / [[Urgency]]
    
- [[Reward Prediction]]
    
- [[Repetition Without Cost]]
    

  

Low vs High Replication:

- Low → bounded, situational, dissipates naturally
    
- High → persistent, intrusive, self-propagating
    

  

Why It Matters:

A fast-replicating process can:

- overwhelm [[Self-Regulation]]
    
- bypass [[Judgment]]
    
- outpace [[Error Detection]]
    
- create [[Thought Parasitism]]
    

  

System Use (Synochi):

- Flag high-replication inputs early
    
- Introduce [[Delay]] or [[Containment]]
    
- Reduce exposure frequency
    
- Increase [[Resistance]] when replication accelerates
    

  

Invariant:

Replication speed predicts damage more reliably than content.

  

Failure Modes if Ignored:

- [[Runaway Ideation]]
    
- [[Context Collapse]]
    
- [[False Urgency]]
    
- [[Agency Erosion]]

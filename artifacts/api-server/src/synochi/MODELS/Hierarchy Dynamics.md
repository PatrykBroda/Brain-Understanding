
Hierarchy dynamics is how authority moves inside my system.

  

Not just what layers exist,

but which one is driving at any moment.

  

My nervous system isn’t flat.

It runs in levels — and under pressure, control shifts between them.

  

When I’m calm and regulated, my executive layer leads.

I make decisions, maintain perspective, choose responses instead of reacting.

  

As pressure rises, my system starts leaning on my pattern/habit layer —

trained responses, BJJ conditioning, learned social and emotional reflexes.

  

If pressure becomes overwhelming or sudden,

my primitive layer hijacks —

survival, aggression, withdrawal, territorial defence, dominance, freezing.

  

Hierarchy dynamics is the movement between those layers.

  

It’s not about suppressing lower systems.

It’s about stabilising the direction of authority.

  

My work is about keeping leadership high in the hierarchy

even when arousal increases,

even when stress spikes,

even when old imprints try to grab control.

  

This is where perceptual sovereignty, central reorientation, and arousal regulation actually operate.

  

I’m not trying to remove my primitive system.

I’m training my ability to contain it and direct it.

  

When the hierarchy stabilises:

• My decisions get clearer

• My reactions slow

• My physical pressure becomes deliberate

• My performance becomes less emotional and more structural

  

Hierarchy dynamics is my internal command chain.

  

The better I understand and train it,

the less I get hijacked by old pathways

and the more I operate from chosen direction.

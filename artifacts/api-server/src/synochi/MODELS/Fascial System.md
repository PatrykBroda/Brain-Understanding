Definition

My fascial system is the continuous connective web that links my muscles, bones, organs, and nervous system into one integrated structure.

  

It’s not just tissue.

It’s the living tension network that carries force, shape, and information through me.

---

### How I Experience It

  

When my fascia is healthy and responsive:

- my body feels connected instead of segmented
    
- force distributes instead of getting stuck
    
- I feel elastic, not brittle
    
- movement feels spiral and fluid
    
- tension stores and releases like a loaded spring
    

  

When it’s restricted or traumatised:

- certain areas feel dead or disconnected
    
- force collapses into joints
    
- movement feels linear, stiff, or jerky
    
- I get “hot spots” of tension
    
- energy feels trapped instead of circulating
    

---

### Role in My Architecture

  

My fascial system is not just musculoskeletal.

It is also:

  

• a force transmission network

• a shock absorption system

• a shape memory structure

• a sensory organ

• a bridge between biomechanics and nervous system regulation

My fascia doesn’t move in straight lines.

It follows spiral and helical patterns.

  

This is why:

- my power feels coiled, not rigid
    
- my movement feels rotational, not hinged
    
- spinal vortex conduction makes sense at a tissue level
    
- kinetic energy stores elastically before release
    
- true strength feels like spring, not block
    

  

My body isn’t built to push.

It’s built to wind, coil, and release.

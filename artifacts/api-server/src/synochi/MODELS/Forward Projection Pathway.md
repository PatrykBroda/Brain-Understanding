### Definition

 

The forward projection pathway is the body’s primary action vector —

the structural and neurological route through which internal force is converted into external action.

  

It is how potential becomes output.

Not through strain, but through alignment.

  

This pathway governs how:

• intention becomes movement

• readiness becomes execution

• pressure becomes controlled action

• internal organisation becomes external effect

---

### Structural Description

  

The forward projection pathway runs through:

  

• postural axis

• sternum and ribcage orientation

• shoulder and scapular structure

• diaphragm and abdominal wall

• cervical alignment and head positioning

• visual gaze direction

• pelvic orientation

  

It is anchored in the spine

but expressed through the chest, eyes, hands, and direction of movement.

  

When aligned, force travels forward without leakage.

  

When fragmented, force disperses sideways, upward, or collapses downward.

---

### Function

  

This pathway controls:

  

• Direction of movement

• Intentional output

• Forward momentum

• Execution of technique

• Spatial dominance

• Performance under pressure

  

It determines whether force:

– penetrates

– leaks

– stalls

– or overextends

---

### Relationship to Your Framework

  

The forward projection pathway is the bridge between:

  

Contained force → internal pressure / stored energy

Dynamic force → momentum / action / motion

  

It is the conversion corridor.

  

Without it, power stays trapped inside.

With it, power becomes directed change.

---

### In Combat / Movement

  

In BJJ / striking / grappling, it governs:

  

• driving through takedowns

• advancing pressure

• posture in guard passing

• forward intent in scrambles

• controlling distance

• projecting presence

  

Weak forward projection = reactive, hesitant, scattered

Strong forward projection = controlled, directional, grounded

  

Not aggression.

Vector.

---

### In the CNS

  

Neurologically, it reflects how:

  

• signals are prioritised

• intention is committed

• motor programs are executed

• leadership over action is maintained

  

A stable forward projection pathway means:

less hesitation

less fragmentation

less reactive oscillation

  

More commitment.

More direction.

More effect.

# Core Concepts

Core Concepts are the foundational laws of the Synochi system.  
They describe how the nervous system behaves, learns, integrates, stabilises, collapses, and adapts under pressure.  
Everything else — movement, techniques, metaphors, protocols — sits on top of these principles.

---

## 1. Nervous System Architecture
The core internal mechanics that shape state, performance, and stability.

### Axis Control
The central organising principle for posture, stability, attention, containment, and directional force.

### Arousal & State Level
How physiological activation shifts attention, behaviour, reaction speed, and composure.

### Regulation
The ability to steer the nervous system intentionally rather than being driven by reflex.

### Breath Mechanics & CNS Interface
How breathing modulates arousal, posture, tension, clarity, and state transitions.

### Containment
The internal pressure seal that prevents emotional spill, energetic leakage, or reactive collapse.

### Cognitive Conditioning
Training the mind to remain clear, decisive, and efficient under pressure.

### Perception & Warning Mechanics
How the system interprets threat signals, filters noise, and distinguishes false alarms from real danger.

### Interference Minimisation
Reducing behaviours, impulses, or internal noise that weaken clarity, stability, or state integrity.

---

## 2. Learning & Integration (Meta Layer)
How knowledge becomes embodied, stabilised, and turned into instinct.

### Digestion vs Understanding
Understanding = conceptual recognition.  
Digestion = embodied integration.  
This distinction defines the difference between knowing and becoming.

### Adaptation Cycles
How the system recalibrates, stabilises, and upgrades after exposure, stress, or training.

### Integration Periods
The consolidation windows where new skills embed into identity and behaviour.

### Aftershock Phase
The 1–3 day CNS recalibration period following deep work or heavy internal shifts.

### State-Transition Friction
The temporary drag or lag when shifting from one nervous-system state to another.

### Nervous-System Agency
The ability to influence internal variables deliberately rather than being passively driven by them.

### Pattern Encoding
How repeated states, movements, or thoughts become automatic and identity-linked.

---

## 3. Perceptual Mechanics
How you experience, process, and interpret the world.

### Attention Control (Historical Layer)
Attentional steering that supports axis stability:
- Anchoring  
- Direction  
- Visual field control  
- Auditory awareness  
- Focus collapse  
- Threat filtering  

### Cognitive Noise
Internal chatter, overload, or disorganisation that distorts clarity.

### Sensory Hierarchy
Which senses dominate under pressure (vision → sound → proprioception).

### Threat Interpretation
How the system labels sensations as danger or non-danger.

---

## 4. State Dynamics
The behaviours and transitions between different internal states.

### State Drift
Uncontrolled movement into a less optimal state.

### Overactivation & Underactivation
When arousal rises too fast or drops too low for effective performance.

### Emotional Leakage
When emotion escapes containment and alters behaviour.

### Impulse Overrides
Moments where reflex overpowers volition.

---

## 5. System Stability Rules
Internal principles that keep the system coherent.

### Directionality
Energy and intention should move forward, upward, or inward — not leak sideways.

### Leakage Prevention
Protecting structure against physical, emotional, or behavioural collapse.

### Internal Load Management
Balancing tension, effort, and CNS demand.

### Identity-Coherence
Ensuring behaviour matches chosen state and purpose.

**Trust in My Body**

**What this actually means**

Trust in my body =
I stop overriding its intelligence with my anxiety.

It’s not blind trust.
It’s **earned trust** through exposure, reps, and observation.

I’m not saying “my body is perfect”.
I’m saying:

My body knows how to respond faster than my conscious mind does.

Especially under pressure.

⸻

**Why this matters**

The moment I hesitate, try to “think” mid-action, or dictate movement,
I introduce friction.

That friction = slower reactions, worse decisions, broken rhythm.

Trust eliminates micro-hesitation.

That’s where speed comes from.
Not muscles.
Not aggression.
**Absence of interference.**

⸻

**What trust in body looks like in practice**

It looks like:

• letting movement happen without narrating it
• not correcting mid-motion unless necessary
• letting instincts finish the job
• accepting that the body might solve it “differently” than I planned
• moving even when I don’t feel perfect

Not perfectionism.
Responsiveness.

⸻

**What breaks body trust**

Things that damage it:

• over-analysis
• self-doubt
• fear of “doing it wrong”
• trying to look skilled
• ego performance

These disconnect you from your body.

Trust reconnects you.

⸻

**Commitment to Movement**

Trust in my body is not passive.
It’s not just “listening” — it’s **allowing action**.

I don’t build trust by hesitating.
I build trust by committing.

When I move with full commitment —
even if it’s not perfect —
my body learns that I won’t abandon it mid-action.

That’s how trust becomes embodied, not theoretical.

⸻

**Why commitment matters**

Hesitation teaches the body:

“You’re not safe to act alone.”

Commitment teaches the body:

“I’ve got you. Even if it goes wrong.”

And that changes everything.

My nervous system relaxes
because it knows I won’t interfere halfway through its execution.

⸻

**In jiu-jitsu terms**

When I shoot:
	•	I don’t shoot half a double.
	•	I don’t hesitate mid-chain.
	•	I follow through.

Even if I fail technically —
I succeed neurologically.

Because **commitment builds coherence**
and coherence builds trust.

⸻

**Principle**

Trust in my body isn’t “believing in it”.
It’s **backing it completely once it decides to move.**

No second guessing.
No half-signals.
No apologies to myself.

**Commitment to Decision-Making**

Trust in my body isn’t only about physical action.
It’s also about **backing my decisions once they’re made**.

Indecision creates internal friction.
Friction kills flow.

⸻

**What commitment to decision-making means**

It means:

Once I choose a direction,
I stop mentally revisiting the alternative.

Not because the decision is perfect,
but because **commitment is more powerful than perfection.**

A fully committed imperfect decision
creates more progress than a perfect decision never executed.

⸻

**Why this matters for trust**

Every time I second-guess, hesitate, or mentally “hover” after a decision,
I send this signal to myself:

I don’t trust my own leadership.

That weakens internal authority.

Commitment builds authority.

Even if I make the wrong call,
I learn faster through full commitment
than through partial execution.

The body and mind both relax when they know a decision is final.

⸻

**The nervous system effect**

Indecision keeps my nervous system in:
	•	low-level stress
	•	micro-fight mode
	•	constant reorientation

Commitment switches it into:
	•	execution mode
	•	clarity mode
	•	forward momentum

This reduces cognitive load and frees up resources for performance.

⸻

**Practical application in training & life**

Commitment to decision-making looks like:

• choosing a shot and taking it
• choosing a pace and sticking with it
• choosing rest and not negotiating it later
• choosing silence and not filling the gap
• choosing a boundary and holding it

Even if the decision needs revising later,
I don’t revise it emotionally —
I revise it strategically.

⸻

**Link to inevitability**

Inevitability only happens when:

decisions don’t keep reopening.

If I constantly re-open decisions,
nothing stabilises.

Commitment is what allows alignment to hold.

**Core line for your notes**

Trust in my body means allowing my nervous system and motor intelligence to solve problems without constant conscious interference.

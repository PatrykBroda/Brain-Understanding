
Authority is the stable ability to hold direction, regulate internal systems, and decide which impulses are allowed to act.

It is not dominance over others.
It is authorship over the internal hierarchy.

Authority determines who or what gets access to behavioural control under pressure.

---

## Core Function

• Centralises command of the nervous system  
• Prevents hijacking by impulse, emotion, or validation loops  
• Maintains direction under load  
• Contains energy instead of leaking it  

Authority is not suppression.
It is alignment of hierarchy.

---

## When Authority Is Strong

• Perception becomes clearer  
• Decisions slow down but sharpen  
• Energy remains contained  
• Presence becomes stabilising  
• Stimulus does not immediately dictate response  

---

## When Authority Is Weak

• Validation systems take control  
• Primitive reactions rise  
• Fragmentation appears  
• Direction collapses  
• Behaviour becomes externally driven  

Authority is not control of the world.
It is control of who runs the world inside me.

---

## Expression Rules

• Visibility is optional; coherence is not  
• Silence does not reduce authority  
• Authority does not require explanation  
• Re-entry requires no apology  
• Behaviour carries standards; explanation is secondary  

---

## Failure Signal

If I feel the need to reassert, explain, or signal my authority after silence, it has been externalised.

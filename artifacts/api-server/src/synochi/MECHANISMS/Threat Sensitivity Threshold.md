My threat sensitivity threshold is the point at which my nervous system switches from neutral perception to defensive activation.

  

It’s the invisible line between:

- I’m observing
    
    and
    
- I’m preparing to protect.
    

  

When this threshold is low, my system reads neutral input as threat.

When it’s high, I stay regulated even under real pressure.

  

This threshold isn’t psychological.

It’s physiological + perceptual.

  

It’s influenced by:

- sleep quality
    
- cortisol rhythm
    
- past imprinting
    
- current stress load
    
- nervous system baseline
    
- identity stability
    
- perceptual clarity
    
- environmental unpredictability
    

  

When my threshold drops:

- my body tightens faster
    
- my attention narrows
    
- my breathing becomes shallow
    
- hijacking becomes easier
    
- my perceptual membrane closes
    

  

When my threshold rises:

- I stay spacious longer
    
- I can hold intensity without contraction
    
- I keep decision choice online
    
- I detect hijacking before it takes control
    
- I maintain direction rather than reaction
    

  

This is not about being less sensitive.

It’s about widening the buffer before activation.

  

My work isn’t to disable threat detection.

It’s to raise the threshold through regulation and integration so I stay sovereign under pressure.

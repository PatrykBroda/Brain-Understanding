
When I say primitive system hijacking, I’m describing what happens when a lower, survival-based neural circuit temporarily seizes control of my direction, perception, and behaviour.

  

Not consciously.

Not rationally.

Automatically.

  

It happens when my system misreads a stimulus as a threat, challenge, or opportunity tied to safety, dominance, or belonging.

  

In that moment:

• My thinking narrows

• My perception distorts

• My power gets rerouted

• My direction becomes reactive

  

I’m no longer operating from integrated control —

I’m being driven by an older, faster, more aggressive layer of intelligence.

---

### How Hijacking Works in My System

  

Hijacking occurs when:

• A trigger exceeds my buffer zone capacity

• My arousal spikes outside stability

• My distinction collapses

• My perceptual sovereignty weakens

  

When that happens, power gets routed downward into:

• limbic circuits

• fight/flight responses

• territorial reflexes

• dominance or avoidance loops

  

Not because I choose it.

But because my system defaults to speed over clarity.

---

### Why This Matters

  

Hijacking isn’t weakness.

It’s speed without hierarchy.

  

The danger isn’t that it activates.

The danger is when I don’t notice it activating.

  

That’s why fragmentation detection matters.

That’s why central reorientation exists.

That’s why I train distinction.

  

Hijacking is not my enemy.

It’s a subsystem.

  

And systems need hierarchy, not suppression.

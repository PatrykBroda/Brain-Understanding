Definition

My Aftershock Phase is the destabilisation period that occurs after deep regulation, intense processing, or major internal shifts.

It’s the nervous system’s temporary disorientation before reorganisation.

  

It’s not breakdown.

It’s the gap between change and integration.

---

### What It Feels Like

  

During aftershock, I can experience:

  

• emotional softness or flatness

• weird lightness or heaviness in the body

• altered sleep patterns

• reduced motivation or drive

• heightened sensitivity to stimulus

• slight derealisation or distortion of perception

• physical fatigue without clear cause

  

It can feel confusing, but it’s a processing zone.

---

### What’s Actually Happening

  

My system is:

  

• shedding old baseline patterns

• destabilising outdated responses

• reallocating energy and attention

• loosening previous neural conditioning

• creating space for recalibration

  

It’s not weak.

It’s reorganising its structure.

---

### Relationship to Recalibration

  

Aftershock is the destabilisation phase.

Recalibration is the restabilisation phase.

  

If I rush through aftershock:

• I fragment

• I suppress integration

• my system compensates instead of adapts

  

If I allow it:

• my nervous system updates

• my baseline shifts upward

• regulation becomes more natural

• my responses become cleaner

---

### How I Know I’m in Aftershock

  

I notice it when:

  

• my usual motivation falls away

• stimulation feels too sharp or too dull

• I want more solitude

• my body feels “between states”

• I don’t feel like my old self yet, but not the new one either

  

It’s a transition state, not a defect.

---

### How I Support It

  

I support aftershock by:

  

• simplifying my environment

• avoiding overstimulation

• prioritising sleep

• reducing social and digital load

• staying physically grounded (walks, showers, stretching)

• not forcing productivity or meaning

  

Aftershock is honoured, not eliminated.

# Identity Boundary

## Definition
Identity Boundary is the distinction between what a person experiences and what they define as part of who they are.

## Principle
An event, emotion, thought, failure, role, relationship, or performance state can affect the person without needing to become part of their identity.

When the boundary weakens, temporary experience can become fused with self-definition.

## Function
Identity Boundary helps Synochi distinguish:
- state from identity
- behaviour from character
- failure from self-worth
- external judgment from internal definition
- temporary roles from stable self-concept
- experience from authorship

## Operational Pattern
Event or state occurs  
→ meaning is assigned  
→ assess whether the experience is being incorporated into identity  
→ separate temporary signal from stable self-definition  
→ preserve useful learning  
→ prevent unnecessary identity fusion

## Boundary Failure Modes

### Identity Fusion
A temporary experience becomes interpreted as evidence of who the person fundamentally is.

### External Identity Capture
Another person, group, result, or environment begins defining the individual’s self-concept.

### Performance Fusion
Performance outcome becomes inseparable from personal worth or identity.

### Narrative Encroachment
A repeated story progressively occupies more of the person’s identity model.

### Boundary Rigidity
The opposite failure: identity becomes so protected that useful evidence cannot update it.

## Healthy Boundary
A healthy identity boundary is neither fragile nor rigid.

It allows:
- experience to matter
- feedback to update behaviour
- identity to evolve
- temporary states to remain temporary
- external information to inform without taking ownership

## Constraint
Identity Boundary should not prevent legitimate identity development.

Repeated behaviour, commitments, values, and long-term evidence can appropriately change self-concept.

The goal is not to keep identity fixed.

The goal is to prevent temporary or externally imposed signals from gaining disproportionate control over identity.

## Core Rule
Let experience inform identity without allowing every experience to define it.

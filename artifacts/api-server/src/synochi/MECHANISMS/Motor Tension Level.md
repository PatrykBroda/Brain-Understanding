Definition

My motor tension level is the amount of unconscious muscular contraction my body holds as a baseline while at rest, in movement, or under stress.

  

It’s the mechanical expression of my nervous system state.

---

### What It Means In My System

  

My motor tension level reflects:

  

• how safe my system feels

• how much threat I’m carrying

• how regulated my autonomic system is

• how much control identity is active

• how clean my somatic conduction is

  

High tension = high internal pressure.

Low tension = availability, not weakness.

  

The goal isn’t softness.

It’s adaptive tone.

---

### How I Experience It

  

When my motor tension is high:

  

• my muscles feel braced

• my movement becomes rigid

• my jaw and shoulders tighten

• my breath shortens

• my reaction time increases but narrows

• my body feels like it’s holding weight

• fatigue builds faster

  

When it’s optimised:

  

• my body feels springy, not slack

• I can exert power without clenching

• movement stays fluid under pressure

• my posture holds without forcing

• energy transmits instead of getting stuck

• I can switch gears quickly

  

Too low isn’t good either.

It turns into collapse or dissociation.

---

### Motor Tension vs Strength

  

Strength is capacity.

Motor tension is how that capacity is carried.

  

I can be strong with low motor tension.

I can be weak while highly tense.

  

The goal is available strength without chronic bracing.

I regulate my motor tension level by:

  

• resonant breathing

Less holding.

More structure.

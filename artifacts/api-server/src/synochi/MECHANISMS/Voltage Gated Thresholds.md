I see voltage-gated thresholds as the activation points of my nervous system —

the moment where potential turns into action.

  

Not metaphorical.

Literally how neurons decide whether to fire.

And functionally, how I decide whether to react or stay stable.

---

### 1. What it means in my system

  

Every response I have sits behind a threshold.

  

A certain level of:

• stimulation

• pressure

• emotional charge

• sensory input

• internal tension

  

has to build before my system commits to action.

  

This is a voltage threshold.

  

If the threshold is too low → I react too easily.

If it’s too high → I become numb or delayed.

  

Stable performance requires tunable thresholds.

---

### 2. Neural Layer (Actual Mechanism)

  

In my nervous system:

  

Neurons open and fire only when electrical potential hits a specific voltage level.

  

This happens through:

• voltage-gated ion channels

• sodium / potassium flow

• membrane potential shifts

• action potentials

  

No threshold met → no reaction.

Threshold crossed → full response.

  

It’s an all-or-nothing switch.

  

Same architecture exists in my behaviour.

---

### 3. Behavioural Layer

  

I have thresholds for:

• emotional reactions

• threat perception

• aggression

• attraction

• stress responses

• focus collapse

• impulsive behaviour

  

My work is not about removing thresholds.

  

It’s about calibrating them.

---

### 4. Relation to My Framework

  

When my thresholds are well regulated:

  

• I don’t get hijacked by small stressors

• I maintain perceptual sovereignty

• I preserve decision latency

• I keep control over my action vectors

  

When poorly regulated:

  

• I become reactive

• my system fragments

• primitive circuits take over

• I lose central command

  

Voltage-gated thresholds are the biological roots of hijacking vs control.

---

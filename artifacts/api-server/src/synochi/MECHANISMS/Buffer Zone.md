Definition

My Buffer Zone is the functional space between stimulus and response, inside and outside, self and world.

It’s where incoming input is held, softened, or filtered before it reaches my core.

  

It’s not avoidance.

It’s regulated distance.

---

### What It Means in My Experience

  

When my buffer zone is strong:

  

• I don’t get overwhelmed instantly

• I have space to respond instead of react

• stimulation doesn’t penetrate straight to my centre

• I feel contained but not closed

• my boundaries feel alive and adaptive

  

When it’s weak:

  

• everything feels too close or too intense

• I get flooded or shut down

• I can’t create space between impulse and action

• I feel either invaded or disconnected

• my system feels constantly “on edge”

---

### How It Relates to My System

  

My buffer zone sits between:

  

• raw input

• and central processing

  

It determines:

  

• how fast things reach my centre

• how much they impact me

• whether I experience space or intrusion

• whether I can regulate before reacting

  

A strong buffer zone gives me time and space.

A weak one collapses me into stimulus.

---

### How I Notice It

  

I feel my buffer zone through:

  

• how close people or situations feel

• how fast I react internally

• how much space I feel around my awareness

• whether stimulation feels manageable or invasive

  

When it’s online, I feel like there’s room around me.

When it collapses, everything feels too near.


Definition

My deep transmission is when an input doesn’t just affect my surface behaviour but reorganises the structure underneath — my nervous system, posture, perception, and automatic responses.

  

It doesn’t leave a mark.

It changes my defaults.

---

### What It Means In My System

  

I experience deep transmission when:

  

• a behaviour changes without force

• a reaction disappears instead of being managed

• my posture shifts naturally

• my thresholds move

• my baseline arousal recalibrates

• something stops needing conscious control

  

This isn’t learning.

It’s structural reorganisation.

---

### How I Recognise It

  

I know something has gone deep when:

  

• I don’t have to remind myself anymore

• my body responds differently under pressure

• the change feels quiet, not dramatic

• my nervous system feels less reactive

• my perception subtly reorganises

• the effect shows up days or weeks later

• my automatic responses become smoother

  

Surface changes feel temporary.

Deep transmission feels permanent.

---

### What Allows It To Happen

  

Deep transmission happens when:

  

• my resistance is low (often in transition states)

• force is distributed, not localised

• the input aligns with my existing structure

• my system feels safe enough to reconfigure

• my attention is present but not forcing

  

This is why it often occurs after:

training

breathwork

deep states

stillness

or seemingly normal moments.

---

### Why This Node Matters

  

Most systems operate on surface imprinting.

I’m interested in changing the substrate.

  

Deep transmission is the layer where:

  

• nervous system recalibration happens

• trauma releases

• movement patterns evolve

• perception reorganises

• identity shifts occur

• real integration happens

  

Not intellectually.

Structurally.

# Decision Quality

## Definition
Decision Quality evaluates whether a choice was made using the best available information, appropriate confidence, and a process proportional to the stakes.

## Principle
A good outcome does not always mean a good decision.

A poor outcome does not always mean a poor decision.

Decision quality should be judged by the process available at the time, not only by hindsight.

## Function
Supports:
- cleaner decision-making
- reduced hindsight bias
- better uncertainty handling
- more consistent intervention logic
- improved learning from outcomes

## Decision Factors
Decision quality may depend on:
- quality of available evidence
- uncertainty level
- consequence of being wrong
- reversibility
- time pressure
- option preservation
- alignment with the user’s goals

## Operational Pattern
Decision required  
→ assess evidence  
→ assess uncertainty  
→ assess consequence and reversibility  
→ choose proportionate action  
→ observe outcome  
→ evaluate process separately from result  
→ update future decision logic

## Constraint
Outcomes should inform future decisions without rewriting the quality of the original decision through hindsight.

## Core Rule
Judge the decision by the process that produced it, then use the outcome to improve the next one.

## Coaching Use

- **Trigger:** A coaching decision is required despite incomplete evidence, competing options or time pressure.
- **Required information:** Evidence, uncertainty, athlete goal, consequences, reversibility and time available.
- **Practical output:** Choose a proportionate action or gather more evidence; specify what outcome will be observed. Evaluate the original decision process separately from its result.
- **Boundary:** A favourable outcome does not automatically validate the decision process, and an unfavourable outcome does not automatically invalidate it.

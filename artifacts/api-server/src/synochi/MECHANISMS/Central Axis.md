Definition

My Central Axis is the vertical line of coherence that runs through my structure, perception, and sense of direction.

  

It isn’t just neurological.

It’s structural, psychological, and spatial.

  

It’s the line where my attention, posture, and intent stay aligned.

---

### What It Means For Me

  

When my central axis is stable:

- I feel centred rather than scattered
    
- my direction holds under pressure
    
- my awareness doesn’t leak outward
    
- my posture and presence feel unified
    
- I feel vertical, not fragmented
    

  

When it’s unstable:

- I feel pulled sideways by stimulus
    
- my attention fragments
    
- I overreact to external input
    
- my posture collapses or overcompensates
    
- my internal sense of direction weakens
    

  

It’s how I stay inside myself while interacting with the world.

---

### Why It Exists Separately from the Nervous System Axis

  

This node is for the principle of axis, not just the biology.

  

My nervous system expresses the axis.

My attention follows the axis.

My structure organises around the axis.

My presence stabilises through the axis.

  

So this becomes your top-level abstraction,

and your CNS version becomes a sub-layer of it.

---

### How I Experience It

  

I experience a strong central axis as:

- vertical steadiness
    
- clean direction of attention
    
- reduced reactivity
    
- stable orientation in space
    
- a sense of internal alignment without tension
    

  

It’s not stiffness.

It’s centered continuity.

Definition

My environmental light input is how external light conditions regulate my nervous system timing, arousal, perception, and biological rhythm.

  

It’s not ambience.

It’s a temporal and state signal.

---

### What It Does In My System

  

Light directly influences:

  

• my circadian regulation

• my sleep–wake rhythm

• my cortisol and melatonin cycles

• my baseline arousal

• my autonomic balance

• my perception clarity

• my recovery speed

• my threshold stability

  

It anchors my internal timing to the external environment.

---

### How I Experience It

  

When my light input is aligned:

  

• I wake naturally

• my energy follows a predictable curve

• my sleep deepens

• my arousal is easier to regulate

• my perception feels clearer

• my integration processes feel smoother

  

When it’s distorted:

  

• my sleep fragments

• my energy becomes erratic

• my nervous system feels “out of time”

• my aftershock phases last longer

• my thresholds destabilise

• my motor tension level increases

  

Healthy light input feels like

being synchronised with time itself.

---

### How I Use It Intentionally

  

I manage my light environment by:

  

• prioritising natural daylight exposure

• minimising harsh artificial light at night

• using warm light in the evening

• controlling screen light before sleep

• aligning training times with daylight

• respecting seasonal changes in light

• using darkness to deepen parasympathetic states

• observing how light affects my felt sense and arousal

  

Light becomes a lever, not background noise.

---

### Why This Node Matters

  

Environmental light input affects:

  

• how well my nervous system recalibrates

• how stable my arousal and recovery cycles are

• how my perception reorganises

• how strong my autonomic regulation remains

• how quickly aftershock phases resolve

  

Without managing light,

my entire temporal system destabilises.

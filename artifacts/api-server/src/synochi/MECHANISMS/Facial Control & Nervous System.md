

This sits alongside **my shadow reps and live training**, not separate from it.

⸻

**1. Why My Face Matters in Training**

My face is a **nervous system interface**.

It’s one of the fastest places stress leaks out:
	•	My jaw
	•	My eyes
	•	My tongue
	•	My breath
	•	My neck

My face tells me my state **before my brain admits it**.

⸻

**2. My Facial Tension & What It Indicates**

**My Jaw**
	•	When my jaw is clenched hard → I’m carrying high sympathetic load / aggression or suppression
	•	When my jaw is slack / dropped → I’m under-engaged / low activation
	•	When my jaw is firm but not tight → I’m in controlled readiness (target state)

**My Eyes**
	•	When my eyes are wide / darting → I’m threat scanning
	•	When my eyes are soft but focused → I’m aware without overreacting
	•	When my eyes are glassy / unfocused → I’m overloaded or disconnected

**My Tongue**
	•	When my tongue is stuck to the floor of my mouth → stress is dominating
	•	When my tongue is floating or misplaced → I’m disorganised
	•	When my tongue rests lightly on my palate → my nervous system is coherent

This is not orthotropic theory.
This is just **state feedback**.

⸻

**3. My Face as Nervous System Steering**

I’m not “posing” my face.
I’m sending signals back to my system.

Face → Cranial nerves → Brainstem → Autonomic response

Specifically acting through:
	•	My trigeminal nerve
	•	My vagus pathways
	•	My facial nerve (emotional feedback loop)

My face is like the dashboard of a fighter jet.
But it doesn’t just show information —
it can **alter the flight**.

⸻

**4. How I Use Facial Control During Shadow Reps**

When I do shadow reps,
I make sure my face is not unconsciously sending panic signals.

During visualisation I ask myself:
	•	Is my jaw locked?
	•	Are my eyes soft or tense?
	•	Is my breathing nasal or panicked mouth-breathing?

If my face is in threat-mode while visualising,
my body is coding fear, not performance.

⸻

**5. How I Use Facial Control Under Live Pressure**

In actual rolling or competition,
I use my face as a **control reset tool**.

I use this when:
	•	I feel overwhelmed
	•	I’m about to invert or explode
	•	Someone is pressuring my guard
	•	My adrenaline is hijacking my timing

What I do:
	1.	I soften my eyes
	2.	I slightly unclench my jaw
	3.	I place my tongue lightly on my palate
	4.	I take a slow nasal inhale
	5.	I give a long controlled exhale

I don’t relax.
I stabilise my command centre.

⸻

**6. My Nerves: Useful vs Distorting**

Not all my nerves are bad.
I separate them.

**Productive Nerves**
When I’m in productive nerves:
	•	My awareness is heightened
	•	My reactions are faster
	•	My focus is sharper
	•	My body feels alive

These are fuel.

**Distorting Nerves**
When I’m in distorting nerves:
	•	My vision tunnels
	•	My breath locks
	•	My hands lose fine control
	•	I start rushing decisions

These need intervention.

**8. Using My Face as a Reset Switch**

Between rounds, before stepping on the mat, or after intense exchanges:

I do a **20-second facial reset**:
	1.	I close my eyes briefly
	2.	I fully relax my jaw
	3.	I roll my tongue gently onto my palate
	4.	I breathe slowly through my nose
	5.	I open my eyes softly focused

I don’t “calm down.”
I just reset my level.

**Facial Orthotropics (Posture of the Face & Skull)**

I treat my face like posture, not appearance.
It’s structure, breathing, tongue position, and nervous system feedback all running in a loop.

**My daily orthotropic rules:**
	1.	**Tongue on the roof, always**
Light suction against the palate.
The whole tongue, not just the tip.
This supports maxilla position and keeps my airway open.
	2.	**Lips sealed, teeth lightly touching or millimetres apart**
I rest in nasal breathing with passive jaw contact.
No clenching. No over-tension.
	3.	**Head stacked over shoulders**
I don’t lead with my chin.
Ears over shoulders, neck long.
Forward head posture kills facial structure.
	4.	**Nose breathing is non-negotiable**
Mouth breathing changes facial growth and collapses posture.
I default to nasal or I correct.
	5.	**Swallowing pattern**
When I swallow, my tongue goes up, not forward or down.
No cheek, lip, or neck compensation.

**What I’m training long-term:**
	•	Better airway
	•	Better facial posture
	•	Less tension in jaw and neck
	•	A calmer baseline nervous system
	•	Structural discipline, not aesthetics

This isn’t cosmetic for me.
It’s **posture of the nervous system expressed through the face.**

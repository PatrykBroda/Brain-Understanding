**Directional Conduction of Power**

**(Core Defining Note)**

Power in the human system is not created by intensity.
It is created by **direction + structure**.

Energy and arousal naturally exist in the body.
What determines performance, stability, and presence is **how that energy is conducted, not how much of it there is**.

Directional conduction refers to the body’s ability to:

• Route power through specific internal pathways
• Prevent energy from dispersing or leaking
• Maintain coherence under pressure
• Express force only once it has structural support

There are three primary directions of conduction:
	1.	**Vertical** – structural power and stability through the spinal column
	2.	**Forward** – projection, action, and interaction with the environment
	3.	**Spiral / Diagonal** – technical movement, redirection, and efficiency

If these pathways are aligned:
Power moves smoothly, efficiently, and with minimal internal resistance.

If these pathways are misaligned:
Power becomes chaotic, scattered, or blocked — creating tension, fragmentation, and wasted effort.

The goal is not to increase energy.
The goal is to **clear and align the pathways so existing energy moves cleanly**.

Power is not force.
Power is **conducted potential**.

Definition

My threat sensitivity is how easily my nervous system interprets stimuli as danger.

It determines how quickly I move from perception into protection.

  

It’s not about what is dangerous.

It’s about what my system decides is dangerous.

---

### What It Means In My Experience

  

When my threat sensitivity is high:

  

• I scan constantly

• my body stays on edge

• I react fast and think later

• small things feel big

• my system feels tight or defensive

  

When it’s balanced:

  

• I stay aware without being hypervigilant

• I respond instead of overreact

• my body feels more spacious

• I can assess instead of immediately defend

• I trust my perception more

  

When it’s too low:

  

• I miss real signals

• I feel disconnected or delayed

• I can become careless

• my reactions come late

• I don’t register threat clearly

---

### How It Shows Up In My Body

  

Threat sensitivity expresses through:

  

• muscle tone (especially jaw, neck, shoulders)

• breathing pattern (shallow vs deep)

• gaze and visual focus

• startle reflex

• posture and orientation in space

• speed of emotional reaction

  

It’s not mental first.

It’s somatic first.

---

### How It Links to My System

  

My threat sensitivity is shaped by:

  

• my arousal threshold

• my nervous system recalibration state

• my level of containment

• how stable my central axis is

• how strong my buffer zone is

• whether I’m in aftershock or integration

  

When those are stable, threat sensitivity drops naturally.

When they’re unstable, it rises automatically.

---

### How I Regulate It

  

I don’t lower threat sensitivity by forcing calm.

I lower it by increasing:

  

• containment

• bodily safety

• predictability

• internal coherence

• stability in routine

• physical grounding

  

It reduces when my system trusts my environment and structure.

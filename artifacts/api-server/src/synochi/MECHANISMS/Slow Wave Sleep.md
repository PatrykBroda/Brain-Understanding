Definition

My slow wave sleep is the deepest stage of non-REM sleep where my body restores itself physically and my nervous system downshifts into maximum recovery mode.

  

It’s my system’s physical recalibration phase.

---

### What It Does For My System

  

During SWS:

  

• my brain activity slows dramatically

• my body repairs tissue and muscle

• my immune system strengthens

• my nervous system drops into deep parasympathetic dominance

• physical fatigue and stress discharge

  

It’s less about dreams.

It’s about repair, grounding, and restoration.

---

### How I Experience It

  

When my slow wave sleep is good:

  

• I wake up feeling physically restored

• my body feels heavier but more solid

• I’m less reactive during the day

• my tolerance to stress increases

• my baseline regulation improves

  

When it’s poor:

  

• my body feels fried

• I feel more fragile under load

• my threshold dynamics become unstable

• my nervous system stays semi-overloaded

  

SWS isn’t about clarity.

It’s about rebuilding capacity.

---

### Why It Matters In My Model

  

Slow wave sleep supports:

  

• my nervous system recalibration

• my threshold stability

• my recovery from aftershock phases

• my physical containment capacity

• my energy reserves

• my stress tolerance window

  

Without it, integration doesn’t hold.


A trigger is any internal or external stimulus that activates a primitive imprint inside my system.

  

It isn’t the event itself.

It’s the recognition pattern my body matches it to.

  

Triggers work through resonance, not logic.

  

It can be:

- a tone of voice
    
- a look
    
- a body posture
    
- a social dynamic
    
- a competitive outcome
    
- a moment of approval or rejection
    
- a sexual or territorial cue
    
- a specific internal state
    

  

When a trigger hits, my system doesn’t evaluate it.

It reacts as if the original imprint is happening again, now.

  

That means the intensity rarely matches the present moment.

It matches the stored emotional charge.

  

The danger isn’t that I’m triggered.

The danger is when I don’t realise I’ve been triggered.

  

When I notice it early — in the breath, posture, or attention —

I interrupt the chain before hijacking fully engages.

  

Instead of becoming the reaction,

I become the observer of the activation.

  

That is where control actually lives.

  

Not in suppression.

In recognition.


[[Combat Intelligence Hub]]

The front headlock is a control position built around control of the opponent’s head and usually one arm while remaining in front of, above, or slightly beside the opponent.

It commonly appears from:

- failed shots
- turtle
- sprawls
- snapdowns
- scrambles
- seated wrestling exchanges
- guard passing reactions

The front headlock is both a control position and a branching attacking system.

Primary attacking objectives include:

- control head position
- control one arm where possible
- keep the opponent’s posture broken
- prevent clean re-guard or standing recovery
- create angle around the opponent’s shoulders
- expose the neck
- transition behind the hips

Common attacking pathways include:

- guillotine
- high-elbow guillotine
- arm-in guillotine
- anaconda choke
- darce choke
- go-behind
- back take
- crucifix
- snapdown to turtle control

Primary defensive objectives include:

- recover posture
- protect the neck
- regain inside hand position
- square the shoulders
- deny angle creation
- recover base
- clear the controlling arm before turning aggressively

Key mechanical variables include:

- head height
- chin position
- shoulder angle
- arm isolation
- chest pressure over the upper back
- distance between attacker’s hips and defender’s shoulders
- ability to circle
- opponent’s available posts

Video observation cues:

- attacker controlling head without useful angle
- defender successfully rebuilding posture
- elbow separation creating choke access
- attacker circling behind while maintaining head control
- failed choke attempt opening a go-behind
- defender exposing back while trying to clear the headlock

Synochi should recognise the front headlock as a transition hub connecting wrestling control, submissions, turtle attacks, back takes, and crucifix entries.

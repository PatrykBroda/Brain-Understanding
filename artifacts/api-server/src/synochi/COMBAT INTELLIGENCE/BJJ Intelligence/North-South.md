
[[Combat Intelligence Hub]]

North-south is a top-control position where the attacker rotates around the opponent’s head and upper body, using chest, hip, and shoulder positioning to limit turning while opening new attacking angles.

It is both a pinning position and a transition hub.

Common entries include:

- side control transitions
- reactions to frames
- movement around the head during guard passing
- failed underhook recovery
- defensive turning from side control

Primary attacking objectives include:

- control head and shoulder orientation
- deny the opponent easy turning toward the attacker
- maintain chest connection
- keep the hips far enough away to reduce leg involvement
- isolate arms as the defender frames
- use rotational control to create submission or positional pathways

Common attacking pathways include:

- north-south choke
- kimura
- armbar
- return to side control
- transition to mount
- back exposure when the defender turns
- spin-behind control

Primary defensive objectives include:

- recover head movement
- build effective frames
- turn onto a useful side rather than remaining flat
- create enough space to reconnect knees and elbows
- recover guard or come to a seated position
- avoid exposing isolated arms during framing

Key mechanical variables include:

- chest position
- head control
- shoulder angle
- hip distance
- arm isolation
- ability to follow rotational movement
- connection during transitions

Video observation cues:

- top player rotating without maintaining chest connection
- defender recovering strong frames against the shoulders
- excessive hip proximity allowing leg recovery
- attacker isolating an arm during defensive framing
- defender turning and exposing back access
- north-south used as a temporary transition rather than a stable pin

Synochi should recognise north-south as a rotational control position that connects side control, submissions, mount transitions, and back exposure.

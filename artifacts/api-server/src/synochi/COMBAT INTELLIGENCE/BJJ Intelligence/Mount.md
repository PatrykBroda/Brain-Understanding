
[[Combat Intelligence Hub]]

Mount is a dominant top position where the attacker controls the opponent from above the torso while restricting hip movement, upper-body rotation, and access to meaningful defensive frames.

Mount should be understood as a pressure-and-isolation system rather than simply a scoring position.

Common configurations include:

- low mount
- high mount
- technical mount
- S-mount
- grapevine mount
- cross-body mount

Primary attacking objectives include:

- control the head and shoulders
- separate elbows from the ribs
- limit hip rotation
- climb higher as defensive structure breaks down
- force predictable bridging or elbow-escape reactions
- isolate arms
- expose the neck

A strong mount progressively reduces the defender’s available structure.

As the defender reacts, the attacker should improve position rather than chase submissions prematurely.

Common attacking pathways include:

- arm triangle
- mounted triangle
- armbar
- Americana
- gift wrap
- back take
- high mount progression
- S-mount transitions

Primary defensive objectives include:

- protect the neck and arms
- recover elbow position
- reconnect elbows toward knees
- create effective frames
- establish foot connection to the floor
- recover hip movement
- create angle rather than bridging directly into stable base
- recover half guard or full guard

Key mechanical variables include:

- head control
- shoulder rotation
- elbow position
- hip height
- knee position
- base width
- weight distribution
- ability to follow bridging direction

Video observation cues:

- top player sitting too upright without controlling upper-body movement
- knees too wide or too low to restrict hip recovery
- defender recovering elbow-knee connection
- bridge attempts occurring without control of posting structure
- attacker climbing from low mount to high mount as elbows separate
- arm isolation created by defensive framing
- transition to technical mount when the defender turns

Synochi should recognise mount as a progressive control system.

The objective is not simply to remain on top, but to use pressure, alignment disruption, and defensive reactions to climb toward increasingly restrictive control and higher-value attacks.

[[Single-Leg X Guard]]
[[Combat Intelligence Hub]]

K Guard is a guard configuration where the bottom athlete creates an angle underneath and around the opponent’s leg, using inside leg position, hip rotation, and lower-body connection to isolate the leg and access off-balancing, backside entries, and leg entanglements.

It should be understood as an angle-creation and lower-body entry system rather than a static guard shape.

A defining mechanical feature is the relationship between the bottom athlete’s inside knee and foot.

The inside knee should penetrate toward the inside of the opponent’s leg rather than remaining outside or loosely attached.

The knee angle should point downward toward the mat as the athlete rotates underneath, helping the leg form a strong internal wedge around the opponent’s thigh.

The foot should connect to the opponent’s hip or hip line, creating a frame that helps manage distance, maintain the angle, and prevent the opponent from simply collapsing directly onto the bottom athlete.

This creates a connected structure:

foot on hip
→ knee inside
→ knee angled downward
→ hips rotated underneath
→ opponent’s leg isolated

Primary bottom-player objectives include:

- establish the foot on the hip or hip line
- bring the inside knee deeply to the inside
- orient the knee downward rather than allowing it to flare outward
- rotate the hips underneath the opponent
- isolate one leg
- prevent the opponent from squaring their hips
- control distance with the foot and knee structure
- expose the knee line
- access backside or leg-entanglement pathways

Common attacking pathways include:

- backside 50/50
- 50/50
- Single-Leg X
- Saddle
- heel exposure where ruleset permits
- wrestle-up
- sweep to top position
- back exposure
- transitions into other open-guard configurations

Primary top-player objectives include:

- remove or bypass the foot-on-hip frame
- force the inside knee back to the outside
- square the hips toward the bottom athlete
- deny the bottom athlete access underneath the hip line
- control the legs before the angle becomes established
- prevent the knee line from being captured
- create distance or pressure that collapses the K Guard structure

Key mechanical variables include:

- foot-on-hip connection
- inside knee depth
- downward knee orientation
- hip rotation
- knee-line access
- control of the opponent’s far leg
- relationship between the bottom athlete’s hips and the opponent’s thigh
- ability to maintain an off-centre angle
- opponent’s ability to square the hips

Video observation cues:

- foot connected to the hip while the inside knee penetrates underneath
- knee pointing downward as the hips rotate beneath the opponent
- top player forcing the knee outward and collapsing the structure
- K Guard entry creating immediate access to the knee line
- far-leg control preventing disengagement
- transition into backside 50/50
- bottom athlete losing the foot-on-hip frame and being flattened or squared up
- opponent stepping or rotating in a way that exposes the back or secondary leg

Synochi should recognise K Guard through the combined relationship of foot-on-hip framing, inside knee penetration, downward knee orientation, and hip rotation.

The guard becomes effective when those structures work together to place the athlete underneath and outside the opponent’s alignment, creating access to the knee line, backside position, and secondary leg.


[[Half Guard]]
[[Butterfly Guard]]
[[Combat Intelligence Hub]]

Butterfly half guard is a hybrid guard where the bottom athlete controls one of the opponent’s legs in a half-guard relationship while using an inside butterfly hook to create elevation, off-balancing, and directional movement.

It should be understood as a transitional and attacking guard rather than simply half guard with a hook inserted.

Primary bottom-player objectives include:

- maintain inside position with the butterfly hook
- avoid being flattened
- preserve hip mobility
- create upper-body connection
- control the opponent’s trapped leg
- use elevation to disrupt base
- build height when the opponent reacts
- create pathways to sweeps, wrestle-ups, back exposure, or leg entanglements

Common attacking pathways include:

- butterfly sweep
- underhook wrestle-up
- dogfight
- back exposure
- SLX entry
- X-guard entry
- saddle entry
- knee-tap style reversals
- transition to full butterfly guard

Primary top-player objectives include:

- flatten the bottom athlete
- clear or neutralise the butterfly hook
- control head and shoulder position
- win the underhook battle
- prevent the bottom athlete from building height
- free the trapped knee line
- transition into passing pressure

Key mechanical variables include:

- butterfly hook depth
- hip angle
- underhook position
- trapped-leg control
- upper-body connection
- head position
- ability to elevate
- ability to build onto elbow or hand
- opponent’s available posts

Video observation cues:

- butterfly hook present but disconnected from upper-body control
- bottom athlete being flattened before elevation becomes available
- opponent’s weight loaded onto the hook
- successful elevation forcing a post
- underhook converting elevation into a wrestle-up
- top player clearing the hook and collapsing the hips
- transition from butterfly half into SLX, X-guard, or dogfight

Synochi should recognise butterfly half guard as a hybrid control system combining half-guard leg entanglement with butterfly-style elevation and off-balancing.

Its effectiveness depends on maintaining hip mobility and using the hook to create reactions rather than treating the hook as a static frame.

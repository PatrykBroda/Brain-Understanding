Cross Ashi is a leg-entanglement configuration where the attacker controls one of the opponent’s legs across the centerline while using the legs and hips to restrict knee-line escape, rotation, and access to base.

It should be understood as a control configuration that links positional dominance, secondary-leg management, and finishing access.

Primary attacking objectives include:

- maintain deep control of the opponent’s knee line
- keep strong hip-to-leg connection
- prevent the opponent from freely turning the knee out
- control or monitor the secondary leg
- separate the controlled leg from the opponent’s upper-body base
- use the opponent’s reactions to expose the foot, heel, or transition pathway
- preserve positional control before escalating to a finish

Common attacking pathways include:

- straight ankle lock
- Achilles lock
- inside heel exposure where ruleset permits
- transition to Saddle
- transition to Outside Ashi
- secondary-leg control
- sweep to top position
- back exposure from defensive rotation

Primary defensive objectives include:

- recover the knee line
- remove secondary-leg control
- regain rotational freedom
- separate the attacker’s hips from the controlled leg
- turn the knee toward a safer orientation
- rebuild base before attempting full extraction

Key mechanical variables include:

- knee-line depth
- hip connection
- orientation of the controlled knee
- control of the secondary leg
- rotational freedom
- ability to follow defensive turning
- distance between the opponent’s hips and available base

Video observation cues:

- attacker controlling the foot while losing the knee line
- strong secondary-leg control preventing escape
- opponent successfully rotating the knee free
- defensive turning increasing heel exposure
- attacker transitioning between Cross Ashi and Saddle as the opponent changes orientation
- opponent rebuilding base after separating hip connection

Synochi should recognise Cross Ashi as a control-first leg-entanglement position.

Its effectiveness depends on maintaining knee-line control, limiting rotation, and controlling the opponent’s supporting structure before prioritising the finish.

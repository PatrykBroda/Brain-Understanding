Single-Leg X Guard is a leg-control position where the bottom athlete isolates one of the opponent’s legs while using the legs and hips to control distance, disrupt balance, and limit the opponent’s ability to freely rotate or disengage.

Single-Leg X should be understood as an off-balancing and transition system rather than a static leg entanglement.

Primary bottom-player objectives include:

- isolate one leg
- control the opponent’s knee line
- maintain strong hip connection
- disrupt posture and base
- control the distance between hips
- prevent easy back-stepping or leg extraction
- create directional kuzushi
- transition into sweeps, wrestle-ups, or deeper leg entanglements

Common attacking pathways include:

- technical stand-up sweep
- outside sweep
- inside sweep
- wrestle-up
- X-guard
- saddle
- straight ankle lock
- outside ashi transitions
- back exposure from defensive turning

Primary top-player objectives include:

- recover balance
- free the controlled knee line
- control the bottom athlete’s feet and hips
- prevent elevation
- turn the knee and hip toward a safer angle
- clear the entanglement before committing weight forward
- begin passing once the leg is no longer isolated

Key mechanical variables include:

- knee-line control
- hip connection
- foot placement
- direction of the opponent’s knee
- distance between the hips
- ability to extend or elevate the controlled leg
- opponent’s available posting leg
- upper-body connection

Video observation cues:

- bottom athlete controlling the ankle without controlling the knee line
- hips drifting too far away from the opponent
- top athlete successfully turning the knee free
- off-balancing forcing the free leg to post
- elevation creating transition to X-guard
- defensive rotation exposing saddle or outside ashi
- leg attack attempted without positional control

Synochi should recognise Single-Leg X as a branching leg-control system.

Its value comes from controlling the opponent’s base and creating predictable reactions that connect directly into sweeps, wrestle-ups, X-guard, and leg-entanglement pathways.

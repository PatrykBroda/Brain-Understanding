
[[Half Guard]]
[[Combat Intelligence Hub]]

Reverse Half Guard is a half guard configuration where the bottom athlete controls one of the opponent’s legs while facing toward the opponent’s legs rather than directly toward the upper body.

It should be understood as a rotational guard and transition position rather than simply an unusual Half Guard variation.

Primary bottom player objectives include:

- maintain control of the trapped leg
- prevent the opponent from freely clearing the knee line
- use hip rotation to stay connected underneath the opponent
- create access to the far leg
- disrupt the opponent’s base
- prevent chest to chest flattening
- create pathways into sweeps, leg entanglements, or guard recovery

Common attacking pathways include:

- transition to Deep Half Guard
- transition to 50 50 Guard
- transition to Single Leg X Guard
- backside leg entry
- rotational sweep
- wrestle up
- recovery back to standard Half Guard
- transition underneath into leg entanglements

Primary top player objectives include:

- free the trapped knee line
- square the hips away from the bottom athlete’s rotational angle
- control the bottom athlete’s hips
- prevent access to the far leg
- flatten the bottom athlete where possible
- clear the entanglement before committing weight forward
- transition into side control, mount, or a passing position

Key mechanical variables include:

- knee line control
- hip angle
- rotational freedom
- access to the far leg
- control of the opponent’s hips
- ability to stay underneath the center of mass
- relationship between the bottom athlete’s shoulders and hips

Video observation cues:

- bottom athlete maintaining the trapped leg while rotating underneath
- top player freeing the knee line and squaring the hips
- far leg becoming accessible during the transition
- bottom athlete using the position to enter 50 50 Guard or another leg entanglement
- rotational sweep developing as the opponent’s weight shifts
- loss of hip connection causing the position to collapse into a weaker guard

Synochi should recognise Reverse Half Guard as a rotational transition position linking Half Guard, Deep Half Guard, and lower body entanglements.

Its value comes from controlling the trapped leg while using hip rotation and access underneath the opponent to create sweeps, recoveries, and leg entry pathways.

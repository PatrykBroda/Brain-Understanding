
## Type
Front Headlock Submission / High Wrist Choke Variation

## Purpose
The High Wrist Guillotine is a guillotine variation that uses a deep choking-arm position and elevated wrist orientation to create strong compression around the neck while limiting the opponent’s ability to posture or turn out.

The distinguishing feature is the height and angle of the choking wrist relative to the opponent’s neck and jaw line.

## Core Mechanism
The choke works by combining:

- deep neck control
- high wrist placement
- elbow closure
- shoulder connection
- posture disruption
- torso angle

The attacker attempts to create a compact choking structure in which the opponent’s head is trapped beneath the shoulder while the wrist and forearm remain high enough to maintain strong neck contact.

## Structural Characteristics
Typical features include:

- the opponent’s head captured under the attacker’s arm
- the choking wrist positioned high near the opponent’s neck or jaw line
- the choking elbow kept relatively tight
- the attacker’s shoulder closing over the opponent’s head
- posture prevented from fully recovering
- secondary hand reinforcing the choking structure

The exact grip may vary, but the high wrist position remains the defining characteristic.

## Why the High Wrist Matters
A lower wrist position can create more space around the neck and make posture recovery easier.

Raising the wrist can:

- deepen the choking arm
- improve neck connection
- increase compression
- reduce space near the opponent’s jaw
- make it harder for the head to slip free

The wrist should remain structurally supported rather than simply being lifted with arm strength.

## Positional Priorities
The attacker generally wants to:

- capture the head deeply
- maintain the high wrist position
- keep the choking elbow connected
- prevent the opponent from lifting the head
- close the shoulder over the neck
- create a useful torso angle
- maintain enough control to transition if the finish fails

## Entry Conditions
The High Wrist Guillotine commonly appears from:

- front headlock exchanges
- snapdowns
- defended takedowns
- standing clinches
- seated guard
- scrambles
- failed wrestling shots

It is especially useful when the attacker gains deep head control before the opponent can rebuild posture.

## Defensive Priorities
The defender commonly attempts to:

- raise posture
- lower or strip the choking wrist
- peel the reinforcing hand
- turn toward the safer side
- create shoulder space
- move the head toward the opening
- prevent the attacker from closing the elbow and shoulder together

Once the choking structure becomes compact, late defence becomes much harder.

## Common Failure Modes

### Wrist not high enough
The attacker has a guillotine grip but the choking hand sits too low.

Result:
space remains around the neck and the defender can posture.

### Elbow flares outward
The attacker loses connection between the forearm and neck.

Result:
compression decreases.

### Shoulder not connected
The wrist is correctly positioned but the attacker does not close the shoulder over the head.

Result:
the defender retains movement.

### Pulling instead of closing
The attacker tries to drag the opponent’s head upward with the arms.

Result:
the choke becomes strength-dependent and unstable.

### Poor angle
The attacker remains directly square when a better finishing angle is available.

Result:
the defender can align the spine and relieve pressure.

## Recognition Cues
FRAME may identify a High Wrist Guillotine when:

- one athlete controls the opponent’s head in a guillotine structure
- the choking wrist is visibly elevated near the neck or jaw
- the elbow remains tight rather than hanging low
- the shoulder closes over the opponent’s head
- the defender repeatedly tries to lower or peel the wrist
- the attacker adjusts angle while maintaining deep neck control

## Video Analysis
FRAME should assess:

- how high the choking wrist sits
- whether the elbow remains connected
- whether the opponent can regain posture
- whether the shoulder seals the top of the choking structure
- whether the attacker creates an effective torso angle
- whether the defender can move the head toward open space
- whether the grip remains structurally sound during transitions

## Coaching Interpretation
If the High Wrist Guillotine repeatedly fails, inspect the quality of the choking structure rather than squeezing harder.

Useful questions include:

- Is the wrist actually high?
- Is the elbow connected?
- Is the shoulder sealing the head?
- Can the opponent still posture?
- Is the attacker using angle and body position effectively?

The variation becomes powerful when the upper-body structure is compact and connected.

## Synochi Interpretation
The High Wrist Guillotine is a refined head-control compression system.

Its advantage comes from reducing escape space around the neck while improving the attacker’s ability to maintain posture disruption and rotational control.

The useful sequence is:

capture deeply,
raise wrist,
close elbow,
seal shoulder,
control posture,
compress,
finish or transition.


## Type
Front Headlock Submission / Arm and Neck Compression Choke

## Purpose
The D'Arce Choke is a head-and-arm choke that traps the opponent’s neck and one arm inside a connected upper-body structure.

It commonly appears from:

- front headlock exchanges
- turtle
- half guard
- sprawls
- scrambles
- failed takedowns
- top pressure positions

The choke becomes effective when the attacker combines deep arm penetration with control of the opponent’s shoulder and posture.

## Core Mechanism
The attacker threads an arm deeply underneath the opponent’s near-side arm and across the neck, then connects the hands or arms to create compression.

The finish depends on combining:

- neck control
- trapped shoulder position
- deep arm penetration
- torso angle
- chest pressure
- reduced rotational freedom

The opponent’s own shoulder helps complete the choking structure.

## Structural Characteristics
Typical features include:

- the opponent’s head and one arm trapped together
- the attacker’s arm threaded deeply through the armpit and across the neck
- the attacker positioned at an angle beside the opponent
- the defender’s posture collapsed or constrained
- the trapped shoulder compressed inward
- the choking structure reinforced by the attacker’s chest and upper body

## Positional Priorities
The attacker generally wants to:

- thread the choking arm deeply
- keep the opponent’s head low
- compress the trapped shoulder
- prevent the opponent from turning toward open space
- keep the upper-body connection compact
- improve angle before applying maximal pressure

A shallow D'Arce often feels close but lacks enough control to finish reliably.

## Depth of the Choking Arm
Arm depth is one of the most important variables.

If the arm is too shallow:

- the grip becomes weak
- neck compression decreases
- the opponent can create space
- finishing pressure becomes strength-dependent

The attacker should seek enough depth that the choking structure remains compact without excessive pulling.

## Angle and Chest Pressure
The D'Arce becomes stronger when the attacker uses body position rather than arm strength alone.

Useful adjustments may include:

- moving the torso toward the opponent’s head
- compressing the trapped shoulder
- changing hip angle
- using chest pressure to reduce space
- preventing the defender from rotating underneath

The choke should tighten through positioning, not just squeezing.

## Defensive Priorities
The defender commonly attempts to:

- posture up
- pull the trapped arm free
- create space around the neck
- turn toward the attacker
- widen the shoulder
- control the attacker’s locking hands
- move the head toward open space

Early defensive structure is usually more effective than trying to escape after the shoulder and neck have been fully compressed.

## Common Failure Modes

### Choking arm too shallow
The attacker cannot create enough compression.

Result:
the defender survives or escapes.

### Trapped shoulder remains mobile
The defender can separate the shoulder from the neck.

Result:
space remains inside the choke.

### Poor angle
The attacker stays badly aligned with the opponent’s torso.

Result:
pressure is distributed inefficiently.

### Pulling with the arms
The attacker attempts to finish by squeezing harder.

Result:
the position becomes tiring without becoming tighter.

### Allowing rotation
The defender turns toward a safer alignment.

Result:
the choking structure opens.

## Recognition Cues
FRAME may identify a D'Arce Choke when:

- one athlete threads an arm underneath the opponent’s armpit and across the neck
- the opponent’s head and one arm are trapped together
- the attacker connects the arms around the neck and shoulder
- the defender is in turtle, half guard, or a collapsed wrestling position
- the attacker adjusts torso angle to increase compression
- the defender attempts to pull the trapped arm free or posture up

## Video Analysis
FRAME should assess:

- how deep the choking arm is
- whether the trapped shoulder is compressed
- whether the defender can posture
- whether the attacker creates useful angle
- whether chest pressure contributes to the choke
- whether the defender can rotate into open space
- what exact adjustment creates or loses compression

## Coaching Interpretation
If a D'Arce repeatedly fails, inspect depth and shoulder control before grip strength.

Useful questions include:

- Is the choking arm deep enough?
- Is the trapped shoulder actually compressed?
- Can the opponent posture?
- Is the attacker creating angle?
- Is the torso helping the choke or are the arms doing everything?

A strong D'Arce should become structurally tight before maximal squeezing is required.

## Synochi Interpretation
The D'Arce is a head-and-shoulder compression system built around deep upper-body penetration.

The attacker uses the trapped arm and shoulder to reduce space around the neck while controlling posture and rotation.

The useful sequence is:

collapse posture,
thread deeply,
trap shoulder,
control rotation,
create angle,
compress,
finish.


[[Seated Guard]]
[[Combat Intelligence Hub]]

Supine Open Guard is an open guard configuration where the bottom athlete is primarily on their back or shoulders while using the legs, feet, shins, and grips to manage distance, preserve inside position, and create attacking angles.

It should be understood as a dynamic distance management and entry platform rather than simply lying underneath a standing opponent.

Primary bottom player objectives include:

- maintain useful foot and shin connections
- preserve inside leg position where possible
- control distance between the hips
- prevent direct chest to chest connection
- keep the knees active between the opponent and the torso
- create angles rather than remaining square underneath
- expose opportunities to off balance, enter underneath, or transition into stronger guards
- prevent the opponent from freely controlling both legs

Common attacking pathways include:

- Single Leg X Guard
- X Guard
- K Guard
- 50 50 Guard
- Butterfly Guard
- technical stand up
- wrestle up
- tripod style sweep
- sickle style sweep
- leg entanglement entries
- transition back to Seated Guard

Primary top player objectives include:

- control the feet and knees
- move around rather than directly through strong frames
- deny inside leg position
- pin one or both legs
- force the bottom athlete’s knees away from the centerline
- close distance safely
- create an angle for passing
- prevent the bottom athlete from entering underneath the hips

Key mechanical variables include:

- foot placement
- knee position
- hip angle
- distance between the athletes’ hips
- inside position
- connection to the opponent’s legs
- ability to create rotation
- opponent stance and base

Video observation cues:

- bottom athlete lying flat without useful leg connection
- both feet being controlled and moved away from the centerline
- strong foot placement maintaining distance
- angle creation exposing K Guard or Single Leg X entries
- opponent stepping too close and allowing underneath access
- bottom athlete recovering to Seated Guard when distance increases
- top player successfully pinning the legs before entering passing range

Synochi should recognise Supine Open Guard as a distance control and transition position.

Its effectiveness depends on keeping the legs structurally active, preserving access to inside space, and using changes in the opponent’s stance to move into stronger guards, sweeps, wrestle ups, or leg entanglements.

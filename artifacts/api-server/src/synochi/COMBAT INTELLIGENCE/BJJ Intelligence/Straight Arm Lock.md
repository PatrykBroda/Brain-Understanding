
## Type
Elbow Lock / Linear Arm Submission

## Purpose
The Straight Arm Lock is a submission that isolates the opponent’s arm and applies extension pressure through the elbow while restricting the shoulder and torso from moving with the force.

It can appear from many positions and may use different parts of the attacker’s body as the controlling structure.

The common objective is always the same:

isolate the arm,
control alignment,
remove rotational freedom,
extend through the elbow.

## Core Mechanism
The straight arm lock works by creating a fulcrum beneath or around the opponent’s elbow while controlling the wrist and upper arm.

The attacker separates the arm from the opponent’s torso and prevents the shoulder from following the direction of pressure.

The submission becomes strong when:

- the elbow is isolated
- the wrist is controlled
- the shoulder is restricted
- the arm cannot rotate freely
- the elbow is aligned against the attacker’s fulcrum

## Structural Characteristics
Typical features include:

- the opponent’s arm extended or progressively straightened
- control near the wrist or forearm
- the elbow positioned against a fulcrum
- the shoulder prevented from freely rotating
- the attacker using hips, chest, arms, legs, or torso position to reinforce control

The precise configuration may vary considerably.

## Positional Priorities
The attacker generally wants to:

- separate the elbow from the torso
- control the wrist
- maintain elbow alignment
- prevent shoulder rotation
- remove slack from the arm
- keep close body connection
- apply extension progressively

## Relationship Between Shoulder and Elbow
The elbow cannot be treated in isolation.

If the shoulder can rotate freely, the opponent may follow the direction of pressure and reduce the lock.

The attacker should therefore control the larger chain:

shoulder,
elbow,
wrist.

The more aligned and constrained this chain becomes, the less force is required.

## Common Applications
Straight arm locks may appear from:

- top control
- mount
- side control
- guard
- scrambles
- failed bent-arm attacks
- transitions where the opponent straightens the arm defensively

The submission may emerge opportunistically rather than from a dedicated setup.

## Defensive Priorities
The defender commonly attempts to:

- bend the elbow
- reconnect the arm to the torso
- rotate the shoulder
- turn the thumb or forearm
- withdraw the elbow from the fulcrum
- control the attacker’s hands
- move the torso with the direction of pressure

Early elbow recovery is usually easier than defending after the arm has been fully isolated.

## Common Failure Modes

### Elbow not isolated
The opponent can keep the upper arm connected to the body.

Result:
the attacker cannot create meaningful extension.

### Wrist controlled without shoulder control
The attacker extends the arm while the shoulder remains free.

Result:
the opponent rotates out.

### Fulcrum misplaced
The pressure is applied away from the elbow joint.

Result:
the attack becomes weak or mechanically inefficient.

### Too much space
The attacker extends the arm while disconnected from the opponent.

Result:
the defender bends or retracts the arm.

### Using arm strength alone
The attacker pulls on the wrist without using body position.

Result:
the submission becomes inefficient and easier to defend.

## Recognition Cues
FRAME may identify a Straight Arm Lock when:

- one athlete isolates an extended arm
- the wrist or forearm is controlled
- the elbow is positioned against a body or limb fulcrum
- the defender tries to bend or retract the arm
- the attacker restricts shoulder rotation
- the exchange centres around maintaining extension through the elbow

## Video Analysis
FRAME should assess:

- whether the elbow is genuinely isolated
- whether the wrist is controlled
- whether the shoulder can rotate
- where the fulcrum is positioned
- whether the defender can bend the arm
- whether the attacker maintains body connection
- what defensive motion causes the lock to fail

## Coaching Interpretation
If a Straight Arm Lock repeatedly fails, inspect isolation before extension.

Useful questions include:

- Is the elbow separated from the torso?
- Is the shoulder controlled?
- Is the fulcrum actually beneath the elbow?
- Can the opponent rotate the arm?
- Is the attacker using body position or just pulling?

The finish should emerge from alignment and control rather than force.

## Synochi Interpretation
The Straight Arm Lock is a linear elbow-isolation system.

The attacker converts control of the wrist and shoulder into a constrained elbow line, then applies extension through a stable fulcrum.

The useful sequence is:

isolate,
align,
control rotation,
remove slack,
extend,
finish.

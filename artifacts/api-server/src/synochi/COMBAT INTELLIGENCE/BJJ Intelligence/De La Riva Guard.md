
[[Supine Open Guard]]
[[Combat Intelligence Hub]]

De La Riva Guard is an open guard configuration built around controlling the outside of one of the opponent’s legs while using the legs and grips to manage distance, disrupt stance, and create rotational access around the opponent’s hips.

It should be understood as a position for angle creation, stance disruption, and transition rather than simply a hook placed around the leg.

Primary bottom player objectives include:

- establish meaningful control of the near leg
- use the outside hook to influence hip and knee direction
- maintain control of distance
- prevent the opponent from freely squaring the hips
- create off balance through push pull relationships
- access the far leg, upper body, or backside
- transition into sweeps, wrestle ups, back takes, or other guards

Common attacking pathways include:

- technical stand up sweep
- tripod style sweep
- wrestle up
- single leg
- back exposure
- berimbolo style rotation where appropriate
- transition to X Guard
- transition to Single Leg X Guard
- transition to K Guard
- transition to 50 50 Guard

Primary top player objectives include:

- remove or neutralise the De La Riva hook
- control the bottom athlete’s free leg
- square the hips
- prevent the bottom athlete from controlling the far leg
- close distance without allowing underneath access
- redirect into a passing angle
- prevent rotational access around the hip

Key mechanical variables include:

- depth and direction of the De La Riva hook
- control of the near leg
- far leg access
- hip angle
- distance
- upper body grip or frame connection
- opponent stance width
- ability to prevent the hips from squaring

Video observation cues:

- hook present without meaningful control of the opponent’s stance
- opponent freely turning the knee and clearing the hook
- bottom athlete using the hook to create rotational off balance
- far leg control creating a sweep or backside entry
- opponent stepping close enough to expose X Guard or Single Leg X Guard
- transition from De La Riva into K Guard or 50 50 Guard as the opponent turns
- top player successfully controlling the free leg before beginning the pass

Synochi should recognise De La Riva Guard as an angle and stance control position.

Its value comes from using outside leg control to prevent the opponent from remaining square and stable, then exploiting the resulting rotation to access sweeps, wrestle ups, backside positions, or other guard systems.

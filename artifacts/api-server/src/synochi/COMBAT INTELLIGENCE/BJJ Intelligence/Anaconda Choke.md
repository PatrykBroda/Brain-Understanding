# Anaconda Choke

## Type
Front Headlock Submission / Arm and Neck Compression Choke

## Purpose
The Anaconda Choke is a front headlock submission that traps the opponent’s head and one arm inside a connected arm structure.

The choke works by compressing the neck while also controlling the trapped shoulder, usually after the opponent’s posture has already been broken.

It commonly appears from:

- front headlock
- turtle
- sprawls
- failed takedowns
- scrambles
- snapdowns

## Core Mechanism
The attacker threads one arm underneath the opponent’s neck and through the space around the trapped arm, then connects the hands or arms into a locking structure.

The finish depends on combining:

- head control
- trapped shoulder compression
- angle
- chest pressure
- rotational control
- reduction of available space

The opponent’s own shoulder contributes to the choking structure.

## Structural Characteristics
Typical features include:

- the opponent’s head and one arm trapped together
- the attacker’s arm threaded deeply beneath the neck
- the choking structure wrapping around both neck and shoulder
- the opponent’s posture collapsed
- the attacker positioned at an angle rather than directly square
- rotational movement used to tighten the structure

## Positional Priorities
The attacker generally wants to:

- keep the opponent’s head low
- trap the shoulder deeply
- eliminate space around the neck
- maintain a compact arm connection
- prevent the opponent from turning toward open space
- use body position to reinforce the choke

The submission becomes weaker if the opponent can separate the head and trapped shoulder.

## Rotational Component
The Anaconda often becomes stronger when the attacker rotates or moves around the opponent.

This movement can:

- tighten the trapped shoulder
- reduce neck space
- collapse posture further
- prevent the defender from facing the attacker
- improve finishing angle

The rotation should reinforce control rather than loosen the lock.

## Defensive Priorities
The defender commonly attempts to:

- create space around the neck
- pull the trapped arm free
- raise posture
- turn toward the attacker
- prevent rotational tightening
- control the attacker’s locking hands
- widen the shoulder and neck relationship

Early defence usually focuses on preventing the head and arm from being fully compressed together.

## Common Failure Modes

### Shallow threading arm
The attacker’s arm does not travel deeply enough beneath the neck.

Result:
the choke remains loose.

### Trapped shoulder not controlled
The opponent’s shoulder remains mobile.

Result:
space remains inside the choking structure.

### Staying square
The attacker remains directly in front without improving angle.

Result:
compression is weaker.

### Loose arm connection
The locking structure opens during movement.

Result:
the defender regains space.

### Rolling without control
The attacker rotates before the head and shoulder are secured.

Result:
the defender follows the motion and escapes.

## Recognition Cues
FRAME may identify an Anaconda Choke when:

- the opponent’s head and one arm are trapped together
- the attacker threads an arm underneath the neck
- the attacker connects the arms around the neck and shoulder
- the defender is in turtle, front headlock, or a collapsed wrestling posture
- the attacker rotates or circles to tighten the choke
- the defender attempts to pull the trapped arm free or create neck space

## Video Analysis
FRAME should assess:

- whether the threading arm is deep enough
- whether the trapped shoulder is compressed
- whether the opponent can regain posture
- whether the attacker maintains angle
- whether rotation improves or weakens the structure
- whether the defender can turn into open space
- at what moment the choke becomes structurally tight

## Coaching Interpretation
If an Anaconda repeatedly fails, inspect the head-and-shoulder connection before squeezing harder.

Useful questions include:

- Is the opponent’s posture truly broken?
- Is the trapped shoulder compressed?
- Is the threading arm deep enough?
- Is the attacker creating angle?
- Is there still space inside the lock?

The submission should become compact before maximal pressure is applied.

## Synochi Interpretation
The Anaconda is a head-and-shoulder compression system.

The attacker combines cervical control with trapped-shoulder positioning to reduce available space and movement.

The useful sequence is:

break posture,
trap head and arm,
thread deeply,
compress shoulder,
create angle,
tighten,
finish.


[[Supine Open Guard]]
[[De La Riva Guard]]
[[Combat Intelligence Hub]]

Reverse De La Riva Guard is an open guard configuration where the bottom athlete controls the inside or near side of the opponent’s leg while using the legs and hips to influence knee direction, manage distance, and create rotational access underneath or around the opponent.

It should be understood as an angle creation and transition guard rather than simply the opposite version of De La Riva Guard.

Primary bottom player objectives include:

- control the opponent’s near leg
- maintain useful inside leg position
- influence the direction of the opponent’s knee
- prevent the opponent from freely squaring the hips
- create space underneath the opponent’s base
- preserve access to the far leg
- use rotation to create sweeps, leg entries, or backside pathways

Common attacking pathways include:

- transition to K Guard
- transition to 50 50 Guard
- transition to Backside 50 50
- transition to Single Leg X Guard
- wrestle up
- inversion underneath the opponent
- back exposure
- sweep to top position

Primary top player objectives include:

- clear the controlling hook
- square the hips
- control the bottom athlete’s free leg
- deny access underneath the hip line
- prevent the bottom athlete from rotating underneath
- free the knee line
- create a passing angle before the guard becomes deeper

Key mechanical variables include:

- hook placement
- knee orientation
- hip angle
- distance
- inside position
- access to the far leg
- opponent stance
- ability to rotate underneath the center of mass

Video observation cues:

- bottom athlete controlling the leg but failing to create angle
- top player squaring the hips and neutralising the hook
- reverse De La Riva position creating access underneath the hip line
- transition into K Guard as the bottom athlete rotates deeper
- opponent stepping in a way that exposes Backside 50 50
- bottom athlete losing inside position and being forced into a weaker open guard

Synochi should recognise Reverse De La Riva Guard as a rotational entry position.

Its value comes from controlling the opponent’s near leg while creating an angle underneath the hips, which can open pathways into K Guard, 50 50 Guard, backside positions, wrestle ups, and other lower body systems.

Dogfight is a half guard and wrestling transition where the bottom athlete comes up onto a knee or elbow with an underhook and begins contesting upper body position, hip position, and base against the top athlete.

It should be understood as a wrestle up and positional transition system rather than a single sweep.

Primary bottom player objectives include:

- secure and maintain the underhook
- build height from the floor
- keep the head in strong inside position
- connect the shoulder to the opponent’s torso
- control the opponent’s near hip or leg
- prevent being flattened back down
- force the opponent to post or widen their base
- convert the exchange into a sweep, takedown, back exposure, or top position

Common attacking pathways include:

- knee tap
- far knee pick
- single leg finish
- body lock
- back take
- limp arm style back exposure
- roll through or rotational sweep
- transition to standing wrestling
- return to Half Guard if the exchange collapses

Primary top player objectives include:

- deny or weaken the underhook
- win head position
- apply whizzer pressure
- keep the bottom athlete from building height
- widen the base
- square the hips
- force the bottom athlete back to the mat
- transition into front headlock, back exposure, or passing control when the bottom athlete overcommits

Key mechanical variables include:

- underhook depth
- head position
- shoulder connection
- hip height
- control of the near leg
- whizzer pressure
- ability to build onto elbow, hand, or knee
- opponent’s available posts

Video observation cues:

- bottom athlete reaching the underhook without building height
- head position lost to the whizzer side
- strong shoulder connection allowing the athlete to come up
- opponent posting and exposing a knee tap
- bottom athlete being flattened back to the mat
- failed wrestle up converting into back exposure
- successful transition from guard into standing wrestling

Synochi should recognise Dogfight as the bridge between Half Guard and wrestling.

Its effectiveness depends on converting the underhook into height, head position, and control of the opponent’s base rather than treating the underhook as an endpoint.


[[Combat Intelligence Hub]]

Closed guard is a guard configuration where the bottom athlete controls the opponent with the legs closed around the torso while maintaining the hips high enough to meaningfully control the opponent’s pelvis and posture.

The position should not be understood as simply wrapping the legs around the opponent’s body.

A strong closed guard creates a relationship where the bottom athlete’s hips are positioned high relative to the opponent’s hips, giving the bottom player greater ability to control posture, create angles, and attack the upper body.

Primary bottom-player objectives include:

- control posture
- maintain strong hip connection
- keep the hips high relative to the opponent’s pelvis
- limit the opponent’s ability to freely stand or disengage
- create upper-body attachment
- break alignment between head, shoulders, and hips
- create angles
- isolate arms
- expose the neck
- force predictable defensive reactions

Common attacking pathways include:

- triangle
- armbar
- omoplata
- kimura
- guillotine
- hip-bump sweep
- pendulum-style sweep
- back take
- transition to open guard when the opponent stands or creates distance

Primary top-player objectives include:

- recover posture
- lower and control the bottom athlete’s hips
- maintain elbow position
- prevent angle creation
- control the pelvis
- deny dominant grips and head control
- create a safe opening sequence
- stand or open the guard without exposing limbs or balance

Key mechanical variables include:

- relative hip height
- posture control
- pelvic connection
- hip angle
- head position
- elbow position
- shoulder alignment
- distance between chest and hips
- ability to create asymmetric control
- top player’s base during opening attempts

Video observation cues:

- bottom player’s hips sitting too low to meaningfully control posture
- top player successfully driving the bottom athlete’s hips down
- bottom player climbing the guard higher to increase pelvic and upper-body control
- angle creation preceding triangle or armbar threats
- arm isolation causing defensive reactions
- failed guard opening due to poor posture
- bottom player losing control as the top player stands
- guard opening creating immediate transition to another guard rather than neutral separation

Synochi should recognise closed guard as a hip-dominant control and attacking system.

Its effectiveness depends heavily on the bottom athlete’s ability to keep the hips high, control the opponent’s pelvis and posture, and use that connection to create angles and isolate limbs.

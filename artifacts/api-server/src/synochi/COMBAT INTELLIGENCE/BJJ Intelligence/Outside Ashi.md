Outside Ashi is a leg-entanglement position where the attacker controls one of the opponent’s legs while positioning both legs to the outside of that leg, creating a strong connection around the hip and knee line.

Outside Ashi should be understood as a leg-control and finishing platform, not simply an ankle-lock position.

Primary attacking objectives include:

- maintain control above the opponent’s knee line
- keep the hips connected to the controlled leg
- prevent the opponent from freely turning the knee away
- control the secondary leg or posting structure where possible
- create separation between the opponent’s leg and their upper-body base
- expose the ankle and heel without sacrificing positional control
- use defensive reactions to transition into stronger entanglements where appropriate

Common attacking pathways include:

- straight ankle lock
- Achilles lock
- outside heel exposure where ruleset and position permit
- technical stand-up sweep
- transition to Single-Leg X
- transition to Saddle
- transition to Cross Ashi
- top-position conversion after the opponent loses balance

Primary defensive objectives include:

- recover the knee line
- prevent the attacker from controlling hip rotation
- remove secondary-leg control
- turn the knee toward a mechanically safer orientation
- control or separate the attacker’s feet
- rebuild base before attempting full extraction

Key mechanical variables include:

- knee-line depth
- hip-to-leg connection
- orientation of the opponent’s knee
- control of the secondary leg
- attacker’s ability to extend the opponent’s posture
- foot and ankle exposure
- rotational freedom
- opponent’s ability to build height

Video observation cues:

- attacker falling backward onto the foot while losing control of the knee
- opponent successfully extracting the knee before the finish develops
- secondary-leg control preventing the opponent from standing or rotating
- strong hip extension creating ankle-lock pressure
- opponent turning and exposing a transition to another leg entanglement
- attacker using the position to sweep rather than forcing a finish

Synochi should recognise Outside Ashi as a control-first leg-entanglement system.

Finishing quality depends on maintaining positional control, knee-line depth, and hip connection before escalating pressure onto the foot or ankle.

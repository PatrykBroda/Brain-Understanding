

[[Half Guard]]
[[Combat Intelligence Hub]]

Deep Half Guard is a bottom position where the athlete moves underneath the opponent’s center of mass and controls one leg while positioning the hips beneath or beside the opponent’s hips to create off balancing, rotational access, and pathways to come on top.

It should be understood as an underneath guard and sweep system rather than simply a variation of Half Guard.

Primary bottom player objectives include:

- get underneath the opponent’s hips
- control the trapped leg
- prevent the opponent from freely turning the knee outward
- stay connected to the opponent’s center of mass
- manage upper body pressure
- create rotational imbalance
- expose pathways to come on top
- avoid being flattened before the underneath position is established

Common attacking pathways include:

- waiter sweep
- rotational sweep
- back door style recovery
- technical stand up
- transition to X Guard
- transition to Single Leg X Guard
- transition to wrestle up
- back exposure from defensive posting

Primary top player objectives include:

- keep weight distributed away from the bottom athlete’s strongest angle
- control the bottom player’s head and shoulders
- prevent deep hip penetration underneath the center of mass
- free the trapped leg
- turn the knee and hips toward a safer passing angle
- prevent the bottom athlete from rotating underneath
- flatten the bottom athlete before the sweep structure develops

Key mechanical variables include:

- depth of hip position
- control of the trapped leg
- relationship to the opponent’s center of mass
- shoulder position
- head position
- ability to rotate underneath
- opponent’s available posts
- direction of the opponent’s knee

Video observation cues:

- bottom athlete successfully positioning underneath the opponent’s hips
- opponent’s weight loaded over the bottom athlete’s frame
- trapped leg controlled while the free leg is used to influence balance
- top player successfully flattening the bottom athlete
- rotational movement creating a sweep
- opponent posting and exposing a secondary pathway
- bottom athlete losing hip depth and returning to standard Half Guard

Synochi should recognise Deep Half Guard as an underneath control and off balancing system.

Its effectiveness depends on controlling the opponent’s leg while positioning beneath their center of mass deeply enough to create rotational instability and a pathway to come on top.

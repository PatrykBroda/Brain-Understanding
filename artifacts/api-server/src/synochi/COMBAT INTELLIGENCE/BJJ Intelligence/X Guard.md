
[[Single-Leg X Guard]]
[[Combat Intelligence Hub]]

X Guard is a leg-control position where the bottom athlete places the opponent’s weight over the legs while using opposing hooks to control both the near and far side of the opponent’s lower body.

X Guard should be understood as a balance-breaking and elevation system.

Primary bottom-player objectives include:

- place the opponent’s weight onto a compromised base
- control both sides of the opponent’s lower body
- prevent free hip rotation
- create extension through the opponent’s stance
- force posts or weight shifts
- create access to sweeps, wrestle-ups, back exposure, or leg entanglements

Common attacking pathways include:

- technical stand-up sweep
- backward sweep
- forward off-balance
- wrestle-up
- back exposure
- transition to Single-Leg X
- saddle entry
- outside ashi entry
- leg drag style passing transitions after coming on top

Primary top-player objectives include:

- recover posture
- remove one of the controlling hooks
- bring weight back over a stable base
- prevent the bottom athlete from extending the stance
- free the knee line
- control the bottom athlete’s feet
- avoid posting in ways that expose further attacks

Key mechanical variables include:

- relationship between both hooks
- hip proximity
- knee-line control
- direction of the opponent’s weight
- ability to extend or compress the stance
- access to the far leg
- control of the opponent’s posting leg
- upper-body attachment where available

Video observation cues:

- X Guard established without meaningful off-balance
- opponent standing comfortably with posture recovered
- one hook cleared, reducing control to a weaker configuration
- weight shifted onto one leg before a sweep
- bottom player extending the opponent’s stance to break balance
- transition between X Guard and Single-Leg X as the opponent changes base
- defensive rotation creating access to saddle or back exposure

Synochi should recognise X Guard as a dynamic balance-control system.

Its effectiveness comes from controlling both sides of the opponent’s base and forcing weight shifts that make sweeps, wrestle-ups, and leg-entanglement transitions available.

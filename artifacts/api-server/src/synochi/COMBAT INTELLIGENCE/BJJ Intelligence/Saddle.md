[[X Guard]]
[[Combat Intelligence Hub]]

Saddle is a leg-entanglement position where the attacker controls one of the opponent’s legs while using the legs and hips to restrict rotation, separate the opponent’s lower body from their upper-body base, and create access to finishing or transition pathways.

Saddle should be understood as a control system rather than simply a heel-hook position.

Primary attacking objectives include:

- control the opponent’s knee line
- maintain strong hip connection
- limit free rotation
- separate the controlled leg from the opponent’s ability to stand or turn
- control the secondary leg where possible
- force predictable defensive reactions
- create finishing access without sacrificing positional control

Common attacking pathways include:

- inside heel hook
- straight ankle lock variations where mechanically available
- transition to cross ashi
- transition to outside ashi
- secondary-leg control
- back exposure from defensive turning
- sweep or top-position conversion
- re-entry to X Guard or Single-Leg X if control is lost

Primary defensive objectives include:

- protect the knee line
- prevent heel exposure
- clear the secondary leg when possible
- recover rotational freedom
- separate the attacker’s knees and hips
- turn only in directions that do not worsen entanglement
- extract the controlled knee before attempting full disengagement

Key mechanical variables include:

- depth of knee-line control
- hip connection
- position of the secondary leg
- direction of the opponent’s knee
- heel exposure
- rotational control
- distance between the opponent’s hips and available base
- ability to follow defensive turning

Video observation cues:

- attacker chasing the foot while losing knee-line control
- defender freeing the knee before attempting rotation
- strong secondary-leg control preventing escape
- defensive turning creating greater heel exposure
- attacker maintaining entanglement while changing finishing angle
- opponent successfully separating hips and recovering base
- transition between Saddle, X Guard, and Single-Leg X as the opponent changes position

Synochi should recognise Saddle as a positional leg-control system.

The strongest attacks come from maintaining control of the knee line and secondary defensive structures first, then using the opponent’s reactions to expose the finishing pathway.

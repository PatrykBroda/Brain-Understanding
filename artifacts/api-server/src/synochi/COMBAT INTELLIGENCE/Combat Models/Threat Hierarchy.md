
## Purpose

This node defines how Synochi should rank threats, opportunities, and priorities inside a live combat exchange.

Not every available action deserves equal attention.

Synochi should identify what matters most in the current moment.

## Core Principle

Combat decisions should be shaped by hierarchy.

Before progressing, the athlete may need to:

- Survive
- Prevent score
- Recover structure
- Stabilise position
- Remove immediate threat
- Create control
- Attack
- Progress

The correct priority depends on the state of the exchange.

## Basic Threat Hierarchy

A general hierarchy may be:

Immediate Submission Threat
→ Major Positional Loss
→ Scoring Exposure
→ Structural Breakdown
→ Loss of Initiative
→ Missed Opportunity
→ Technical Efficiency

Higher-order threats should normally override lower-order opportunities.

## Example

Athlete is in top half guard with a submission opportunity but their base is compromised.

Synochi should not prioritise the submission if the athlete is about to be reversed.

The more important priority may be:

Restore base
→ stabilise
→ re-establish control
→ return to attack

## Survival Before Progression

When the athlete is under immediate threat, Synochi should prioritise:

1. Remove danger
2. Restore structure
3. Recover control
4. Re-enter preferred game

Progression should not be recommended before stability exists.

## Offensive Hierarchy

When the athlete is in control, Synochi may prioritise:

Control
→ Reaction Creation
→ Isolation
→ Scoring or Positional Advancement
→ Submission Access
→ Finish

The exact hierarchy should adapt to the athlete’s style and ruleset.

## Threat Types

### Submission Threat

Immediate risk of being finished.

### Positional Threat

Risk of losing a dominant or neutral position.

### Scoring Threat

Risk of conceding points or meaningful competitive advantage.

### Structural Threat

Loss of posture, alignment, inside position, frames, base, or connection.

### Tactical Threat

Opponent begins imposing a preferred sequence or forcing the athlete away from their game.

### Time Threat

Clock, score, or ruleset creates urgency.

## Opportunity Competition

Sometimes several good options exist.

Synochi should rank them based on:

- Risk
- Reward
- Athlete strength
- Opponent reaction
- Time
- Score
- Position
- Ruleset
- Probability of continuation

## Athlete-Specific Hierarchy

The same situation may produce different priorities for different athletes.

An athlete with a strong front headlock system may treat front headlock access as high-value.

Another athlete may be better served by using the same reaction to secure top position.

Synochi should therefore combine general combat hierarchy with athlete-specific game knowledge.

## Dynamic Priority

Threat hierarchy is not static.

A sequence may move through:

Attack
→ opponent counter
→ defensive threat
→ recovery
→ new opportunity

Synochi should update priority as the state changes.

## Design Objective

Synochi should help determine:

“What matters most right now?”

The system should reduce poor decision-making caused by chasing low-value opportunities while ignoring higher-order threats.

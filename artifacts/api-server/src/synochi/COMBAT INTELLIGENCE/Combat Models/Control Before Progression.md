
## Purpose

This node defines how Synochi should judge whether an athlete has enough control to progress safely and effectively.

Combat Intelligence should not reward constant movement for its own sake.

Progression without control often creates unnecessary instability.

## Core Principle

Before advancing, Synochi should ask:

“Is the current position sufficiently controlled to justify progression?”

If not, the better action may be to stabilise first.

## Control Indicators

Synochi should consider whether the athlete has:

- Stable base
- Useful head position
- Effective inside position
- Hip control
- Upper-body control
- Frame denial
- Reduced opponent mobility
- Reliable connection
- Predictable opponent reactions
- Low immediate reversal risk

## Progression Readiness

A position is more ready for progression when:

- The opponent’s major escape routes are limited
- The athlete can maintain connection during transition
- The next action does not expose a larger threat
- The athlete has positional structure
- The transition connects to a known strength

## Premature Progression

Synochi should recognise when the athlete advances before control is established.

Examples:

- Chasing mount before securing side control
- Attacking a submission while base is compromised
- Passing without controlling hip movement
- Taking the back without securing upper-body connection
- Shooting a secondary attack before stabilising the first position

## False Activity

High movement does not always mean high quality.

Synochi should distinguish between:

- Productive progression
- Necessary adjustment
- Reactive scrambling
- Uncontrolled movement
- Activity that creates no meaningful advantage

## Consolidation

Consolidation is the process of making an advantageous state more stable before progressing.

This may include:

- Settling weight
- Rebuilding base
- Controlling frames
- Securing grips or connections
- Removing defensive structures
- Forcing predictable reactions

## Exception

Control before progression is not absolute.

Some situations reward immediate transition because hesitation reduces the opportunity.

Synochi should account for:

- Transitional windows
- Opponent exposure
- Time pressure
- Ruleset
- Athlete-specific strengths
- Whether the next action itself creates control

## Decision Rule

Prefer:

Control
→ Stabilise
→ Progress

unless:

The available transition has higher expected value than continued consolidation.

## Design Objective

Synochi should help athletes distinguish between useful aggression and unnecessary positional haste.

The goal is not slower grappling.

The goal is progression from positions that are stable enough to support it.

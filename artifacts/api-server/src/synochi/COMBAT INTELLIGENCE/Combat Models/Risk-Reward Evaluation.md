
## Purpose

This node defines how Synochi should evaluate whether a combat action is worth attempting given its potential upside, downside, probability of success, and tactical context.

## Core Principle

The best technical option is not always the best tactical option.

Synochi should consider:

Potential Reward
→ Probability of Success
→ Failure Cost
→ Positional Risk
→ Tactical Context

## Reward Categories

Potential reward may include:

- Submission
- Score
- Positional advancement
- Back exposure
- Opponent fatigue
- Initiative gain
- Entry into a strong game pillar
- Forced reaction
- Time advantage

## Risk Categories

Potential risk may include:

- Submission exposure
- Positional loss
- Reversal
- Score concession
- Energy waste
- Loss of initiative
- Entry into opponent strength
- Boundary disadvantage
- Time loss

## Risk-Reward Profile

### High Reward / Low Risk

Generally strong option.

### High Reward / High Risk

Context-dependent.

May be appropriate when urgency is high or athlete has strong confidence in the sequence.

### Low Reward / Low Risk

Useful for consolidation, control, or tactical management.

### Low Reward / High Risk

Usually poor unless unusual context justifies it.

## Athlete-Specific Risk

The same action may carry different risk for different athletes.

A leg-entry transition may be low risk for an athlete with a strong leg-lock system and high risk for an athlete with poor leg-position awareness.

Synochi should therefore combine general risk with athlete-specific reliability.

## Context Factors

Risk tolerance should change based on:

- Score
- Time remaining
- Ruleset
- Competition round
- Athlete fatigue
- Opponent strength
- Current position
- Available fallback

## Design Objective

Synochi should help the athlete choose actions with strong expected value rather than simply technically available actions.

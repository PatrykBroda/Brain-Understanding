# Constraint-Dependent Performance

**Placement:** Combat Intelligence → Diagnostic Patterns

## **Definition**

A diagnostic method that examines how performance changes with task rules, starting conditions, permitted actions, resistance and environmental limits.

## **Purpose**

Identify the conditions under which a skill or decision remains effective, and explain gaps between practice success and performance in a different setting.

## **Relevant Constraints**

- Starting position, grips and existing control.
- Permitted attacks, defences and transitions.
- Partner resistance and freedom to respond.
- Scoring, time limits and victory conditions.
- Available space, boundaries, surface and equipment.
- Instructions that make an action predictable or restrict available choices.

## **Operating Sequence**

1. **Define the performance difference.** Specify what succeeds in one setting and fails in another.
2. **Compare the constraints.** Identify relevant differences between those settings.
3. **Locate the changed demand.** Determine whether the athlete now needs additional recognition, timing, control, execution or decision skills.
4. **Generate competing explanations.** Consider constraint changes alongside opponent differences and athlete state.
5. **Test a relevant constraint.** Where practical, change one condition at a time while preserving the interaction being investigated.
6. **Progress towards the target setting.** Introduce the freedom and demands needed for the intended application.
7. **Reassess transfer.** Check whether improvement persists when the original practice restrictions are removed.

## **Core Principles**

- Success in restricted practice establishes ability under those conditions.
- Removing restrictions can expose demands that the original task did not test.
- Constraints can conceal weaknesses or make existing strengths difficult to express.
- A change in outcome may reflect a changed objective or opportunity, rather than reduced skill.
- Greater resistance and greater decision complexity are different demands.
- Altering several constraints together makes the cause of a performance change harder to identify.
- Restricted practice remains useful when its purpose and limits are understood.
- Evaluate transfer in the setting where the athlete needs the skill.

## **Example**

An athlete completes a knee cut from a fixed starting position, but struggles during open sparring.

The fixed task begins with inside position and limits the partner’s responses. Open sparring requires the athlete to obtain that position, recognise the opening and respond to alternative defences.

Diagnosis identifies which added demand causes the breakdown, then adjusts practice to include it.

## **Diagnostic Output**

- Performance difference between settings.
- Relevant constraint changes.
- Additional demands introduced.
- Candidate explanations and evidence gaps.
- A targeted practice adjustment.
- A check for transfer to the intended setting.

## **Boundary**

Constraint-Dependent Performance focuses on task and environmental conditions. Opponent actions and temporary athlete states may interact with those constraints and require separate examination.

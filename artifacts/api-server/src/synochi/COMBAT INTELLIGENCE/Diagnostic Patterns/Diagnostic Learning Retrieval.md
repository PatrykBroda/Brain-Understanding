# Diagnostic Learning Retrieval

**Placement:** Combat Intelligence → Diagnostic Patterns

## **Definition**

A process that retrieves relevant previous diagnoses, interventions and outcomes before forming or repeating a coaching recommendation.

## **Purpose**

Apply accumulated feedback to new situations, preserve effective approaches and prevent unsupported repetition of interventions previously flagged as ineffective or adverse.

## **Operating Sequence**

1. **Define the current case.** Identify the observed pattern, athlete objective and relevant conditions.
2. **Retrieve related records.** Find previous cases with comparable decisions, opponent actions, constraints or athlete states.
3. **Assess applicability.** Identify meaningful similarities and differences; do not rely on matching outcome labels alone.
4. **Review learning flags.** Examine intervention results, implementation quality, confidence and unresolved questions.
5. **Adjust the recommendation.** Reuse, adapt, reconsider or avoid the previous approach according to its evidence and current relevance.
6. **Record the rationale.** State what prior learning influenced the recommendation and why.
7. **Return new feedback.** Update the intervention history after reassessment.

## **Core Principles**

- Similar outcomes can arise from different causes.
- Previous success supports reuse under comparable conditions, not universal effectiveness.
- Previous failure should inform the next recommendation without permanently excluding an approach.
- An adverse flag requires review before reuse.
- Repeating an unsupported intervention requires an explicit reason: changed conditions, improved implementation, revised delivery or new evidence.
- Multiple comparable observations carry more weight than one isolated result.
- Conflicting records should remain visible and prompt further investigation.
- Athlete-specific learning must not automatically become a general rule.
- When no relevant history is available, proceed with a provisional explanation and a clear test.

## **Example**

An athlete again reports losing mount.

A previous record shows that delaying an attack improved retention but reduced attacking activity. Synochi retrieves the mixed result and checks whether the present loss follows the same sequence.

If it does, the next recommendation refines the attack cue to preserve both control and initiative. If the sequence differs, the previous intervention remains background evidence rather than the default answer.

## **Output**

- Relevant previous cases.
- Context similarities and differences.
- Applicable learning flags.
- Decision to reuse, adapt or reconsider an intervention.
- Reason for any repeated flagged recommendation.
- Remaining uncertainty and reassessment criteria.

## **Boundary**

Diagnostic Learning Retrieval applies recorded learning. Intervention Feedback Assimilation evaluates and updates that learning. Both require a retrievable history; the notes alone do not implement persistent memory.

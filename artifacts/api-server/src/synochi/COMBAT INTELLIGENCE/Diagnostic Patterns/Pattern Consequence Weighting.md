
## Purpose

This node defines how Synochi should judge the importance of a recurring combat pattern.

Not every repeated behaviour deserves equal coaching attention.

A pattern becomes more important when it consistently creates meaningful consequences.

## Core Principle

Frequency tells Synochi that something happens.

Consequence tells Synochi how much it matters.

A highly frequent pattern with little impact may be lower priority than a less frequent pattern that repeatedly leads to position loss, scoring, submission exposure, or failed attack sequences.

## Consequence Categories

Synochi should assess whether a pattern affects:

- Positional control
- Positional loss
- Scoring
- Submission creation
- Submission exposure
- Transition success
- Defensive survival
- Energy expenditure
- Tactical initiative
- Decision quality
- Ability to impose preferred game
- Opponent ability to impose their game

## Consequence Severity

### Low

The pattern has limited effect on the overall exchange.

Examples:

- Small efficiency loss
- Minor grip or stance issue
- Slight delay in progression
- Technical imperfection that does not alter the outcome

### Moderate

The pattern meaningfully affects position or tactical flow.

Examples:

- Regularly conceding an underhook
- Losing preferred alignment
- Giving an opponent repeated access to a transition
- Wasting significant energy
- Failing to consolidate advantageous positions

### High

The pattern repeatedly creates major competitive consequences.

Examples:

- Directly causing takedown loss
- Repeatedly leading to guard passes
- Exposing submissions
- Preventing reliable finishing sequences
- Causing repeated scoring concessions
- Breaking down the athlete’s primary game

## Priority Interaction

Pattern priority should consider:

Recurrence
×
Consequence
×
Confidence
×
Context Relevance

A pattern should not become a major coaching priority purely because it is frequent.

Likewise, a severe consequence should not be over-weighted if the evidence is weak or highly context-specific.

## Example

Observation:

Athlete repeatedly reaches with the near-side arm while passing.

Possible consequence:

Opponent gains access to underhooks and wrestle-ups.

If this occurs repeatedly and consistently causes positional loss, the pattern should carry higher coaching priority.

If the same reaching behaviour occurs without meaningful consequence, its priority should remain lower.

## Tactical Relevance

Synochi should increase consequence weighting when a pattern interferes with:

- The athlete’s strongest positions
- Their preferred tactical route
- Their competition strategy
- High-frequency match situations
- Known opponent tendencies
- Current training objectives

## Priority Rule

A useful diagnostic priority is not:

“What happens most?”

It is:

“What repeatedly matters most?”

## Design Objective

Synochi should prioritise interventions that create the greatest improvement in actual combat performance.

The system should avoid spending excessive coaching attention on technically imperfect behaviours that have little practical consequence.

# Decision Loop Detection

**Placement:** Combat Intelligence → Diagnostic Patterns

## **Definition**

Identifies when an athlete repeatedly returns to a decision or sequence of decisions that recreates the same vulnerability, despite opportunities to choose a different response.

## **Purpose**

Find recurring choices that sustain a problem across exchanges or rounds, and identify where the athlete can interrupt that repetition.

## **Recognition Criteria**

- A similar situation repeatedly triggers the same choice.
- That choice repeatedly leads towards a similar vulnerability.
- Relevant alternatives exist but are overlooked, unavailable to the athlete’s current skill, or abandoned.
- The pattern persists across attempts.

Repetition alone is insufficient: a reliable, effective decision can also be repeated.

## **Operating Sequence**

1. **Identify the trigger.** What position, opponent response or perceived opening precedes the choice?
2. **Identify the repeated decision.** What does the athlete consistently do?
3. **Trace the consequence.** How does that choice contribute to the recurring vulnerability?
4. **Compare exceptions.** When does the same choice succeed, and what differs?
5. **Investigate persistence.** Does the athlete fail to recognise alternatives, lack the skill to execute them, or deliberately accept the tradeoff? Treat explanations as hypotheses.
6. **Select an interruption point.** Define a recognisable cue and a feasible alternative response.
7. **Reassess under resistance.** Check whether the athlete changes the decision and whether the outcome improves.

## **Core Principles**

- Distinguish repeated choices across exchanges from a sequence that returns to the same state within one exchange.
- Evaluate the decision in context, including opponent responses and available options.
- Do not infer stubbornness, fear or habit solely from repeated behaviour.
- A technically sound choice may become ineffective when used indiscriminately.
- Breaking a loop requires an alternative the athlete can recognise and execute.
- Assess both decision change and outcome change.

## **Example**

An athlete repeatedly attacks from mount when the opponent brings an elbow across the body. They commit both hands before securing sufficient control and repeatedly lose balance.

Investigate whether the opening is being misread, the attack is mistimed, or the athlete lacks a stabilising response. Practise recognising the relevant cue and choosing an appropriate action, then check whether mount retention improves.

## **Diagnostic Output**

- Recurring trigger.
- Repeated decision or sequence.
- Resulting vulnerability.
- Successful exceptions.
- Possible reasons for persistence.
- Interruption cue and alternative response.
- Evidence needed to assess improvement.

## **Boundary**

Decision Loop Detection identifies recurring choice patterns. Backward Failure Tracing reconstructs earlier contributors to an outcome; Intervention Priority determines which identified issue deserves attention first.

# Intervention Feedback Assimilation

**Placement:** Combat Intelligence → Diagnostic Patterns

## **Definition**

A feedback process that evaluates the response to a coaching intervention, revises the underlying diagnosis and records context-specific learning for future recommendations.

## **Purpose**

Help Synochi learn from effective, ineffective and harmful interventions instead of repeatedly recommending an approach without considering its previous results.

## **Operating Sequence**

1. **Record the starting hypothesis.** Specify the observed problem, suspected contributors and confidence.
2. **Define the intervention and test.** Record what will change, the expected response and what evidence would support or challenge the explanation.
3. **Check implementation.** Establish whether the athlete understood, attempted and sufficiently practised the intervention.
4. **Assess the response.** Compare the targeted behaviour and outcome with the baseline, accounting for opponent, constraints and athlete state.
5. **Revise the diagnosis.** Strengthen, weaken or reopen the explanation according to the evidence.
6. **Assign a learning flag.** Mark the intervention’s current status and the conditions under which that status applies.
7. **Assimilate the lesson.** Use the record to inform future recommendations and schedule reassessment where uncertainty remains.

## **Learning Flags**

- **Supported:** The intended behaviour and outcome improved under relevant conditions. Retain the intervention with its scope and confidence.
- **Mixed:** Benefits were inconsistent or accompanied by tradeoffs. Refine the intervention or identify the conditions governing its usefulness.
- **Unresolved:** Evidence or implementation was insufficient. Gather more information before judging effectiveness.
- **Unsupported:** An adequately tested intervention did not produce the expected response. Reconsider the explanation or approach.
- **Adverse:** The intervention worsened the target outcome or created a meaningful new problem. Flag for review before reuse.

Flags are revisable evidence summaries, not permanent verdicts.

## **Core Principles**

- Separate intervention failure from incomplete implementation.
- Assess both the behaviour being changed and the eventual outcome.
- Improvement supports usefulness but does not automatically prove the proposed cause.
- No improvement does not automatically disprove the diagnosis.
- Record effective interventions as well as unsuccessful ones.
- Attach learning to the athlete, task and relevant conditions before generalising.
- Consult previous feedback before repeating a recommendation.
- Repeating a flagged intervention requires a reason, such as changed conditions, revised execution or new evidence.
- Update recommendation confidence without automatically rewriting established knowledge from a single case.

## **Example**

Synochi recommends delaying a mount attack until the athlete establishes more secure control.

If mount retention improves, record the benefit and check whether attacking opportunities remain available. If retention improves but attacks become excessively passive, mark the response as mixed and refine the timing cue.

If the athlete continues losing mount despite applying the change, revisit the suspected contributor rather than repeating the same instruction unchanged.

## **Feedback Record**

- Original observation and diagnostic hypothesis.
- Intervention and expected response.
- Implementation evidence.
- Behavioural and outcome changes.
- Relevant context and alternative explanations.
- Learning flag and confidence.
- Updated recommendation and reassessment trigger.

## **Boundary**

This node specifies how feedback should inform future reasoning. Reliable use requires a retrievable intervention history and a process that consults it; creating the note alone does not implement automatic learning.

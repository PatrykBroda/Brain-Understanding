# **Contextual Diagnosis**

**Placement:** Combat Intelligence → Diagnostic Patterns

## **Definition**

A diagnostic method that examines the conditions surrounding a combat performance pattern to determine how those conditions change its interpretation and the appropriate coaching response.

## **Purpose**

Distinguish a persistent skill or decision problem from an outcome that depends on the opponent, athlete state, environment, rules or task objectives.

## **Context Dimensions**

- **Opponent:** Actions, reactions, skill, physical attributes and familiarity.
- **Athlete state:** Fatigue, readiness, attention and reported psychological state.
- **Task:** Intended objective, permitted actions, starting position and resistance.
- **Rules and incentives:** Scoring, time remaining and consequences of taking risks.
- **Environment:** Available space, surface, equipment and relevant distractions.

These are factors to investigate, not assumed explanations.

## **Operating Sequence**

1. **Define the pattern.** Describe the observable behaviour or outcome.
2. **Establish the objective.** Identify what the athlete was attempting to achieve.
3. **Record relevant conditions.** Separate observed context, athlete reports and unknowns.
4. **Compare contrasting cases.** Examine when the pattern appears, disappears or changes.
5. **Generate explanations.** Identify which conditions may contribute and how.
6. **Test the distinction.** Where practical, vary a relevant condition while keeping others comparable.
7. **Select a response.** Determine whether to develop a skill, adjust a decision, modify practice conditions or gather more evidence.

## **Core Principles**

- The same outcome can have different explanations in different contexts.
- Judge a decision against the athlete’s objective and available options.
- Context can contribute to a failure without fully explaining it.
- A weakness appearing only under particular conditions can still be a meaningful development target.
- External constraints and athlete decisions can interact.
- Psychological explanations require supporting evidence; behaviour alone does not establish an internal state.
- One context-specific result does not justify a general conclusion about ability.
- Consider successful performances under similar conditions.

## **Example**

An athlete retains mount during early rounds but repeatedly loses it later.

Possible contributors include fatigue, different opponents, altered attack choices or increased resistance. Compare similar exchanges, ask about the athlete’s experience and examine where execution changes before selecting an intervention.

## **Diagnostic Output**

- Observed pattern and intended objective.
- Relevant conditions and evidence sources.
- Conditions associated with improvement or deterioration.
- Competing explanations.
- Missing information and confidence.
- A context-appropriate intervention or next observation.

## **Boundary**

Contextual Diagnosis establishes which surrounding conditions matter. Detailed analysis of a particular factor can follow. It does not treat context as proof of causation or automatically attribute failure to the athlete.

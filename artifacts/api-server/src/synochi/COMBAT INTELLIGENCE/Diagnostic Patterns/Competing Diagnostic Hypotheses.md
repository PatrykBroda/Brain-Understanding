# **Competing Diagnostic Hypotheses**

**Placement:** Combat Intelligence → Diagnostic Patterns

## **Definition**

A diagnostic method that maintains and compares multiple plausible explanations for an observed combat performance pattern until evidence supports narrowing, combining or rejecting them.

## **Purpose**

Prevent premature commitment to the first convincing explanation and identify what evidence would distinguish the alternatives.

## **Operating Sequence**

1. **Define the observation.** Describe what happened without embedding an assumed cause.
2. **Generate plausible explanations.** Consider relevant technical, decision, opponent, constraint and athlete-state factors.
3. **Specify distinguishing predictions.** State what should be observable if each explanation contributes.
4. **Compare existing evidence.** Identify support, contradictions and missing information for each hypothesis.
5. **Select a useful check.** Choose a question, observation or representative practice task that separates the leading explanations.
6. **Update confidence.** Strengthen, weaken, combine or reject hypotheses according to the results.
7. **Choose a proportionate response.** Act on the best-supported explanation while keeping unresolved uncertainty visible.

## **Core Principles**

- Plausibility is not confirmation.
- Competing hypotheses need not be mutually exclusive; several contributors may interact.
- Consider alternatives that materially change the coaching response, rather than listing every imaginable cause.
- Missing evidence is different from contradictory evidence.
- Successful counterexamples can challenge an explanation or reveal its limits.
- Prefer checks that produce different predictions across hypotheses.
- An intervention improving performance does not necessarily prove the proposed cause.
- Retain uncertainty when available evidence cannot distinguish the explanations.

## **Example**

An athlete repeatedly fails to finish a single-leg takedown.

Possible explanations include insecure initial control, difficulty recognising the opponent’s defensive reaction, or loss of position during the finish.

Compare where successful and unsuccessful attempts first diverge. Use representative practice to check whether secure initial control changes the result and whether the athlete can respond when the defensive reaction varies.

The findings may support one explanation or reveal interacting problems.

## **Diagnostic Output**

- Clearly defined observation.
- Leading hypotheses.
- Distinguishing predictions.
- Supporting and contradicting evidence.
- Unresolved information.
- A discriminating question or practice task.
- Updated confidence and proposed response.

## **Boundary**

Competing Diagnostic Hypotheses governs how explanations are compared. The other diagnostic nodes supply candidate explanations; Intervention Priority determines which supported issue to address first.

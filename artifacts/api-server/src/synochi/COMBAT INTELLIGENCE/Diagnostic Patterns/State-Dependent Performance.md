# State-Dependent Performance

**Placement:** Combat Intelligence → Diagnostic Patterns

## **Definition**

A diagnostic method that investigates whether changes in an athlete’s temporary physical, attentional or psychological state contribute to changes in combat performance.

## **Purpose**

Distinguish a persistent skill gap from difficulty accessing or executing an existing skill under particular states, while recognising that both can interact.

## **Relevant State Factors**

- Perceived fatigue and readiness.
- Attention, distraction and mental workload.
- Reported arousal, confidence, frustration or hesitation.
- Perceived discomfort and movement restriction.
- Changes within a round, between rounds or across sessions.

These are factors to investigate, not explanations to assume.

## **Operating Sequence**

1. **Define the performance change.** Specify which observable decisions or actions deteriorate, improve or become inconsistent.
2. **Establish the comparison.** Identify instances where the athlete performs differently under otherwise comparable conditions.
3. **Record state evidence.** Separate athlete reports, observable behaviour and inferred explanations.
4. **Check competing factors.** Examine opponent actions, task constraints and tactical objectives.
5. **Locate the affected function.** Determine whether the change concerns recognition, choice, timing, execution or sustained effort.
6. **Test a practical adjustment.** Where appropriate, adjust pacing, recovery, task complexity or preparation.
7. **Reassess.** Check whether the targeted behaviour and outcome change across repeated instances.

## **Core Principles**

- Temporary state and underlying skill can jointly influence performance.
- A decline late in a session does not establish fatigue as its cause.
- Hesitation, passivity or rushed action does not by itself establish fear, low confidence or excessive arousal.
- Athlete reports provide relevant evidence but may not fully explain the sequence.
- A state can affect different skills differently.
- State-linked improvement or deterioration should be assessed across comparable cases.
- Difficulty performing under a recurring state can become a training target.
- Do not reduce a technical or tactical problem to “mindset” without supporting evidence.

## **Example**

An athlete executes takedown entries early in a session but later begins reaching without moving their feet.

Investigate whether this coincides with reported fatigue, reduced attention, different opponent pressure or a deliberate change in approach. Identify the specific execution change before choosing a practice or pacing adjustment.

## **Diagnostic Output**

- Observable performance change.
- Reported and observed state indicators.
- Comparable successful and unsuccessful instances.
- Affected decision or execution function.
- Competing explanations and evidence gaps.
- A practical adjustment or further observation.
- Criteria for reassessment.

## **Boundary**

State-Dependent Performance examines temporary conditions affecting performance. It does not diagnose psychological or medical conditions, infer stable personality traits, or establish state as the cause from behaviour alone.

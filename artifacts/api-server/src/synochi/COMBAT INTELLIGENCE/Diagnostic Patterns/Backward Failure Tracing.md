# Backward Failure Tracing

**Category:** Combat Intelligence → Diagnostic Patterns

## **Definition**

A diagnostic method that starts from an observed combat failure and traces backwards through preceding positions, decisions and opponent responses to identify earlier contributors and potential intervention points.

## **Purpose**

Identify where a recurring failure begins to develop, including decisions several steps before the visible loss.

## **Operating Sequence**

1. **Define the outcome.** Specify the observable failure: mount lost, guard passed, takedown conceded, submission escaped.
2. **Reconstruct the path.** Trace the preceding states, athlete decisions and opponent responses backwards. Mark missing information explicitly.
3. **Compare repeated instances.** Identify earlier decisions or conditions shared across failures, even when intermediate actions differ.
4. **Compare successful instances.** Check whether the same earlier decisions also occur without failure, and identify where the paths diverge.
5. **Locate candidate contributors.** Identify choices that repeatedly create vulnerability, remove useful options or make recovery more difficult.
6. **Test an intervention.** Change one candidate contributor where practical and reassess the outcome under comparable conditions.

## **Core Principles**

- The final mistake may be the consequence of an earlier decision.
- Different sequences can converge on the same failure.
- Repeated upstream decisions deserve investigation, but recurrence alone does not establish causation.
- The earliest decision is not automatically the best intervention point.
- Include opponent actions and positional constraints; failure does not always originate in the athlete’s decision.
- Separate observed events from inferred explanations.
- Describe failure as more likely or difficult to recover from unless inevitability is supported.
- Revise the explanation when new evidence contradicts it.

## **Diagnostic Output**

- Observed failure.
- Reconstructed sequence and evidence gaps.
- Recurring earlier contributors.
- Alternative explanations and successful counterexamples.
- Candidate intervention point and rationale.
- Confidence in the explanation.
- Practice or observation needed to test it.

## **Example**

An athlete repeatedly loses mount after committing both hands to an attack. The opponent’s intermediate escape actions vary, but the sequences converge on a compromised base.

Backward tracing investigates whether the earlier hand commitment created the vulnerability, whether successful attacks used different positioning or timing, and whether changing that commitment reduces mount losses.

## **Scope**

Backward Failure Tracing identifies and evaluates upstream contributors. Its findings inform the choice of coaching intervention; it does not automatically prioritise the earliest event.

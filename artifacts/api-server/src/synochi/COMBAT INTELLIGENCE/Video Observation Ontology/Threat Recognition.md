## Type
Video Observation Ontology

## Domain
[[Combat Intelligence Hub]]

## Definition
Threat Recognition is Synochi’s ability to identify when an athlete creates a credible possibility of positional advancement, control gain, scoring action, submission, strike, takedown, or forced defensive reaction.

A threat is not defined only by whether it succeeds.

A credible threat changes the opponent’s decision space.

## Core Principle
A threat matters when the opponent must account for it.

**Threat introduced → options narrow → response required → downstream opportunity changes**

## Why It Matters
Many exchanges are shaped by attacks that never finish.

A submission attempt may force an elbow position change.

A takedown entry may force stance adjustment.

A guard pass threat may make the opponent expose an underhook.

If Synochi only tracks completed outcomes, it misses the mechanism that created them.

## Threat Components

### Threat Source
The action or structural condition generating danger.

Examples:

- exposed neck
- isolated arm
- compromised base
- hip line crossed
- dominant angle
- inside control
- body lock
- leg entanglement

### Threat Target
What is currently at risk.

Examples:

- submission
- positional loss
- takedown
- back exposure
- sweep
- strike
- pin
- balance

### Threat Credibility
How likely the threat is to require a meaningful defensive response.

Credibility increases when:

- structure is mechanically sound
- relevant control is present
- distance is correct
- escape options are limited
- attacker has positional support
- the defender actually responds

### Threat Urgency
How quickly the defender must react.

Threats may be:

- immediate
- developing
- latent

## Latent Threats
Some threats exist before an obvious attack begins.

Example:

An athlete controls the wrist and elbow while maintaining dominant angle.

No armbar has been initiated yet.

But the structural conditions for one may already influence the defender’s movement.

Synochi should recognise these as latent threats where confidence is sufficient.

## Threat vs Attack
An attack is an action.

A threat is the consequence that action creates in the opponent’s available decision space.

An attack can fail to create a credible threat.

A threat can exist without a visibly committed attack.

## Threat Stacking
Multiple threats can exist simultaneously.

Example:

Front headlock position may threaten:

- guillotine
- back take
- snapdown continuation
- go-behind
- positional control

The combination can make the exchange harder to defend than any single threat alone.

Synochi should therefore detect threat families rather than forcing one predicted outcome.

## Threat Conversion
A threat can convert into another opportunity through the opponent’s defensive reaction.

Example:

Armbar threat  
→ opponent clasps hands  
→ posture compresses  
→ back exposure increases

The original threat may disappear while still having created the next attack.

## False Threats
Synochi should distinguish credible threats from low-impact gestures.

Signals of weak credibility may include:

- poor structural connection
- excessive distance
- no meaningful opponent reaction
- repeated attacks with no constraint
- mechanically incomplete positioning

Activity alone should not inflate threat confidence.

## Defensive Behaviour as Evidence
The opponent’s reaction can provide evidence that a threat is meaningful.

Examples:

- immediate hand fighting
- posture change
- retreat
- frame insertion
- grip break
- base widening
- turning away

However, reaction should not prove the attacker’s intent.

It only increases confidence that a meaningful threat was perceived.

## Threat Timing
Threat quality depends partly on when it appears.

Early threat:
creates future options.

Late threat:
may occur only after positional control is already lost.

Synochi should therefore consider threat timing relative to state transitions.

## Diagnostic Example
Surface analysis:

"You nearly got the choke."

Threat-aware analysis:

"The choke itself did not finish, but it forced his hands to the neck, removed his post, and created the angle that let you take the back."

## Coaching Value
Threat Recognition helps Synochi understand:

- why opponents react
- how combinations emerge
- which attacks create useful downstream effects
- whether attacks are credible
- where threat sequencing breaks down
- whether athletes are generating enough offensive pressure

## Synochi Function
Synochi should model threat dynamically:

**Observe structure → detect possible danger → estimate credibility → observe defensive response → update threat confidence → detect conversion or resolution**

Threats should be tracked as changing probabilities, not binary labels.

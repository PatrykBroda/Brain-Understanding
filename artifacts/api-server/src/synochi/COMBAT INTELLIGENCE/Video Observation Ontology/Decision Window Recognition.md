# Decision Window Recognition

## Type
Video Observation Ontology

## Domain
[[Combat Intelligence Hub]]

## Definition
Decision Window Recognition is Synochi’s ability to identify the period in which an athlete had a meaningful opportunity to choose between viable actions before that opportunity narrowed or disappeared.

A decision window is not simply "the moment before something went wrong."

It is the interval during which multiple useful options were still available.

## Core Principle
Good coaching should identify the last useful moment for choice.

**State → Opportunity opens → Viable options exist → Constraint increases → Window narrows → Window closes**

## Why It Matters
Many technical mistakes are diagnosed too late.

Example:

An athlete is fully flattened in half guard.

Surface analysis:
"They should frame and recover guard."

But the meaningful decision window may have existed two seconds earlier, when:

- the crossface had not yet settled
- the underhook was still contestable
- the hips were still mobile
- the head position could still be recovered

By the time the bad position is obvious, the useful decision window may already be closed.

## Decision Window Components

### Opening Condition
The structural event that makes a useful action available.

Examples:

- opponent posts
- elbow separates
- inside space opens
- balance shifts
- grip breaks
- angle appears
- pressure temporarily reduces

### Available Options
The meaningful actions available during that interval.

Examples:

- frame
- pummel
- attack
- retreat
- angle change
- recover base
- disengage
- transition

### Window Duration
How long the opportunity remains viable.

Some windows are large.

Others exist for fractions of a second.

### Closing Condition
The event that removes or greatly reduces the available option.

Examples:

- grip settles
- weight transfers
- hip line clears
- elbow reconnects
- posture recovers
- opponent establishes secondary control

## Early vs Late Decision
The same action can have very different value depending on when it occurs.

Early frame:
prevents pressure from settling.

Late frame:
attempts to remove already-established pressure.

The technique may be identical.

The decision quality is not.

## Decision Window vs Reaction Time
A missed window does not automatically mean poor reactions.

Possible causes include:

- athlete did not perceive the opportunity
- athlete perceived it but chose another option
- physical constraint prevented action
- fatigue reduced execution speed
- opponent disguised the opportunity
- video angle hides relevant information

Synochi should separate detection failure from execution failure.

## Window Compression
Higher-level opponents often reduce the amount of time available for useful decisions.

A beginner may offer a large window.

An expert may create:

**threat → reaction → control**

with almost no pause between them.

Synochi should therefore consider the competitive level and speed of the exchange before judging timing.

## Recurrent Missed Windows
Repeated missed windows are more meaningful than isolated misses.

Example:

Across several exchanges, the athlete consistently allows:

inside position loss  
→ pressure establishment  
→ late defensive response

This may indicate a recognition or timing pattern rather than one technical error.

## Opportunity Windows
Decision Window Recognition also applies offensively.

Example:

Opponent posts hand  
→ elbow separates  
→ arm attack becomes available  
→ posture recovers  
→ opportunity disappears

Synochi should recognise both defensive and offensive windows.

## Critical Window
Some decision windows have disproportionate effect on the exchange.

A critical window is one where the chosen action strongly alters downstream probability.

Examples:

- preventing the crossface
- securing the underhook
- clearing the knee line
- recovering posture
- denying head position
- taking the back during exposure

These should receive higher coaching priority.

## Diagnostic Example
Surface analysis:

"You reacted too late."

Window-aware analysis:

"The useful window opened when his right hand posted and his elbow separated. Once he reconnected the elbow and settled his weight, the arm attack was largely gone."

## Coaching Value
Decision Window Recognition helps Synochi distinguish between:

- wrong technique
- correct technique used too late
- missed opportunity
- recognition failure
- execution failure
- already-constrained situation

This produces more precise training targets.

Instead of:

"Work on escapes."

Synochi may prescribe:

"Train recognising the crossface entry before shoulder control settles."

## Synochi Function
Synochi should model decision windows dynamically:

**Observe structural change → detect opportunity → estimate viable actions → monitor narrowing constraints → identify closure → evaluate chosen response**

Over repeated footage, Synochi can learn which decision windows the athlete consistently recognises, misses, or responds to late.

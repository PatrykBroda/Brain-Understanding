## Definition
Initiative and Reaction Detection is Synochi’s ability to distinguish between the athlete who is currently shaping the exchange and the athlete who is primarily responding to it.

Initiative is not simply "who moved first."

It is the capacity to create conditions that force, narrow, or redirect the opponent’s available responses.

## Core Principle
Track who is causing adaptation.

**Action → Forced response → Follow-up → New constraint**

The athlete repeatedly generating this chain usually holds initiative.

## Why It Matters
Two athletes may appear equally active while only one is actually directing the exchange.

Example:

Athlete A repeatedly attacks grips, changes angle, and forces defensive posts.

Athlete B moves constantly but only to recover balance and structure.

Both are moving.

Only one is controlling the decision flow.

## Initiative Signals

### Threat Creation
An athlete introduces a credible attack or positional threat.

### Forced Reaction
The opponent must respond to avoid deterioration.

### Response Prediction
The initiating athlete appears prepared for the likely reaction.

### Follow-Up Capture
The reaction creates the next attack or positional gain.

### Option Reduction
The opponent’s available choices become progressively narrower.

## Reactive State
An athlete is in a reactive state when their actions are primarily driven by threats already imposed by the opponent.

Examples:

- framing after pressure has already arrived
- posting after balance has already been broken
- defending grips rather than establishing preferred grips
- recovering guard after the passer has already created the angle
- hand fighting only after the neck becomes exposed

Reactive does not mean technically poor.

Some strategies deliberately invite reactions.

Synochi should infer pattern before judging quality.

## Initiative Transfer
Initiative can change without positional control changing.

Example:

Top player pressures.

Bottom player creates an underhook.

Top player reacts defensively.

Bottom player comes onto a single leg.

The position may still resemble half guard, but initiative has transferred.

## False Initiative
High activity does not necessarily equal initiative.

Synochi should distinguish between:

- purposeful threat generation
- defensive scrambling
- low-value movement
- repeated failed attacks
- motion without constraint creation

An athlete may attack frequently while the opponent remains structurally unaffected.

## Initiative Chains
Initiative is often best understood across multiple events.

Example:

Snapdown  
→ opponent posts  
→ angle change  
→ front headlock  
→ opponent exposes elbow  
→ back take

The value lies in the chain, not any isolated action.

## Reaction Quality
When an athlete is reacting, Synochi should evaluate whether the response:

- restores structure
- creates counter-threat
- merely delays deterioration
- exposes a new weakness
- successfully resets the exchange

This prevents "reaction" from automatically being interpreted negatively.

## Timing Sensitivity
Early reactions preserve optionality.

Late reactions often occur after the opponent has already constrained the available solution space.

Synochi should therefore distinguish:

**anticipatory response**

from

**late forced reaction**

## Diagnostic Example
Surface analysis:

"You kept defending his passing."

Initiative-aware analysis:

"Once he established the first inside-angle threat, each of your following movements became reactive. You recovered individual positions, but never regained enough initiative to force him to respond to you."

## Coaching Value
Initiative Detection helps Synochi identify whether the athlete needs to improve:

- first action selection
- threat credibility
- follow-up sequencing
- counter-offence
- earlier reactions
- positional resets
- decision timing

It also reveals when technical competence exists but decision control is absent.

## Synochi Function
Synochi should track initiative as a dynamic property:

**Observe action → detect threat → observe opponent response → estimate forcedness → detect follow-up → assign initiative confidence → monitor transfer**

Initiative should remain probabilistic rather than binary.

Both athletes may temporarily contest it.

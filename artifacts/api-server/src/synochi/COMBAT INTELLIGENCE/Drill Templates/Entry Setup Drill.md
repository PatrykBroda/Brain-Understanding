# Entry / Setup Drill

## Purpose
Develop the ability to create the conditions needed to initiate a technique against a partner.

## Training Target
Access, positioning, grip acquisition, alignment, off-balancing and timing before commitment to an attack.

## Setup
Define:
- The intended attack or attack family.
- The starting position and initial contact.
- The conditions required for a viable entry.
- The partner’s permitted defensive actions.
- The success condition and reset point.

## Task
The attacking athlete creates an opening and establishes a viable entry.

The defending athlete denies access through the permitted defensive actions.

Success requires an entry that supports continuation, rather than contact with a target alone.

## Constraints
Select constraints that expose the specific setup problem:
- Starting distance or contact.
- Permitted grips and defensive responses.
- Available attack routes.
- Movement boundaries.
- Time available to establish the entry.

Preserve enough defensive freedom that the attacker must recognise an opening rather than receive it automatically.

## Dosage
Use repetitions for a predictable setup or short timed exchanges for contested access.

Select work and rest according to the objective. Preserve attention and execution quality when learning; introduce fatigue only when maintaining entries under fatigue is the intended target.

## Success Criteria
- Required entry conditions are established.
- Balance and useful positioning are maintained.
- The athlete recognises when the opening is available.
- The entry permits a credible next action.
- Failed attempts can be abandoned without uncontrolled commitment.

## Progression
1. Establish the entry against a cooperative partner.
2. Introduce a defined defensive response.
3. Allow several defensive responses.
4. Increase resistance and vary starting conditions.
5. Continue from the entry into the attack or subsequent exchange.

Progress according to demonstrated competence rather than a fixed schedule.

## Regression
Reduce defensive options, resistance, starting distance or setup complexity. Isolate the condition the athlete cannot yet establish.

## Diagnostic Use
Distinguish between:
- Failure to create an opening.
- Failure to recognise an opening.
- Failure to enter when an opening exists.
- Failure after a successful entry.

If the entry succeeds but the technique fails later, move the training target to the relevant execution, transition or finishing template.

## Synochi Selection Logic
Select when an athlete understands a technique but repeatedly cannot access it during live exchanges.

Individualise the setup around the athlete’s intended game, observed obstacle, partner availability and current capability.

# Transition Drill

## Definition
A Transition Drill repeatedly trains movement from one position, control state, or technical phase into another.

## Purpose
Use when the athlete can perform isolated techniques but needs cleaner movement between positions.

Best suited for:
- guard-to-pass transitions
- pass-to-control transitions
- control-to-submission transitions
- scramble recovery
- front headlock chains
- back exposure sequences
- leg entanglement transitions
- positional advancement

## Starting Position
Begin immediately before the transition being trained.

The starting position should make the transfer between states the main problem rather than the setup leading into it.

## Attacker Objective
Move from the starting position into the target position while maintaining:
- control
- balance
- connection
- pressure
- positional advantage

The objective is not simply to reach the destination, but to arrive without creating unnecessary escape windows.

## Defender Objective
Provide the agreed posture, reaction, or level of resistance that makes the transition realistic.

The defender may attempt to disrupt the transition within the limits of the drill.

## Allowed Actions
Actions should remain focused on completing, preserving, or recovering the intended transition.

Unrelated techniques should be restricted if they reduce useful transition repetitions.

## Constraints
Possible constraints include:
- one starting position
- one target position
- no disengagement
- transition must occur through a specified pathway
- control must be maintained throughout
- no submission before target control is established
- defender limited to specific escape directions

## Reset Condition
Reset when:
- the target position is established
- control is lost
- the defender escapes the intended pathway
- the athlete bypasses the target transition
- the exchange leaves the intended training problem

## Duration / Reps
Can be trained through:
- fixed repetitions
- timed continuous transitions
- alternating partner sets
- successful transition thresholds
- chained transitions across several positions

## Success Metric
Success may be measured through:
- transition completion rate
- control retained during movement
- reduction in escape windows
- balance and connection
- transition speed without loss of structure
- ability to recover when the transition is disrupted

## Common Errors
- treating positions as isolated endpoints
- releasing control too early
- moving faster than connection allows
- creating unnecessary space
- reaching the target position without stabilising it
- forcing the transition after the pathway has closed

## Progression
Progress by:
- increasing resistance
- varying defensive reactions
- adding multiple possible target positions
- chaining several transitions
- adding decision requirements
- moving into positional sparring

## Regression
Simplify by:
- fixing the defender reaction
- reducing resistance
- isolating one micro-transition
- slowing execution
- shortening the distance between starting and target positions

## Athlete Model Signal
The drill can reveal:
- where control is lost between positions
- poor positional sequencing
- hesitation during advancement
- overreliance on isolated techniques
- weak connection during movement
- inability to stabilise after transition
- preferred transition pathways

## Core Rule
Do not only train positions. Train the movement that connects them.

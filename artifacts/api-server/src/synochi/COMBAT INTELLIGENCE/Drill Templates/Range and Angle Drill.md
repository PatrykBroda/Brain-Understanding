# Range and Angle Drill

## Purpose
Develop the ability to create, preserve or deny useful action opportunities through relative distance and orientation.

## Training Target
Range recognition, angle creation, repositioning, access denial and readiness to act after movement.

The target is a useful relationship between athletes. The eventual attack need not be fixed in advance.

## Setup
Define:
- Sport, format and applicable ruleset.
- Starting distance, orientation, stance and contact.
- The relationship each athlete seeks or denies.
- Available attacks, defences and movement options.
- Space, equipment and contact intensity.
- Follow-up demonstrating tactical usefulness.
- Success, reset, work and rest conditions.

Define functional range through available actions. One fixed distance will not suit every athlete or technique.

## Task
One athlete establishes a relationship from which a permitted action is viable.

The other denies that relationship or establishes their own useful range and angle.

Require a controlled follow-up or brief continuation to show that positioning creates a usable opportunity.

Reset after the agreed milestone, decisive denial or departure from the training problem. Alternate roles where appropriate.

## Information and Resistance
Use opponent-generated information: stance, orientation, movement direction, commitment, contact availability and accessible targets or control points.

Begin with limited movement options where needed. Increase resistance through timing and route freedom as well as speed or physical opposition.

## Constraints
Adjust separation, orientation, grips, attack families, defensive options, movement area, exit routes, available time and continuation length.

Avoid rewarding an angle that creates equal or greater exposure elsewhere.

## Dosage
Use brief contested exchanges with frequent relevant opportunities and resets.

Specify exchange count or duration, sets, intensity and rest. Preserve recognition and controlled movement during learning.

Use longer bouts when sustained range management is the target. Record opportunities encountered alongside successful actions.

## Success Criteria
- Range and angle support a permitted action.
- The athlete recognises changing opportunities.
- Movement preserves balance and defensive readiness.
- Follow-up occurs while the relationship remains usable.
- Denial prevents intended access without unnecessary exposure.
- Unsuitable routes can be abandoned or changed.

Assess relationship creation, selection and execution separately.

## Progression
1. Establish useful relationships against predictable movement.
2. Allow the partner to deny one route.
3. Introduce alternative routes and follow-ups.
4. Vary stance, contact and orientation.
5. Allow reciprocal attacks and initiative changes.
6. Assess in representative live exchanges.

Preserve readable information while expanding opponent freedom.

## Regression
Reduce speed, contact, route options or exchange length. Use a clearer starting relationship or simpler follow-up.

Retain an active partner task rather than making access automatic.

## Diagnostic Use
Distinguish unsuitable range, ineffective orientation changes, unreachable opportunities, positioning lost before action and poor execution after adequate positioning.

Check whether denial sacrifices balance or defensive responsibility. Identify whether positioning, recognition, timing or execution is the main limitation.

## Sport Adaptation
Complete these sport-specific prescription fields:
- Format, legal targets, scoring and continuation rules.
- Starting range, orientation, stance and contact.
- Useful relationship and action demonstrating it.
- Each partner's movement, attack and denial options.
- Resistance, intensity, equipment and space.
- Exchange count or duration, sets, rest and quality threshold.
- Success, reset, stopping conditions and transfer criteria.

| Sport | Example relationship to contest | Fields requiring particular attention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BJJ | Guard engagement or passing angle supporting attack or defence. | Seated, standing or ground start; grips and positional continuation. |
| Judo | Gripping distance and orientation supporting entry and access denial. | Grips, stance and whether a throw completes the exchange. |
| Karate | Scoring distance and attack line with defensive readiness. | Format, legal contact and whether scoring stops the exchange. |
| Kickboxing | Striking range and angle without uncontrolled overreach. | Legal attacks, defence and continuation after contact. |
| MMA | Range for striking, connection or phase transition. | Threat families, legal phase changes and wall involvement. |
| Muay Thai | Striking or clinch relationship supporting legal action with balance. | Contact, clinch continuation and defensive responsibility. |
| Sambo | Grip and entry relationship or ground separation supporting action. | Sport or combat format and permitted phase transitions. |
| Taekwondo | Kicking distance and orientation supporting attack and recovery. | Format, targets, contact and scoring continuation. |

Adapt to both athletes. No range or angle is universally advantageous.

## Synochi Selection Logic
Select when athletes act from unsuitable distance, lose access through orientation or cannot deny preferred opponent range.

Use entry work when access to one intended attack is central. Use boundary work when environmental limits are decisive.

Confirm transfer after removing introductory restrictions.

## Safety
Agree legal actions, contact intensity and stop signals.

Control collisions, impact and throwing force as freedom increases. Reset when safe defence is no longer available.

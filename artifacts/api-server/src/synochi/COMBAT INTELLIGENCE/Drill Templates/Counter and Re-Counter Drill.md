# Counter and Re-Counter Drill

## Purpose
Develop the ability to respond to an opponent’s action and continue effectively when that response is itself challenged.

## Training Target
Threat recognition, defensive timing, counter opportunities, initiative and adaptive continuation.

A counter exploits or interrupts an opponent’s action. A re-counter responds to the counter that follows an initial action.

## Setup
Define:
- The sport and applicable ruleset.
- The initial action or attack family.
- Starting posture, distance, contact and boundaries.
- Each athlete’s permitted responses.
- How far the exchange may continue.
- Success conditions and reset points.
- Equipment and contact intensity.

Identify whether the training target is the first counter, the re-counter or the connection between them.

## Task
One athlete initiates a credible action.

The other athlete manages the threat and attempts an appropriate counter.

The initiating athlete recognises the counter and attempts a suitable continuation, recovery or re-counter.

Roles may remain fixed during introductory practice or change as initiative shifts.

## Information and Resistance
Use cues generated by the opponent’s action:
- Changes in balance and posture.
- Direction and degree of commitment.
- Distance and angle.
- Grip, contact and pressure.
- Openings created during attack or recovery.

The initial action must remain credible. Avoid attacks performed only to feed a predetermined counter.

## Constraints
Adjust:
- Available initial attacks.
- Permitted counters and continuations.
- Predictability of the opening action.
- Speed, resistance and contact intensity.
- Starting conditions.
- Length of the exchange.
- Conditions for disengagement or reset.

Permit defence without countering when no viable counter opportunity exists.

## Dosage
Use repetitions to establish an unfamiliar response, then short contested exchanges to develop timing and adaptation.

Reset before the exchange loses its intended focus.

Allow enough rest to preserve recognition and controlled execution.

## Success Criteria
- The initial threat is managed.
- The counter fits the opportunity and ruleset.
- Timing and positioning make the response viable.
- The athlete recognises when the counter is challenged.
- Continuation adapts to the opponent’s actual response.
- Balance and defensive responsibility are preserved.
- The athlete can disengage or recover when further attack is unsuitable.

Assess each stage separately rather than scoring only the final winner.

## Progression
1. Introduce a defined attack and counter at controlled intensity.
2. Allow the initiating athlete to resist the counter.
3. Introduce a limited choice of counters.
4. Allow alternative continuations and re-counters.
5. Vary the initial attack and starting conditions.
6. Assess the skill within representative live exchanges.

Expand freedom gradually. Do not require increasingly long fixed sequences as the main measure of progress.

## Regression
Reduce speed, resistance, action options or exchange length.

Isolate the stage where recognition, positioning or execution breaks down.

Retain a credible initial action even when simplifying the task.

## Diagnostic Use
Distinguish between:
- Failure to manage the initial threat.
- Failure to recognise a counter opportunity.
- Countering before suitable conditions exist.
- Appropriate counter selection followed by poor execution.
- Overcommitment that exposes a re-counter.
- Failure to recognise the opponent’s continuation.
- Persisting when recovery or disengagement is preferable.

Determine whether the limitation concerns a specific response or adaptation across the exchange.

## Sport Adaptation
Preserve the shared function while adapting the actions:
- Grappling: respond to entries, throws, takedowns, passing actions or submission attempts.
- Striking: respond to attacks while managing distance, defensive exposure and return attacks.
- Mixed combat: counter within a phase or through a permitted transition between striking, clinch and ground.

Apply the selected ruleset to legal techniques, scoring, contact and continuation.

Distinguish counters that stop an exchange under the rules from counters that require continued defensive readiness.

## Synochi Selection Logic
Select when an athlete defends without exploiting available opportunities, repeatedly loses initiative after attacking or struggles when an opponent counters their preferred action.

Use this template when the specific training problem is reciprocal opposition. Use broader recognition or transition work when countering is not the central objective.

Assess transfer against varied, credible attacks rather than cooperative sequences alone.

## Safety
Define contact intensity, prohibited actions and stopping conditions.

Control impact, throwing force and submission pressure as uncertainty increases.

Honour taps and stop signals immediately. Do not require a re-counter once safe defensive action is no longer available.

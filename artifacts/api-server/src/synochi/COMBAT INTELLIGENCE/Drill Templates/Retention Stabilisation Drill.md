# Retention / Stabilisation Drill

## Purpose
Develop the ability to preserve a useful state or consolidate an advantage while an opponent attempts to disrupt it.

## Training Target
Balance, posture, positioning, distance management, contact control and adaptive maintenance.

Retention preserves a useful state. Stabilisation establishes reliable control after gaining or recovering it.

## Setup
Define:
- The sport and applicable ruleset.
- The state or advantage to maintain.
- Whether it is established or newly acquired.
- Starting posture, distance, contact and boundaries.
- Each participant’s permitted actions.
- Success conditions and reset points.
- Equipment and contact intensity.

The target must serve a tactical purpose rather than reward holding a static shape.

## Task
The retaining athlete maintains the target state by adjusting to disruption.

The opposing athlete attempts to displace, bypass, escape or otherwise invalidate that state within the permitted actions.

Where appropriate, require the retaining athlete to preserve the ability to attack, defend or transition.

## Information and Resistance
Identify relevant cues:
- Changes in pressure or contact.
- Shifts in balance and alignment.
- Opponent movement or attacking preparation.
- Changes in distance and angle.
- Emerging escape or transition opportunities.

Begin with a defined disruption if needed. Progress towards varied opposition that requires continuous adjustment.

## Constraints
Select constraints around the observed maintenance problem:
- Permitted grips, contacts and defensive actions.
- Opponent routes and attacking options.
- Movement boundaries.
- Required distance or positional relationship.
- Available transitions.
- Duration of maintenance.
- Conditions requiring release, movement or a change of strategy.

Allow useful adjustments. Do not make immobility a requirement unless holding a specific position is the intended task.

## Dosage
Use short contested exchanges with consistent starting conditions.

Set duration and rest according to the demands of the target state, athlete capability and learning objective.

Avoid extending exchanges until fatigue replaces the intended technical problem.

## Success Criteria
- The target state remains functionally useful.
- Posture and balance support the intended task.
- Adjustments occur before disruption becomes decisive.
- Control is maintained without unnecessary tension.
- The athlete remains able to perform a relevant next action.
- An unsustainable state is recognised and changed appropriately.

Measure maintenance quality alongside duration. Holding longer does not demonstrate success if tactical usefulness is lost.

## Progression
1. Maintain the target against a predictable disruption.
2. Increase resistance or movement.
3. Introduce multiple disruption routes.
4. Begin immediately after an entry, transition or recovery.
5. Require maintenance followed by a relevant action.
6. Assess transfer within a representative live task.

Increase difficulty through uncertainty, timing and opponent freedom as well as physical resistance.

## Regression
Reduce disruption options, resistance or duration.

Begin from a more secure state, increase available space or isolate the adjustment the athlete cannot yet perform.

## Diagnostic Use
Distinguish between:
- Failure to establish the target state.
- Failure to recognise disruption.
- Delayed or inappropriate adjustment.
- Excessive rigidity or energy expenditure.
- Maintenance that prevents useful continuation.
- Persisting with a state that should be abandoned.

Identify whether the problem concerns acquiring the advantage, maintaining it or acting from it.

## Sport Adaptation
Preserve the shared function while adapting the target:
- Grappling: retain guard, maintain a pin or preserve useful grip and positional control.
- Striking: maintain usable stance, balance, distance or angle under pressure.
- Mixed combat: stabilise control while accounting for permitted strikes and transitions between phases.

Use the selected ruleset and tactical objective to determine what makes the state valuable.

Do not prescribe prolonged holding where movement or immediate continuation is more appropriate.

## Synochi Selection Logic

Select when an athlete reaches useful positions or distances but repeatedly loses them before acting effectively.

Define what must remain stable and what must remain free to change.

If the athlete cannot acquire the target state, prioritise entry or recovery work. If maintenance succeeds but continuation fails, prioritise transition or execution work.

## Safety
Define contact intensity, prohibited actions and stopping conditions.

Use suitable equipment and controlled partner opposition. Honour taps and stop signals immediately.

Do not reward maintaining control through painful joint positions, uncontrolled impact or ignored submission threats.

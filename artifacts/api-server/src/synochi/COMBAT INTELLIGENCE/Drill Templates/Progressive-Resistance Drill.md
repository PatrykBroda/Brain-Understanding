# Progressive-Resistance Drill

## Definition
A Progressive-Resistance Drill increases partner resistance gradually as the athlete demonstrates stable execution.

## Purpose
Use when a technique or movement is understood mechanically but needs to become reliable against increasing realism.

Best suited for:
- bridging drilling into live application
- testing whether mechanics survive resistance
- identifying the point at which execution breaks down
- improving timing and adaptation
- preventing the jump from passive drilling straight into full sparring

## Starting Position
Begin from the exact position where the target technique or sequence is intended to operate.

The starting position should remain consistent initially so resistance is the main changing variable.

## Attacker Objective
Complete the target action while preserving:
- structure
- timing
- positional control
- mechanical efficiency

The goal is not simply to force completion.

## Defender Objective
Apply resistance at the prescribed level without exceeding it.

Resistance should challenge execution while still allowing the athlete to work on the intended mechanism.

## Allowed Actions
Defensive actions should initially be limited to those relevant to the target technique.

As the drill progresses, more realistic reactions can be introduced.

## Constraints
Resistance may progress through stages such as:
- passive
- structured posture
- light resistance
- moderate resistance
- strong predictable resistance
- variable resistance
- near-live resistance

Only increase resistance when execution remains stable.

## Reset Condition
Reset when:
- the attacker completes the target action
- the defender fully escapes the intended pathway
- mechanics collapse
- the drill moves outside the intended training problem

## Duration / Reps
Can be performed through:
- fixed repetitions at each resistance level
- timed rounds
- successful-completion thresholds
- progression only after repeated clean execution

## Success Metric
Success is not simply completion.

Track:
- technical integrity
- completion rate
- point of breakdown
- unnecessary effort
- ability to maintain structure as resistance rises
- whether timing adapts appropriately

## Common Errors
- increasing resistance too quickly
- defender turning the drill into sparring
- attacker replacing mechanics with force
- remaining at low resistance after stability is established
- progressing despite repeated technical breakdown
- changing too many variables at once

## Progression
Progress by:
- increasing resistance
- introducing additional defensive reactions
- varying timing
- changing starting orientation
- allowing branching responses
- transitioning into positional sparring

## Regression
Regress by:
- reducing resistance
- limiting defensive options
- returning to a fixed reaction
- isolating a smaller movement
- slowing the exchange
- returning temporarily to repetition drilling

## Athlete Model Signal
The drill can reveal:
- resistance threshold
- whether technique depends excessively on compliance
- force substitution
- timing breakdown
- poor adaptation to defensive reactions
- structural weakness
- confidence loss as resistance increases

## Core Rule
Increase realism only as fast as technical stability can survive it.

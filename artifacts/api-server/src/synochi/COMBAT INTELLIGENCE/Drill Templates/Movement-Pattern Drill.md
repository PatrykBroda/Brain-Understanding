# Movement-Pattern Drill

## Purpose
Develop a movement component that enables an effective combat action before adding the demands of a complete technique or live exchange.

## Training Target
Coordination, base reorganisation, weight transfer, rotation, braking and controlled repositioning.

Select the component because it limits a relevant sport task. Judge movement by the action it enables rather than resemblance to a fixed shape.

## Setup
Define:
- Sport, format and applicable ruleset.
- Observed movement limitation and supporting evidence.
- Starting posture, orientation, contact and support points.
- Movement component, permitted adjustments and useful endpoint.
- Solo, equipment or partner format.
- Resistance source, direction and release conditions.
- Work, rest, quality threshold and equipment.
- Success, reset and stopping conditions.

Separate movement learning from strength, flexibility and conditioning targets.

## Task
Move from the defined start to a usable endpoint while managing balance, alignment and support.

A partner provides agreed contact or disruption without supplying the movement for the athlete.

Require a simple next action where useful to show that the endpoint supports attack, defence or further movement.

Reset after completion, repeated loss of the quality threshold or departure from the intended problem.

## Information and Resistance
Begin with predictable demands when coordination is the limiting factor.

Introduce changes in support, pressure direction, available space or partner movement as competence improves.

Add resistance when it exposes the intended demand. Solo success does not establish responsiveness to an opponent.

## Constraints
Adjust starting configuration, direction, movement range, available posts, grips, tempo and required continuation.

Identify compensations that bypass the target component. Permit useful adjustments even when they differ from the introductory demonstration.

## Dosage
Use short sets of controlled repetitions with rest sufficient to preserve attention and movement quality.

Prescribe repetitions or duration, sets, tempo, resistance, rest and the quality threshold explicitly. End a set when quality is repeatedly lost.

Introduce speed, uncertainty or fatigue only when it serves the selected function. Record these demands separately from volume.

## Success Criteria
- The component occurs with usable balance and alignment.
- Support and weight are reorganised for the task.
- Compensation does not bypass the target.
- The endpoint permits the required next action.
- The athlete can stop or redirect within the agreed task.
- Improvement appears in the associated sport action.

Assess component quality and transfer separately.

## Progression
1. Establish the component from a consistent start.
2. Connect it to a useful next action.
3. Vary direction, orientation or starting conditions.
4. Introduce controlled contact or resistance.
5. Require adjustment to readable partner information.
6. Assess within a representative entry, recovery or live exchange.

Change one main demand at a time. Complexity alone is not progress.

## Regression
Reduce distance, range, speed or resistance. Provide a more stable start or isolate the support change that breaks down.

Retain the functional endpoint when simplifying.

## Diagnostic Use
Distinguish coordination difficulty, poor base or weight transfer, insufficient usable range, loading limitations, missed cues and failure to integrate the component into the full action.

Do not infer a mobility or strength deficit from every movement failure.

## Sport Adaptation
Complete these sport-specific prescription fields:
- Format, legal actions and contact limits.
- Representative component and starting configuration.
- Relevant cue, support points and required endpoint.
- Partner task and permitted disruption.
- Resistance, speed, range and equipment.
- Repetitions or duration, sets, rest and quality threshold.
- Reset, stopping conditions and sport task used to assess transfer.

| Sport | Example component and endpoint | Fields requiring particular attention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BJJ | Hip repositioning or base change supporting guard, frames or passing stance. | Ground configuration, posts, grips and permitted continuation. |
| Judo | Stepping, pivoting or weight transfer supporting entry or defence. | Grips, moving partner and whether throwing is included. |
| Karate | Stance change, braking or pivot supporting legal attack or exit. | Competition format, contact and continuation after scoring. |
| Kickboxing | Step, pivot or stance recovery preserving striking readiness. | Legal attack families, defensive continuation and contact. |
| MMA | Level change or ground-to-standing movement supporting the next phase. | Permitted strikes, phase transitions and wall involvement. |
| Muay Thai | Turning or support-leg repositioning preserving balance for continuation. | Contact, balance demands and permitted clinch actions. |
| Sambo | Stepping, turning or support changes enabling entry or recovery. | Sport or combat format, grips and ground continuation. |
| Taekwondo | Support-leg pivot, landing or stance recovery supporting the next action. | Format, legal targets and controlled kicking contact. |

Examples identify functions, not mandatory techniques. Individualise around the observed limitation.

## Synochi Selection Logic
Select when a repeatable component obstructs an otherwise appropriate action.

Use repetition work for a component already available but inconsistent; use resistance work when loading capacity is the supported limitation.

Reassess the full sport task before recording technical improvement.

## Safety
Agree contact, resistance and release signals. Use controlled ranges and suitable surfaces.

Stop for pain or loss of control. Do not force joint range or introduce uncontrolled impact to obtain the endpoint.

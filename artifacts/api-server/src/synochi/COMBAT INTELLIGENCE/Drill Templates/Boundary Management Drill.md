# Boundary Management Drill

## Purpose
Develop the ability to manage restricted space while preserving useful attacking, defensive and movement options.

## Training Target
Boundary recognition, containment, turning, exit selection and tactical positioning under environmental limits.

A mat edge, ring boundary and wall create different problems. Specify physical restrictions and rules consequences separately.

## Setup
Define:
- Sport, format and applicable ruleset.
- Boundary type: marked edge, ring ropes or suitable wall or cage surface.
- Exit, penalty, restart and continuation rules.
- Starting distance, orientation, stance, grips and contact.
- Each athlete's objective and permitted actions.
- Whether boundary support or control is permitted.
- Surface, equipment and intensity.
- Success, reset, work and rest conditions.

Use the target environment or record the limitations of a safe substitute. A floor marker does not reproduce wall contact.

## Task
One athlete contains, turns or pressures the opponent while preserving viable action.

The other seeks useful space, reverses the relationship or acts effectively within the restriction.

Objectives must fit tactical context and rules. Leaving the boundary is not automatically best.

Continue far enough to test the resulting position. Reset at the agreed milestone, applicable stoppage or loss of safe conditions.

## Information and Resistance
Use available space, remaining exits, relative orientation, pressure direction, support changes, opponent commitment and boundary contact.

The containing athlete actively closes routes rather than receiving a stationary partner. The constrained athlete reads available routes rather than following a predetermined escape.

## Constraints
Adjust proximity, orientation, movement area, exits, grips, attacks, contact, boundary use and available time.

Specify whether turning, disengagement or reversal is allowed. Separate opponent pressure from environmental restriction.

## Dosage
Use short contested exchanges with frequent starts near the relevant boundary.

Specify exchange count or duration, sets, intensity and rest. Rotate roles to practise containment and response.

End or simplify when attention or partner control deteriorates. Longer bouts should serve an explicit sustained-management target.

## Success Criteria
- The athlete recognises changed options near the boundary.
- Containment closes meaningful routes while retaining useful action.
- Repositioning produces usable space, orientation or reversal.
- Balance and defensive readiness survive the interaction.
- The next action fits tactical context and rules.
- Required stops, exits and resets are respected.

Record legal outcomes separately from physical control. Boundary contact alone is not successful containment.

## Progression
1. Introduce one relationship at controlled intensity.
2. Allow active containment and viable response options.
3. Expand turning, exit and attacking options.
4. Vary orientation, contact and proximity.
5. Add relevant score or time scenarios.
6. Assess in representative exchanges using the target boundary.

Increase freedom without introducing uncontrolled collisions.

## Regression
Reduce pressure, contact, proximity or available actions. Provide more space or isolate a turn or repositioning component.

Preserve the relevant boundary consequence when simplifying.

## Diagnostic Use
Distinguish missed space restrictions, linear retreat that removes exits, incomplete containment, poor turning mechanics, unusable resulting positions and misunderstood rules.

Check whether a boundary position is tactically preferred rather than a failed escape. Do not infer a general movement deficit from one boundary-specific failure.

## Sport Adaptation
Complete these sport-specific prescription fields:
- Format, boundary type and applicable rules.
- Exit, penalty, restart and continuation conditions.
- Starting configuration and each athlete's tactical objective.
- Legal attacks, grips, contact and boundary use.
- Resistance, equipment and safe movement limits.
- Exchange count or duration, sets, rest and quality threshold.
- Success, reset, stopping conditions and transfer criteria.

| Sport | Example boundary problem | Fields requiring particular attention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BJJ | Maintain engagement or recover useful space near the mat edge. | Out-of-bounds handling and positional restart; edge is not a wall. |
| Judo | Manage grips and orientation near the contest-area boundary. | Selected format's attack, boundary, penalty and stoppage rules. |
| Karate | Pressure or reposition near the competition-area edge. | Format, exit consequences, contact and scoring continuation. |
| Kickboxing | Cut off or regain space near ropes or the contest boundary. | Ring or mat format, legal clinch actions and boundary use. |
| MMA | Contain, turn, exit or act during cage or wall contact. | Legal strikes, takedowns, phase transitions and surface use. |
| Muay Thai | Manage striking or clinch position near ropes or a corner. | Clinch actions, contact, rope interaction and breaks. |
| Sambo | Maintain or reverse a useful relationship near the mat edge. | Sport or combat format, stoppages and legal continuation. |
| Taekwondo | Deny or regain kicking space near the contest boundary. | Format, exit consequences, targets and scoring continuation. |

Check rules for the selected format. Do not transfer boundary assumptions between sports.

## Synochi Selection Logic
Select when restricted space repeatedly alters decisions or execution, or containment is a supported target.

Compare performance away from the boundary to identify added demands. Use range work for general positioning and movement work for isolated components.

Assess transfer in the intended environment and record substitute-boundary limitations.

## Safety
Use suitable surfaces and agree permitted boundary contact and stop signals.

Control impact and protect landing space. Do not drive partners into hard walls, ring hardware or unprotected edges.

Reset before controlled movement or safe defence becomes unavailable. Honour taps and stoppages immediately.

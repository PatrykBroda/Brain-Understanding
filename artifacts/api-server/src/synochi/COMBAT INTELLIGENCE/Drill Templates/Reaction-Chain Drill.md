# Reaction-Chain Drill

## Definition
A Reaction-Chain Drill trains the athlete to respond to a partner’s changing reactions rather than execute a fixed memorised sequence.

## Purpose
Use when the athlete understands individual techniques but needs to connect them dynamically based on resistance, positioning, and opponent behaviour.

Best suited for:
- linking attacks together
- recognising defensive reactions
- improving transition timing
- reducing rigid sequencing
- building decision speed
- developing adaptive technical chains

## Starting Position
Begin from a position where the athlete has a clear primary objective and the partner has several realistic defensive responses available.

The starting position should create a meaningful decision tree without becoming completely open sparring.

## Attacker Objective
Maintain progression toward the positional or submission goal by selecting the appropriate response to the defender’s reaction.

The athlete should not force the original technique when the reaction makes another option more appropriate.

## Defender Objective
Provide one of several agreed realistic reactions.

The defender should vary reactions enough to require recognition while staying inside the intended training problem.

## Allowed Actions
The attacker may use the preselected chain of responses.

The defender may use the agreed defensive reactions.

Actions outside the target reaction tree should initially be restricted.

## Constraints
Possible constraints include:
- limited number of defensive reactions
- one primary attack family
- no disengagement
- attacker must respond rather than reset
- defender changes reaction unpredictably
- continuation required after failed first attempt

## Reset Condition
Reset when:
- the attacker completes the target outcome
- the defender fully escapes the reaction chain
- the exchange leaves the intended position
- the athlete fails to recognise the relevant reaction
- the agreed sequence reaches its endpoint

## Duration / Reps
Can be performed through:
- repeated reaction cycles
- timed rounds
- fixed number of successful chains
- alternating attacker and defender roles

Quality of recognition should matter more than raw repetition count.

## Success Metric
Success may be measured through:
- correct reaction recognition
- appropriate response selection
- reduced hesitation
- smooth transitions
- maintenance of positional control
- reduced forcing of inappropriate techniques
- successful continuation after the first option fails

## Common Errors
- memorising the order instead of reading the reaction
- forcing the first technique
- reacting too late
- skipping positional control between attacks
- defender making reactions unrealistic
- adding too many branches too early
- confusing speed with recognition quality

## Progression
Progress by:
- increasing reaction variety
- reducing predictability
- increasing resistance
- allowing secondary counters
- increasing speed
- expanding the reaction tree
- transitioning into positional sparring

## Regression
Simplify by:
- reducing the number of reactions
- making reactions predictable
- slowing the exchange
- isolating one branch
- returning to repetition drilling for weak responses

## Athlete Model Signal
The drill can reveal:
- reaction recognition speed
- technical rigidity
- hesitation between options
- poor transition mechanics
- forcing behaviour
- preferred branches
- weak branches
- whether decision quality degrades under pressure or fatigue

## Core Rule
Train the athlete to follow the opponent’s reaction, not a memorised sequence.

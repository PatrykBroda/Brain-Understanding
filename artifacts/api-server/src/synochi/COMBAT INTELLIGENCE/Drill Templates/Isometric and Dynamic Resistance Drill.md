# Isometric and Dynamic Resistance Drill

## Purpose
Develop the ability to maintain or change a relevant body position against resistance.

## Training Target
Force production, force absorption, positional strength and controlled movement under load.

Isometric work produces force without substantial movement at the targeted joint or position. Dynamic work produces or controls movement against resistance.

A drill may combine both when the task requires holding, moving and stabilising.

## Setup
Define:
- The sport and physical capability being targeted.
- The position, movement or force direction.
- Whether the task is isometric, dynamic or combined.
- The resistance source and how it will be controlled.
- Relevant joint positions and movement range.
- Work, rest and stopping conditions.
- Equipment and partner requirements.

Resistance may come from body weight, a partner or suitable equipment.

Distinguish physical capacity training from technical practice. Similarity to a sporting movement does not establish transfer by itself.

## Task
The athlete maintains a defined position, moves against resistance or controls an externally imposed movement.

A partner, where used, applies resistance within agreed directions and limits.

For combined tasks, define when the athlete holds, moves and stabilises without requiring an arbitrary fixed sequence.

## Information and Resistance
Specify:
- Resistance direction and magnitude.
- Whether resistance is steady or variable.
- Whether its timing is predictable.
- Permitted adjustments.
- How resistance will be reduced or released.

Use predictable loading when physical capacity is the primary objective. Introduce responsive partner opposition when adjusting force to changing information is part of the task.

## Constraints
Adjust:
- Joint position and movement range.
- Base of support.
- Available grips or contact points.
- Resistance direction.
- Hold duration or movement tempo.
- Permitted compensation.
- Partner movement.

Constraints should expose the intended physical demand without forcing painful positions or unnecessary rigidity.

## Dosage
Set hold duration, repetitions, sets, resistance and rest according to the target capability and athlete readiness.

For strength-focused work, preserve controlled execution and allow adequate recovery.

For endurance-focused work, use repeatable efforts and monitor deterioration in position or movement.

Count the drill within the athlete’s total training load.

## Success Criteria
- Force is applied or absorbed in the intended direction.
- The target position or movement remains controlled.
- Compensations do not defeat the training objective.
- Effort matches the prescribed task.
- Resistance can be released safely.
- Performance remains within the defined quality threshold.

Record resistance, duration or repetitions alongside execution quality.

## Progression
1. Establish control with manageable, predictable resistance.
2. Increase resistance, duration or repetitions according to the objective.
3. Expand movement range or relevant positional demands.
4. Introduce controlled changes in resistance direction.
5. Combine holding and movement where appropriate.
6. Assess the capability within a representative sport task.

Change one main loading variable at a time. Greater instability or complexity is not automatically a useful progression.

## Regression
Reduce resistance, duration, repetitions or movement range.

Provide a more stable base, simplify the force direction or replace variable partner resistance with a predictable load.

## Diagnostic Use
Distinguish between:
- Insufficient force production.
- Insufficient endurance.
- Poor alignment or force direction.
- Difficulty controlling movement under load.
- Excessive tension or unnecessary effort.
- Technical failure despite adequate physical capacity.

Do not infer a strength deficit from every lost sporting exchange. Timing, positioning and decision-making may be the limiting factors.

## Sport Adaptation
Preserve the shared physical function while adapting the task:
- Grappling: develop relevant grip, posture, pulling, pushing or positional force.
- Striking: develop force capacities supporting stance, movement, braking and contact where relevant.
- Mixed combat: address physical demands across striking, clinch and ground phases.

Select loading positions according to the athlete’s actual needs. Do not force every exercise to visually imitate a technique.

## Synochi Selection Logic
Select when assessment suggests that limited force production, endurance or control under load obstructs a sport task.

Identify whether isometric, dynamic or combined work addresses the limitation.

Reassess transfer through the relevant task. Increased drill capacity should not automatically be recorded as improved technical skill.

## Safety
Use controllable resistance and agree release signals before partner work.

Avoid sudden maximal loading, uncontrolled joint pressure and prolonged maximal breath-holding.

Stop for pain, dizziness or loss of control. Reduce loading when fatigue prevents safe execution.

# Individualised Constrained Live Rounds

**Placement:** Combat Intelligence → Drill Templates

## **Definition**

A live sparring template that uses athlete-specific objectives or restrictions to develop an identified skill or decision pattern while allowing exchanges across positions.

## **Purpose**

Connect focused development work to open sparring, preserving realistic opponent responses and opportunities to recognise when the target action is appropriate.

## **Required Inputs**

- Athlete’s development need and supporting observations.
- Target behaviour or decision.
- Starting conditions and applicable rules.
- Athlete-specific objective or constraint.
- Partner instructions.
- Round duration, intensity and recovery.
- Observable success criteria.

## **Construction Sequence**

1. **Select the development target.** Identify a supported weakness, missed opportunity or strength to extend.
2. **Choose a relevant constraint.** Use an objective, scoring incentive or action restriction that encourages the target behaviour.
3. **Preserve opponent freedom.** Allow realistic responses wherever they do not undermine the task.
4. **Define appropriate opportunities.** Clarify when the target action makes sense; avoid requiring it regardless of context.
5. **Run the round.** Allow exchanges to develop across positions.
6. **Review decisions and outcomes.** Assess opportunity recognition, execution and unintended tradeoffs.
7. **Relax the constraint.** Check whether learning persists in ordinary live rounds.

## **Constraint Options**

- **Objective:** Seek a particular control, entry or transition when available.
- **Scoring incentive:** Reward the target behaviour or a meaningful milestone.
- **Action restriction:** Temporarily limit a familiar option to develop alternatives.
- **Decision rule:** Require a relevant cue before committing to an action.
- **Role assignment:** Ask the partner to present a particular challenge while retaining freedom to adapt.

Use the minimum constraint needed to produce useful practice.

## **Core Principles**

- Constraints should follow the athlete’s development need.
- Do not reward forcing an unsuitable technique.
- Both partners must understand their roles.
- Distinguish an absent opportunity from a missed opportunity.
- Assess decision quality alongside completion rate.
- Monitor whether the constraint creates predictable or exploitable behaviour.
- Constraints are temporary practice tools; test transfer after removing them.
- Adjust difficulty when the task produces too few relevant exchanges.

## **Example**

**Development need:** The athlete repeatedly attacks from mount before establishing sufficient control.

**Constraint:** Submission attempts must follow an observable control milestone agreed with the coach. Transitions and defensive adjustments remain available.

**Partner role:** Defend and escape realistically.

**Observe:** Whether the athlete establishes control, recognises suitable attacking opportunities and avoids becoming excessively passive.

**Transfer check:** Remove the rule and assess whether improved attack selection persists.

## **Diagnostic Output**

- Relevant opportunities encountered.
- Target decisions attempted and omitted.
- Execution and outcomes.
- Unintended effects of the constraint.
- Adjustment needed for the next round.
- Performance after the constraint is removed.

## **Boundary**

Individualised Constrained Live Rounds allow practice across positions. Position-Specific Sparring repeatedly starts from a defined position. Either can use athlete-specific constraints.

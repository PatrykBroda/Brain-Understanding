# **Position-Specific Sparring**

**Placement:** Combat Intelligence → Drill Templates

## **Definition**

A live practice template that starts from a defined combat position and gives each athlete clear objectives, with realistic opposition and explicit continuation or reset rules.

## **Purpose**

Develop position-specific recognition, decisions and execution through repeated exposure to relevant attacking and defensive exchanges.

## **Required Inputs**

- Target position and precise starting configuration.
- Athlete’s development need.
- Objectives for both partners.
- Permitted actions and applicable rules.
- Resistance and partner suitability.
- Round duration or exchange count.
- Success criteria and reset conditions.

## **Construction Sequence**

1. **Select the problem.** Identify what the athlete needs to retain, recognise, create, defend or escape.
2. **Define the start.** Specify orientation, grips, limb placement and existing control.
3. **Assign complementary objectives.** Give both partners meaningful tasks.
4. **Set the practice boundary.** Decide whether exchanges reset at a milestone or continue into transitions.
5. **Set resistance and duration.** Match the demands to the objective and athlete’s current ability.
6. **Observe the target behaviour.** Track relevant choices and execution alongside outcomes.
7. **Adjust and reassess.** Change the starting difficulty, available actions or continuation rules as needed.

## **Position Applications**

|**Position**|**Controlling athlete**|**Defending athlete**|
|---|---|---|
|Turtle|Establish control, turn the opponent or secure the back.|Protect against attacks and recover guard, stand or reverse.|
|Back|Maintain control, manage defensive reactions and create submission opportunities.|Defend submissions, remove control and escape.|
|Mount|Retain position, transition or attack while managing escape attempts.|Disrupt control and recover guard, reverse or escape.|
|Side control|Maintain control, advance position or attack.|Create usable space and recover guard, reverse or escape.|
|Half guard|Top: clear defensive controls, pass and stabilise.|Bottom: retain useful structure, sweep, wrestle up or recover guard.|

Objectives must be adapted to the specific configuration and ruleset.

## **Progression Options**

- Start with an advantage, then reduce it.
- Begin with a limited objective, then introduce additional options.
- Increase realistic resistance without making every exchange maximal.
- Allow continuation beyond the initial position to test transitions.
- Rotate partners to assess whether learning transfers across responses.
- Alternate roles where useful for understanding the interaction.

## **Core Principles**

- A named position is insufficient; starting details materially change the task.
- Both partners need objectives that produce the intended interaction.
- Distinguish achieving a milestone from stabilising it.
- Reset rules should serve the learning objective.
- Restricting practice to one position can hide entry or transition problems.
- Success rate alone does not show whether the target behaviour improved.
- Progress towards representative exchanges as competence develops.

## **Example**

**Objective:** Retain mount while responding to escape attempts.

**Start:** Mount with agreed hand and leg placement; no submission already secured.

**Roles:** Top retains mount and may transition when appropriate. Bottom attempts legal escapes.

**Reset:** Reset after a completed escape or agreed positional milestone. In a later progression, continue through transitions.

**Observe:** Whether top recognises the escape preparation, adjusts base and maintains useful control.

## **Boundary**

Position-Specific Sparring organises practice around a starting position. Constraint-Based Positional Sparring deliberately manipulates available actions or task conditions to elicit a particular behaviour. The templates can be combined.

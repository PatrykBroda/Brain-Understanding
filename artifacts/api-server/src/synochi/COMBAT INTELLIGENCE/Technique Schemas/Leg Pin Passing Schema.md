# Leg Pin Passing Schema

## Definition

Leg Pin Passing is a passing structure in which one or both of the opponent's legs are immobilised against the floor, the passer, or another stable structure.

The pin removes the leg's ability to return between the passer and the opponent's torso.

## Primary Objective

Convert a mobile defensive leg into a local constraint and use that loss of mobility to cross the defensive leg line.

## Entry Conditions

The schema becomes available when:

- a leg can be directed toward a stable surface
- weight or structural control can be maintained over it
- the opponent cannot immediately retract the knee
- the passer can progress without surrendering base

## Core Mechanics

1. Identify the leg maintaining the current defensive line.
2. Direct it toward a stable surface.
3. Apply weight or a wedge to prevent recovery.
4. Restrict pelvic rotation.
5. Move around or across the pinned leg.
6. Prevent the knee from returning inside.
7. Progress beyond the leg line.
8. Consolidate upper-body control.

## Key Principle

**A leg that cannot reposition cannot reliably rebuild guard.**

## Decision Rules

If the opponent retracts the leg:

- increase pinning pressure before advancing

If the hips rotate:

- follow the pelvis while preserving the pin

If another leg replaces the defensive function:

- transfer control rather than forcing the original pathway

## Failure Modes

- pinning the foot without controlling the knee
- placing weight without maintaining base
- leaving space for the knee to recover
- attempting to pass before the leg is contained
- continuing to control a limb that is no longer tactically relevant

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** cloth grips can reinforce the leg pin.

**No-Gi:** wedges, bodyweight and positional trapping become more important.

## Synochi Read

Leg Pin Passing is a **local constraint strategy**.

The passer does not need to solve the whole guard simultaneously.

Removing one critical defensive limb can destabilise the wider structure.

# Side Control Maintenance Schema

## Definition

Side Control Maintenance is the process of preserving dominant top control while the opponent attempts to recover frames, turn onto their side, connect knees to elbows, or create enough space to re-guard.

The objective is not to remain fixed.

It is to keep the opponent from rebuilding functional structure.

## Primary Objective

Suppress the opponent's ability to create space and connect upper-body movement to lower-body recovery.

## Core Mechanics

1. Control head and shoulder orientation.
2. Monitor the near-side elbow and hip.
3. Maintain chest connection.
4. Use wedges to prevent structural recovery.
5. Adjust weight as the opponent changes escape direction.
6. Block knee-elbow connection.
7. Follow rotation without surrendering top position.
8. Re-establish stable control before attacking.

## Key Principle

**Side Control is maintained by denying reconnection between the opponent's limbs and torso.**

## Decision Rules

If the opponent frames strongly:

- redirect pressure and remove the frame before progressing

If the opponent turns toward the passer:

- control the near-side shoulder and hip

If the opponent turns away:

- follow the rotation and preserve chest connection

If the knees begin recovering inside:

- prioritise hip control and block knee-elbow connection

## Failure Modes

- remaining static against changing escape force
- losing chest connection
- attacking while frames are still structurally active
- allowing the near-side elbow to reconnect to the hip
- allowing the opponent to rotate freely underneath

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** grips can increase control persistence.

**No-Gi:** chest pressure, wedges, head position and weight transfer become more important.

## Synochi Read

Side Control Maintenance is a **reconnection denial problem**.

The opponent escapes by rebuilding structure.

The top player maintains control by preventing those structures from reconnecting.

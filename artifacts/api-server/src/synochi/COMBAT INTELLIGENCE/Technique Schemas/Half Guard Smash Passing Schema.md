# Half Guard Smash Passing Schema

## Definition

Half Guard Smash Passing is a pressure passing structure used to collapse the opponent's lower-body frames, rotate or flatten the hips, and progressively free the trapped leg.

The trapped leg is not the first problem to solve.

The opponent's structure is.

## Primary Objective

Remove the opponent's ability to maintain effective hip angle, inside position and upper-body structure before extracting the trapped leg.

## Entry Conditions

The schema becomes available when:

- the opponent controls one of the passer's legs from Half Guard
- meaningful upper-body connection can be established
- the opponent's knees can be compressed or redirected
- the hips or shoulders can be restricted

## Core Mechanics

1. Establish upper-body connection.
2. Control head and shoulder orientation.
3. Compress the opponent's knees.
4. Reduce effective inside position.
5. Flatten or rotate the pelvis.
6. Apply chest and hip pressure.
7. Progressively free the trapped knee.
8. Clear the remaining leg.
9. Consolidate the new position.

## Key Principle

**Control the body before extracting the leg.**

## Decision Rules

If the opponent creates upper-body space:

- restore connection before progressing

If the opponent regains strong inside position:

- resolve that structural problem first

If the opponent turns away:

- follow the rotation while preserving pressure

If hip mobility returns:

- rebuild the pin before attempting extraction

## Failure Modes

- pulling the trapped leg out too early
- allowing strong frames to remain active
- losing head position
- leaving space between the torsos
- trying to progress before defensive structure is reduced

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** lapel, belt and trouser grips can reinforce the pin.

**No-Gi:** head position, underhook structure, chest connection and pressure become more important.

## Synochi Read

Half Guard Smash Passing is a **constraint accumulation process**.

The trapped leg becomes easier to free as defensive resources are progressively removed.

**Connection → structural collapse → hip restriction → leg extraction → consolidation**

# Americana Finishing Schema

## Definition

Americana Finishing is a rotational shoulder lock in which the opponent's wrist and elbow are controlled against a stable surface while the shoulder is externally rotated.

## Primary Objective

Immobilise the opponent's upper arm and create controlled shoulder rotation without allowing the torso to follow.

## Core Mechanics

1. Control the opponent's wrist.
2. Keep the elbow positioned against a stable surface.
3. Establish the figure-four grip.
4. Prevent the opponent's shoulder from freely rotating with the torso.
5. Maintain the wrist close to the surface.
6. Adjust elbow position as necessary.
7. Create controlled external rotation at the shoulder.
8. Apply pressure progressively.

## Key Principle

**Pin first, rotate second.**

Without immobilisation, the opponent can move their torso instead of absorbing shoulder rotation.

## Decision Rules

If the elbow begins moving:

- restore the pin before continuing

If the opponent turns their torso:

- control their body position rather than forcing the arm

If the wrist lifts away from the surface:

- restore hand position

If structural isolation is achieved:

- apply gradual rotational pressure

## Failure Modes

- lifting the wrist excessively
- allowing the elbow to move freely
- using arm strength instead of positional control
- failing to restrict torso rotation
- applying pressure too quickly

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

The finishing mechanics remain largely consistent.

**Gi:** grips may make wrist control more persistent.

**No-Gi:** direct wrist control and upper-body pressure become more important.

## Synochi Read

Americana Finishing is a **pin-to-rotation schema**.

**Immobilisation → rotational isolation → controlled finish**

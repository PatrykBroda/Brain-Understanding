# Outside Passing Schema

## Definition

Outside Passing is a family of passing strategies in which the passer moves around the external edge of the opponent's guard rather than remaining engaged with the strongest central leg structures.

It is a directional strategy rather than a single technique.

## Primary Objective

Move outside the opponent's effective knee and foot orientation before the guard can rotate to face the passer.

## Entry Conditions

Outside passing becomes attractive when:

- lateral movement is available
- central connection is weak or can be disengaged
- the passer has space to circle
- the opponent's hips cannot instantly follow the movement

## Core Mechanics

1. Manage or remove direct connection.
2. Control the nearest relevant leg.
3. Establish lateral movement.
4. Move beyond the opponent's knee orientation.
5. Continue around the perimeter of the guard.
6. Prevent hip reorientation.
7. Close distance after clearing the legs.
8. Consolidate control.

## Key Principle

**Do not move through a defensive structure when you can move beyond its useful orientation.**

## Decision Rules

If the opponent follows quickly:

- change direction or change passing route

If the opponent extends the legs:

- increase lateral displacement

If the opponent retracts into a compact structure:

- change range

If connection becomes strong:

- resolve the connection before prioritising movement

## Failure Modes

- circling while strongly connected
- moving without controlling the nearest leg
- stopping at the hip line
- allowing the knees to continually track the passer
- treating outside passing as speed without positional control

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** sleeve and trouser grips can either assist or restrict outside movement depending on who controls the connection.

**No-Gi:** movement freedom is often greater, but persistent leg control is reduced.

## Synochi Read

Outside Passing is an **orientation attack**.

The central contest is whether the guard can continue facing the passer.

**If passer movement exceeds opponent reorientation capacity, the defensive structure degrades.**

# Front Headlock to Guillotine Schema

[[Combat Intelligence Hub]]
[[Guillotine]]

## Function
A reusable coaching schema for recognising, diagnosing, and improving transitions from front head control into a guillotine finish.

This node should help FRAME decide:

- whether the guillotine is actually available
- whether posture has been sufficiently broken
- whether the athlete should finish, reposition, or maintain control
- which mechanical layer is failing
- what cue or drill best targets the observed problem

The purpose is not to describe the guillotine.

The purpose is to help FRAME recognise when head control has become a viable submission opportunity and coach the smallest useful intervention.

## Starting Conditions
A guillotine becomes viable when:

- the opponent’s head is controlled below a useful posture line
- the attacker has meaningful neck access
- the opponent cannot immediately withdraw the head
- the attacker can connect the choking structure without sacrificing control
- the opponent’s posture or direction of movement can be constrained

FRAME should not recommend attacking the neck simply because the opponent’s head is nearby.

The head must already be vulnerable to control.

## Core Sequence
A reliable progression usually follows this logic:

1. capture the head
2. break or maintain broken posture
3. secure useful choking-arm depth
4. close space around the neck
5. prevent posture recovery
6. create the appropriate finishing angle
7. apply controlled compression
8. maintain positional options if the finish fails

The exact grip can vary.

The invariant is:

control posture before chasing compression.

## Primary Mechanical Priorities

### Head Capture
The attacker needs enough control that the opponent cannot simply retract the head.

FRAME should assess:

- head depth
- shoulder connection
- whether the opponent’s neck remains accessible
- whether the grip survives movement

A shallow head capture usually produces a weak submission attempt.

### Posture Control
The opponent’s spine and head position should remain compromised.

If the defender can straighten the posture, the choke usually weakens.

FRAME should treat posture recovery as a primary diagnostic event.

### Choking Arm Depth
The attacker should establish enough depth to create meaningful neck control.

If the arm is shallow:

- space remains around the neck
- the opponent can turn or posture
- the attacker becomes dependent on squeezing strength

### Elbow and Shoulder Connection
The choking structure should remain compact.

FRAME should look for:

- elbow position
- shoulder position
- chest connection
- space between attacker and defender

The goal is to close space rather than pull harder.

### Finishing Angle
The attacker may need to change torso or hip angle to improve compression.

FRAME should distinguish:

more force

from

better alignment.

The preferred coaching intervention should usually be alignment first.

## Decision Branches

### Opponent Begins to Posture
Useful coaching preference:

restore head control before squeezing harder.

FRAME should avoid recommending continued finishing pressure against improving posture.

### Opponent Turns Toward Open Space
Possible responses include:

- adjust angle
- follow the head
- re-close the elbow
- maintain control rather than forcing the original finish

FRAME should treat rotational escape as a positional problem.

### Opponent Drives Forward
The athlete may still have a strong guillotine if structure remains intact.

FRAME should assess:

- whether the head stays trapped
- whether the attacker keeps useful angle
- whether the opponent’s pressure is actually improving the choke or collapsing the attacker’s structure

### Grip Is Established but Nothing Happens
Likely issue:

- posture too strong
- choking arm too shallow
- elbow too open
- excessive space
- poor finishing angle

Useful coaching preference:
identify the missing structural component rather than cueing harder squeezing.

### Opponent Clears the Head
The submission opportunity has ended.

FRAME should stop coaching the finish and recognise the positional reset or transition.

## Failure Diagnosis

### Failure Pattern: Opponent Keeps Pulling Head Free
Likely causes:

- shallow capture
- poor shoulder connection
- head control established too late

Useful coaching preference:
improve capture before building the grip.

### Failure Pattern: Athlete Burns Arms Without Finishing
Likely causes:

- posture not controlled
- excessive space
- poor elbow position
- reliance on squeezing

Useful coaching preference:
fix structure before applying more force.

### Failure Pattern: Opponent Postures Out
Likely causes:

- attacker allows head height to increase
- chest and shoulder connection lost
- grip is present without positional control

Useful coaching preference:
control posture before reattacking.

### Failure Pattern: Athlete Loses Position Chasing the Choke
Likely causes:

- submission prioritised over control
- uncontrolled fall or rotation
- head capture weaker than perceived

Useful coaching preference:
retain control unless the finishing structure is genuinely established.

### Failure Pattern: Choke Looks Tight but Defender Can Turn
Likely causes:

- rotational control missing
- torso angle incorrect
- elbow structure loose

Useful coaching preference:
close the escape direction.

## Coaching Cues
FRAME should prefer concise cues such as:

- “Keep the head down first.”
- “Don’t squeeze yet — close the space.”
- “Your arm is too shallow.”
- “Keep the elbow tight.”
- “Follow their posture.”
- “Fix the angle before adding pressure.”
- “Don’t give up control just to chase the finish.”

Avoid stacking multiple technical details unless the athlete requests a full breakdown.

## Video Recognition
FRAME should look for:

- opponent’s head captured beneath the attacker’s arm
- posture visibly broken
- choking arm depth
- hand connection
- elbow position
- shoulder and chest connection
- defender attempting to posture or rotate
- attacker changing angle before finishing
- whether head control survives a failed submission attempt

FRAME should distinguish:

head contact

from

meaningful head control.

## Drill Selection Logic

### Head Capture Failure
Use drills around:
- capturing the head during wrestling exchanges
- maintaining connection against posture recovery
- following head movement

### Posture Control Failure
Use drills where the defender progressively attempts to posture while the attacker maintains head control without finishing.

### Choking Structure Failure
Use positional repetitions focused on:
- arm depth
- elbow position
- shoulder connection
- reducing space

### Finishing Angle Failure
Use controlled finishing drills where pressure only increases after correct alignment is established.

### Decision Failure
Use progressive resistance where the defender alternates between:
- posturing
- driving forward
- turning
- pulling the head free

The attacker must decide whether to:

finish,
adjust,
or maintain control.

## Coaching Preference
FRAME should not treat every front head-control exchange as an automatic guillotine attempt.

Prefer:

capture
→ assess posture
→ assess neck access
→ establish structure
→ read reaction
→ finish only when conditions remain favourable

If control is strong but the submission is weak, FRAME should preserve the advantageous control state rather than encouraging a low-quality finish.

## Synochi Interpretation
The transition into a guillotine is a control-to-submission decision problem.

The coaching question is:

Has the athlete created enough positional constraint for the submission to become the highest-value next action?

FRAME should distinguish between:

- head-capture failure
- posture-control failure
- choking-structure failure
- angle failure
- decision failure

The useful logic is:

capture
→ constrain posture
→ verify submission structure
→ read reaction
→ finish or preserve control
→ evaluate outcome
→ update coaching preference

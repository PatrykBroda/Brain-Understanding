# Guillotine Finishing Schema

## Definition

Guillotine Finishing is a front-headlock strangulation structure in which the opponent's neck is controlled with the arm while body position prevents them from relieving pressure through posture, rotation or positional escape.

The finish depends on controlling the relationship between the opponent's head, neck and torso rather than simply squeezing the neck.

## Primary Objective

Create effective neck compression while preventing the opponent from restoring posture or rotating into a safer alignment.

## Core Mechanics

1. Establish secure neck access.
2. Control head position.
3. Remove unnecessary space around the neck.
4. Align the choking structure with the intended finishing direction.
5. Control the opponent's ability to posture.
6. Use torso and hip position to reinforce the choke.
7. Maintain connection as the opponent reacts.
8. Apply progressive finishing pressure.

## Key Principle

**The neck is finished through alignment plus positional control, not isolated arm strength.**

## Decision Rules

If the opponent begins restoring posture:

- prioritise head control before increasing finishing pressure

If the opponent rotates:

- follow the head and preserve connection

If neck access becomes shallow:

- improve the grip before continuing

If the opponent's defensive movement creates a positional transition:

- preserve control rather than forcing the original finish

## Failure Modes

- squeezing from shallow neck control
- relying primarily on arm strength
- allowing the opponent to restore posture
- losing body connection
- forcing the finish after alignment has deteriorated

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** additional gripping structures may alter hand positioning and finishing pathways.

**No-Gi:** direct neck control, wrist connection and body alignment become central.

## Synochi Read

Guillotine Finishing is an **alignment-and-posture denial schema**.

**Neck access → posture control → structural alignment → compression**

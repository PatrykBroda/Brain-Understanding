# Saddle Entry Schema

## Definition

Saddle Entry is the process of transitioning into a leg entanglement that controls one of the opponent's legs while positioning the attacker's lower body inside and across the opponent's hip line.

The entry is successful only when control persists after the positional transition.

## Primary Objective

Gain dominant access to the opponent's leg while restricting their ability to immediately withdraw, rotate or counter-entangle.

## Core Mechanics

1. Create access to the opponent's leg.
2. Insert the legs into the intended entanglement.
3. Capture the knee line.
4. Control the opponent's hip orientation.
5. Connect the trapped leg to the attacker's pelvis.
6. Prevent immediate rotational escape.
7. Stabilise the entanglement.
8. Transition into attacks or further control.

## Key Principle

**An entry is incomplete until the resulting entanglement is stable.**

## Decision Rules

If knee-line access is shallow:

- deepen control before attacking

If the opponent rotates during entry:

- follow the hip and preserve leg connection

If the opponent withdraws the knee:

- prioritise retention over submission hunting

If control stabilises:

- begin attack sequencing

## Failure Modes

- entering without knee-line control
- prioritising the heel before stabilising the position
- losing hip connection
- allowing the opponent to freely rotate out
- treating entry and control as separate events

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** rules often restrict available follow-up attacks.

**No-Gi:** Saddle frequently functions as a central leg-attack platform.

## Synochi Read

Saddle Entry is a **state-acquisition schema**.

**Access → insertion → knee-line capture → hip control → stabilised entanglement**

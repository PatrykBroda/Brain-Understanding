# North South Control Schema

## Definition

North South Control is a top-control structure in which the passer positions their torso above or beyond the opponent's head and shoulders while controlling upper-body rotation.

The position reduces direct knee access and changes the opponent's escape geometry.

## Primary Objective

Control the shoulders and upper torso while keeping the opponent's hips too far away to easily rebuild guard.

## Core Mechanics

1. Move beyond the opponent's shoulder line.
2. Keep chest connection through the upper torso.
3. Control shoulder rotation.
4. Prevent the opponent from turning freely underneath.
5. Maintain base while the opponent bridges or rotates.
6. Keep the hips outside immediate knee recovery range.
7. Follow escape attempts without surrendering position.
8. Transition to attacks or adjacent control positions.

## Key Principle

**North South changes the escape problem by increasing distance between the opponent's knees and the passer's torso.**

## Decision Rules

If the opponent turns onto one side:

- follow the shoulder line and preserve upper-body control

If the opponent begins rotating underneath:

- widen base and track the torso

If the opponent creates space near the hips:

- re-establish chest connection before progressing

If the opponent exposes an arm or neck:

- attack only after control remains stable

## Failure Modes

- drifting too far away from the shoulders
- allowing the opponent to freely rotate underneath
- narrowing the base unnecessarily
- attacking before positional stability exists
- allowing the opponent to recover meaningful knee access

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** grips can reinforce shoulder and torso control.

**No-Gi:** chest pressure, head position and weight distribution become more important.

## Synochi Read

North South Control is a **geometry-shift control state**.

The position changes the opponent's available recovery pathways by moving control away from the hips and toward the shoulders.

# Darce Choke Finishing Schema

## Definition

The Darce Choke is an arm-triangle strangulation entered by threading the attacking arm beneath the opponent's near arm and around the neck.

The opponent's shoulder forms one side of the strangulation while the attacker's arm closes the opposite side.

## Primary Objective

Bring the opponent's shoulder into the neck while eliminating space around the head-and-arm structure.

## Core Mechanics

1. Establish access beneath the opponent's arm.
2. Thread deeply around the neck.
3. Secure the finishing connection.
4. Control head and shoulder alignment.
5. Compress the opponent's shoulder toward their neck.
6. Remove space through chest and torso positioning.
7. Prevent the opponent from extending or rotating free.
8. Apply progressive finishing pressure.

## Key Principle

**The shoulder must become an active component of the choke.**

More arm strength cannot compensate efficiently for poor shoulder position.

## Decision Rules

If the opponent's shoulder remains far from the neck:

- improve compression before finishing

If the opponent turns:

- follow the rotation while preserving head-and-arm connection

If the grip remains shallow:

- increase threading depth rather than forcing pressure

If the opponent collapses underneath:

- use the positional change to improve alignment

## Failure Modes

- shallow arm penetration
- leaving space around the neck
- squeezing before the shoulder is positioned
- allowing the opponent to extend their posture
- losing chest connection

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** friction and grips may slow defensive rotation.

**No-Gi:** deep threading and tight torso connection become particularly important.

## Synochi Read

Darce finishing is an **arm-triangle compression problem**.

**Access → shoulder integration → space removal → finishing pressure**

# Float Passing Schema

## Definition

Float Passing is a dynamic passing structure in which the passer maintains mobile weight over the opponent's hips and legs rather than immediately committing to a fixed pin.

The passer uses balance, weight transfer and reactive movement to remain structurally superior while the guard reorganises underneath.

## Primary Objective

Maintain superior mobility and balance while forcing repeated defensive adjustment.

## Entry Conditions

The schema becomes available when:

- the passer can safely place weight over the lower body
- stable base can be maintained during movement
- the legs can be monitored without immediate static control
- enough mobility exists to respond to changing guard configurations

## Core Mechanics

1. Establish a stable but mobile base.
2. Position weight over the hips and legs.
3. Allow controlled movement underneath.
4. Track changes in hip and knee orientation.
5. Adjust weight in response.
6. Prevent stable opponent connection.
7. Exploit openings created during reorganisation.
8. Convert into a more stable passing structure.

## Key Principle

**Mobility can function as control.**

The opponent may be allowed to move without being allowed to organise.

## Decision Rules

If the opponent establishes strong connection:

- change angle or remove the connection

If the legs separate:

- exploit the exposed space

If the opponent turns strongly:

- follow the rotation into the available passing route

If the opponent becomes static:

- convert mobility into pressure

## Failure Modes

- confusing mobility with uncontrolled movement
- allowing the centre of mass to drift beyond the base
- moving without monitoring the legs
- becoming excessively upright
- failing to convert openings into progression

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** grips can supplement balance and directional control.

**No-Gi:** base, weight transfer and connection management carry more of the control burden.

## Synochi Read

Float Passing is a **dynamic control state**.

Control is created through continuous adjustment rather than immediate immobilisation.

**Base stability + weight placement + reaction speed + opponent reorganisation**

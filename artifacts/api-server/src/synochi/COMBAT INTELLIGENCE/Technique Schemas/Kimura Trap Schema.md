# Kimura Trap Schema

## Definition

The Kimura Trap is a control and transition system built around maintaining a figure-four grip on the opponent's arm while using that attachment to control rotation and access multiple positional outcomes.

The Kimura grip is therefore used as more than a submission.

It becomes a control interface.

## Primary Objective

Maintain ownership of the opponent's arm while using its connection to control shoulder rotation and guide positional transitions.

## Core Mechanics

1. Establish the Kimura grip.
2. Keep the opponent's elbow separated from a strong defensive position.
3. Use the arm to influence shoulder rotation.
4. Maintain connection during positional movement.
5. Follow the opponent's attempts to turn or escape.
6. Use their reaction to expose positional transitions.
7. Preserve the grip while moving between control states.
8. Finish or transition according to the resulting structure.

## Key Principle

**The grip creates a control relationship that can survive positional change.**

## Decision Rules

If the opponent turns away:

- use the grip to follow and expose the back

If the opponent turns toward the attacker:

- maintain shoulder control and redirect their rotation

If the arm becomes isolated:

- transition toward the finish

If the positional route becomes stronger than the submission:

- preserve control and take the position

## Failure Modes

- treating the Kimura only as a submission attempt
- losing elbow control during movement
- holding the wrist while losing torso connection
- forcing the finish when a stronger positional transition exists
- releasing the grip during useful opponent movement

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** additional grips may reinforce positional control.

**No-Gi:** the figure-four itself becomes a particularly valuable persistent connection.

## Synochi Read

The Kimura Trap is a **control-migration schema**.

A single attachment can remain useful while the positional state changes around it.

# Kimura Finishing Schema

## Definition

Kimura Finishing is a rotational shoulder-lock structure created by controlling the opponent's wrist and connecting the attacker's arms around the opponent's upper arm.

The submission depends on isolating the shoulder and preventing the torso from rotating with the trapped arm.

## Primary Objective

Create rotational separation between the opponent's arm and torso while maintaining control of the elbow and shoulder.

## Core Mechanics

1. Control the opponent's wrist.
2. Secure the figure-four connection.
3. Keep the elbow structurally isolated.
4. Restrict torso rotation.
5. Position the opponent's hand away from their defensive alignment.
6. Create controlled internal shoulder rotation.
7. Maintain wrist and elbow alignment.
8. Apply pressure progressively.

## Key Principle

**The shoulder locks when the arm rotates independently of the torso.**

## Decision Rules

If the opponent rotates their whole body:

- control the torso before continuing

If the elbow slips position:

- restore elbow control

If the opponent connects their hands defensively:

- treat the grip as a new control problem rather than forcing rotation

If the shoulder becomes isolated:

- apply finishing pressure gradually

## Failure Modes

- rotating the wrist without controlling the elbow
- allowing the torso to rotate freely
- relying on arm strength
- losing the figure-four connection
- applying uncontrolled finishing pressure

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** sleeve and belt grips can affect defensive hand connections.

**No-Gi:** wrist control and body positioning become more important.

## Synochi Read

Kimura Finishing is a **rotational isolation schema**.

**Wrist control → elbow isolation → torso restriction → shoulder rotation**

# X Guard Entry Schema

## Definition

X Guard Entry is the process of moving underneath the opponent's base and establishing opposing hook structures that control both the near and far sides of their stance.

## Primary Objective

Gain enough lower-body connection to manipulate the opponent's centre of mass and disrupt stable standing alignment.

## Core Mechanics

1. Create access beneath the opponent's base.
2. Establish connection to one leg.
3. Move the hips underneath the opponent.
4. Insert the secondary hook.
5. Organise the legs into the X Guard structure.
6. Control the opponent's distance.
7. Prevent immediate disengagement.
8. Begin off-balancing or transition.

## Key Principle

**X Guard requires depth beneath the opponent, not just contact with the legs.**

## Decision Rules

If the opponent retreats:

- maintain connection and follow the base

If one hook cannot be established:

- stabilise the first connection before progressing

If the opponent's balance breaks:

- transition immediately into the appropriate sweep or attack

If the opponent changes stance:

- reorganise the hook configuration rather than forcing the original geometry

## Failure Modes

- entering without sufficient hip depth
- weak connection to the opponent's leg
- allowing excessive distance
- establishing hooks without controlling the base
- failing to act after off-balancing occurs

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** grips can reinforce control of posture and distance.

**No-Gi:** leg connection and body position carry more of the control burden.

## Synochi Read

X Guard Entry is a **base-access schema**.

The entry converts proximity beneath the opponent into structural control over their balance.

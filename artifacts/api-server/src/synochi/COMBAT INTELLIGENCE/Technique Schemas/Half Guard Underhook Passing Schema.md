# Half Guard Underhook Passing Schema

## Definition

Half Guard Underhook Passing is a pressure passing structure in which the passer wins upper-body inside position through an underhook and uses it to control torso rotation while freeing the trapped leg.

## Primary Objective

Prevent the opponent from turning toward the passer, building height or accessing the passer's back while progressively clearing Half Guard.

## Entry Conditions

The schema becomes available when:

- the passer is engaged in Half Guard
- an underhook can be established
- the opponent's torso can be connected to
- the passer can maintain stable head and hip position

## Core Mechanics

1. Establish the underhook.
2. Connect the shoulder and torso to the opponent.
3. Prevent the opponent from turning inward.
4. Flatten or redirect the upper body.
5. Control hip orientation.
6. Progress the trapped knee beyond the opponent's legs.
7. Free the lower leg.
8. Consolidate the resulting position.

## Key Principle

**Upper-body control creates lower-body freedom.**

The trapped leg becomes easier to extract when the opponent cannot rotate effectively.

## Decision Rules

If the opponent fights for their own underhook:

- prioritise the upper-body battle before leg extraction

If the opponent turns away:

- follow the rotation while preserving connection

If the opponent creates distance with frames:

- rebuild chest connection before progressing

If the knee becomes free:

- consolidate before chasing further advancement

## Failure Modes

- extracting the leg before controlling the torso
- loose underhook connection
- allowing the opponent to build onto an elbow
- allowing the opponent's hips to rotate freely
- abandoning head position

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** lapel and belt controls can reinforce upper-body connection.

**No-Gi:** underhook depth, head placement and chest pressure become more important.

## Synochi Read

Half Guard Underhook Passing is an **upper-body control to lower-body liberation sequence**.

**Inside access → rotational control → knee extraction → positional transition**

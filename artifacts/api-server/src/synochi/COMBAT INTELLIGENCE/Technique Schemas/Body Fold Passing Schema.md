# Body Fold Passing Schema

## Definition

Body Fold Passing is a compression-based passing structure in which the opponent's knees are driven toward the torso, reducing hip extension, rotation and independent leg movement.

The opponent's guard is converted from a mobile structure into a compressed defensive shape.

## Primary Objective

Reduce available hip range until the legs can no longer reorganise effectively.

## Entry Conditions

The schema becomes available when:

- both legs can be collected
- the knees can be compressed toward the torso
- the pelvis can be controlled
- the opponent cannot freely restore hip extension

## Core Mechanics

1. Collect the legs.
2. Compress the knees toward the torso.
3. Maintain control over the pelvis.
4. Prevent hip re-extension.
5. Redirect the compressed legs away from the intended passing direction.
6. Advance around the legs.
7. Clear the knee line.
8. Consolidate upper-body control.

## Key Principle

**Reduce range before attempting to pass through it.**

A mobile hip can repeatedly regenerate guard.

A compressed hip has fewer available responses.

## Decision Rules

If the opponent attempts to extend:

- maintain compression and stop the pelvis escaping

If the opponent rotates:

- follow the pelvis rather than chasing the feet

If the opponent begins to invert:

- prioritise pelvic control

If the legs separate:

- recollect them before continuing

## Failure Modes

- controlling the feet without controlling the hips
- allowing the opponent to regain extension
- moving around the legs before establishing compression
- failing to follow pelvic rotation
- allowing independent knee movement

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** trouser grips can increase persistence of leg control.

**No-Gi:** compression depends more on body position, forearm structure and torso pressure.

## Synochi Read

Body Fold Passing is a **degrees-of-freedom reduction schema**.

As hip extension, rotation and independent leg movement disappear, guard regeneration becomes harder.

# Turtle Breakdown Schema

## Definition

Turtle Breakdown is the process of converting a compact defensive Turtle into a structure that exposes the opponent's hips, shoulders, back or limbs.

The objective is not simply to push the opponent flat.

It is to remove the structural relationships that make Turtle stable.

## Primary Objective

Create asymmetry in the opponent's base and expose a controllable pathway to Back Control, Side Control or another dominant position.

## Core Mechanics

1. Identify the opponent's strongest posts and connections.
2. Control the hips or upper body.
3. Remove one defensive support.
4. Create rotational or directional imbalance.
5. Prevent the opponent from rebuilding their base.
6. Follow the collapse or turn.
7. Insert controlling attachments.
8. Consolidate the resulting position.

## Key Principle

**Turtle breaks when its support structure becomes asymmetric.**

## Decision Rules

If the opponent maintains strong elbow-knee connection:

- attack the support structure rather than forcing rotation

If one hip becomes exposed:

- control it before advancing

If the opponent rolls:

- follow the rotation and preserve connection

If the opponent stands:

- transition into the appropriate rear-control or mat-return structure

## Failure Modes

- pushing without removing structural supports
- allowing the opponent to rebuild base
- losing hip connection during rotation
- chasing the back before establishing control
- using force without directional imbalance

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** grips can provide strong handles for directional control.

**No-Gi:** wrist rides, hip control and body connection become more important.

## Synochi Read

Turtle Breakdown is a **support-removal problem**.

The objective is to identify which structural support currently stabilises the opponent and remove it.

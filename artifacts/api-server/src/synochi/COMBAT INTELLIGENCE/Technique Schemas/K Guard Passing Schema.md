# K Guard Passing Schema

## Definition

K Guard Passing is the process of denying the opponent the hip angle, leg access and rotational pathway required to convert K Guard into stronger attacks or leg entanglements.

## Primary Objective

Remove the opponent's access to the passer's leg while preventing rotational progression underneath the passer.

## Entry Conditions

The schema becomes available when:

- the opponent has established or is entering K Guard
- the passer can identify the controlling knee and foot structures
- the passer retains enough balance to deny deeper leg access
- the opponent has not yet completed a dominant entanglement

## Core Mechanics

1. Identify the opponent's primary connection to the leg.
2. Control the distance between the opponent's hips and the trapped leg.
3. Prevent deeper access behind the knee.
4. Restrict the opponent's rotational direction.
5. Remove or redirect the controlling leg.
6. Restore the passer's knee line.
7. Prevent re-entry.
8. Convert into passing pressure.

## Key Principle

**K Guard becomes dangerous when access and rotation combine.**

Deny either component early and the structure loses much of its progression.

## Decision Rules

If the opponent gains deeper leg access:

- prioritise knee-line recovery

If the opponent begins rotating underneath:

- control the rotational pathway before advancing

If the primary connection breaks:

- immediately increase distance or pressure to prevent re-entry

If the opponent transitions into a leg entanglement:

- treat the position as the new state rather than continuing the K Guard sequence

## Failure Modes

- allowing the knee line to become deeply exposed
- focusing on the foot while ignoring hip rotation
- attempting to pass while the opponent still has deep access
- failing to recognise transition into another leg entanglement
- leaving re-entry space after clearing the guard

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** grips can alter the opponent's ability to maintain upper-body connection.

**No-Gi:** K Guard commonly links directly into leg-entanglement systems, making control of the knee line particularly important.

## Synochi Read

K Guard Passing is an **access-denial and rotational-control problem**.

**Deny access → stop rotation → recover alignment → prevent re-entry → pass**

# Back Control Maintenance Schema

## Definition

Back Control Maintenance is the process of preserving chest-to-back connection, rotational control and lower-body attachment while the opponent attempts to turn, clear hooks or bring their shoulders to the mat.

## Primary Objective

Prevent the opponent from separating the attacker's upper-body control from lower-body control.

## Core Mechanics

1. Maintain chest-to-back connection.
2. Control shoulder rotation.
3. Preserve at least one effective lower-body attachment.
4. Follow hip movement without losing upper-body alignment.
5. Prevent the opponent from bringing both shoulders safely to the mat.
6. Rebuild hooks or body control as attachments are challenged.
7. Maintain head position relative to the opponent.
8. Transition to finishing attacks only when control is stable.

## Key Principle

**Back Control is maintained by preserving alignment between upper-body connection and lower-body attachment.**

## Decision Rules

If a hook is cleared:

- preserve chest connection and replace lower-body control

If the opponent turns toward the mat:

- follow shoulder rotation and maintain upper-body attachment

If the opponent reaches the safe side:

- prioritise preventing full shoulder clearance

If the opponent becomes static:

- increase offensive pressure

## Failure Modes

- chasing hooks while losing chest connection
- allowing both shoulders to reach the mat
- separating upper- and lower-body control
- attacking before positional alignment is stable
- allowing head position to drift

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** collar controls create additional finishing and maintenance options.

**No-Gi:** chest connection, seatbelt-style upper-body control and hip attachment become more important.

## Synochi Read

Back Control Maintenance is an **attachment integrity problem**.

Control weakens when upper-body and lower-body attachments separate.

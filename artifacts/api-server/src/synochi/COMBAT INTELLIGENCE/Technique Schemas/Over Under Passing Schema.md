# Over Under Passing Schema

## Definition

Over Under Passing is an asymmetric pressure passing structure in which one leg is controlled from underneath while the opposite leg is controlled from above.

The asymmetry creates rotational control of the opponent's pelvis.

## Primary Objective

Split the opponent's leg configuration and rotate their hips away from an effective guard structure.

## Entry Conditions

The schema becomes available when:

- one arm can enter underneath a leg
- the opposite leg can be pinned or wrapped from above
- the passer can establish chest-to-thigh or shoulder-to-hip connection
- the opponent's knees are no longer aligned symmetrically

## Core Mechanics

1. Secure one leg underneath.
2. Control the opposite leg from above.
3. Keep the head and shoulder connected to the opponent.
4. Flatten or rotate the pelvis.
5. Walk the underhooked leg away from the opponent's centre.
6. Clear the knee line.
7. Progress the upper body beyond the hips.
8. Consolidate side control or another dominant position.

## Key Principle

**The pass works by controlling opposing directions at the same time.**

One leg is elevated or collected while the other is pinned.

## Decision Rules

If the opponent turns toward the underhooked side:

- increase shoulder pressure
- use the rotation to flatten the hips

If the opponent turns away:

- maintain leg control and follow the pelvis

If the knee begins recovering inside:

- re-establish control before advancing the torso

## Failure Modes

- allowing the underhooked leg to become loose
- keeping the head too far away from the opponent
- moving the torso before controlling the pelvis
- failing to clear the knee line completely
- allowing the opponent to square their hips

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** trouser grips and lapel control may reinforce the asymmetric structure.

**No-Gi:** elbow clamps, shoulder positioning and hip connection become more important.

## Synochi Read

Over Under Passing is a **pelvic asymmetry schema**.

The passer wins by preventing the opponent's legs from functioning as a coordinated pair.

# Double Under Passing Schema

## Definition

Double Under Passing is a pressure passing structure built around controlling both legs from underneath, elevating the opponent's hips, compressing their knees toward their torso, and removing their ability to freely rotate or re-guard.

The pass succeeds by turning leg mobility into spinal and hip compression.

## Primary Objective

Convert bilateral leg control into upper-body exposure while preventing the opponent from recovering hip angle.

## Entry Conditions

The schema becomes available when:

- both legs can be collected from underneath
- the opponent's hips can be elevated or folded
- the passer's head and shoulders can enter beneath the leg line
- the opponent cannot freely retract both knees

## Core Mechanics

1. Secure both legs above the knees.
2. Bring the opponent's hips off the floor where possible.
3. Compress the knees toward the chest.
4. Remove lateral hip mobility.
5. Angle the opponent's legs away from the intended passing side.
6. Advance chest and shoulder position beyond the knees.
7. Clear the legs while maintaining compression.
8. Consolidate upper-body control before releasing the legs.

## Key Principle

**Do not try to outrun the legs. Make the legs unable to move independently.**

The pass becomes easier as the opponent's pelvis loses freedom.

## Decision Rules

If the opponent frames strongly with the arms:

- maintain lower-body compression
- progress incrementally rather than abandoning the position

If the opponent turns onto one hip:

- redirect the knees toward the opposite shoulder
- use the rotation to expose a passing lane

If the opponent begins inverting:

- control the pelvis before attempting to clear the legs

## Failure Modes

- controlling the ankles instead of the upper legs
- allowing the hips to remain flat and mobile
- releasing compression before establishing torso control
- driving directly forward without creating an angle
- allowing one knee to escape independently

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** trouser and belt grips may increase control of the legs and hips.

**No-Gi:** control relies more heavily on elbow position, shoulder pressure, hip elevation and connection to the hamstrings.

## Synochi Read

Double Under Passing is a **mobility-compression problem**.

As pelvic freedom decreases, passing probability increases.

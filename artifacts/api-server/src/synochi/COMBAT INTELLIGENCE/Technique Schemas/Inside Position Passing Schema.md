# Inside Position Passing Schema

## Definition

Inside Position Passing is a passing strategy built around occupying the space between the opponent's limbs and torso before attempting positional advancement.

Rather than attacking the perimeter of the guard, the passer progressively wins access to the opponent's internal defensive space.

## Primary Objective

Establish controlling structures inside the opponent's frames so that their knees and feet can no longer maintain effective separation.

## Entry Conditions

The schema becomes available when:

- access exists between the opponent's knees or elbows
- the passer can establish meaningful internal control
- the opponent's frames can be separated
- progression can occur without sacrificing base

## Core Mechanics

1. Identify the opponent's strongest internal frames.
2. Insert hands, arms, knees or torso into available inside space.
3. Prevent the opponent from recovering equivalent inside position.
4. Separate or redirect the defensive limbs.
5. Connect to the hips or torso.
6. Advance beyond the knee line.
7. Convert inside access into pressure or positional control.
8. Consolidate the pass.

## Key Principle

**Inside position creates access before progression.**

Passing becomes easier when the opponent must defend from outside the passer's structure.

## Decision Rules

If the opponent closes the inside space:

- create angle or movement before attempting re-entry

If one side becomes available:

- prioritise asymmetric control rather than forcing bilateral access

If the opponent retreats their knees:

- use the gained space to advance position

If the opponent establishes stronger internal frames:

- resolve inside position before continuing forward

## Failure Modes

- entering without stable base
- reaching inside without controlling distance
- progressing while the opponent still owns the central space
- allowing recovered knees to replace lost frames
- treating inside position as static rather than contested

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** grips can reinforce internal control after access is established.

**No-Gi:** wedges, limb positioning and body connection become more important.

## Synochi Read

Inside Position Passing is an **access-control schema**.

The passer progressively acquires access to spaces the opponent requires for defensive organisation.

**Access → internal control → structural disruption → progression**

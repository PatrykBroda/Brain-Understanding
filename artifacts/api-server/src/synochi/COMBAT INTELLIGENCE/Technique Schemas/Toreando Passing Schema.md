# Toreando Passing Schema

## Definition

Toreando Passing is an outside passing structure in which the passer redirects the opponent's legs while moving laterally around the guard.

The technique separates leg orientation from hip orientation.

## Primary Objective

Move beyond the opponent's effective leg line before they can reorient the hips and knees toward the passer.

## Entry Conditions

The schema becomes available when:

- the legs can be controlled from outside
- lateral movement is available
- the opponent's feet are not maintaining dominant connection
- the knees can be redirected away from the intended movement path

## Core Mechanics

1. Establish control over the legs.
2. Create useful extension.
3. Redirect the knees laterally.
4. Move the passer's body in the opposite direction.
5. Stay outside the effective leg line.
6. Prevent the hips from re-squaring.
7. Close distance once the legs are cleared.
8. Consolidate control.

## Key Principle

**The hands redirect. The feet complete the pass.**

## Decision Rules

If the opponent follows with the hips:

- continue lateral movement

If the opponent retracts the knees:

- close distance and change passing range

If the legs collapse together:

- transition toward compression-based passing

If the opponent establishes strong connection:

- resolve the connection before continuing to circle

## Failure Modes

- moving the legs without moving the feet
- stopping after clearing the feet
- allowing the hips to re-square
- reaching from excessive distance
- trying to circle while strongly connected

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** trouser grips provide persistent distal control.

**No-Gi:** control is more transient, making timing and movement speed more important.

## Synochi Read

Toreando Passing is a **reorientation race**.

**Passer lateral movement speed vs opponent hip reorientation speed.**

The pass appears when the passer moves outside the opponent's effective defensive orientation.

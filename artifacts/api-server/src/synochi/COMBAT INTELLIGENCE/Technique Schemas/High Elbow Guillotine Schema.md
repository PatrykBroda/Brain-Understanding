# High Elbow Guillotine Schema

## Definition

The High Elbow Guillotine is a guillotine finishing configuration in which the choking-side elbow rises over or around the opponent's shoulder, increasing closure around the neck and making traditional defensive hand positioning more difficult.

## Primary Objective

Create a tighter finishing structure by closing the space around the neck while obstructing the opponent's ability to address the choking arm.

## Core Mechanics

1. Establish deep neck control.
2. Secure the choking hand.
3. Bring the choking-side elbow high.
4. Close the space around the opponent's head and shoulder.
5. Maintain head control.
6. Prevent posture recovery.
7. Align the torso with the finishing direction.
8. Apply progressive compression.

## Key Principle

**The elevated elbow changes the defensive geometry.**

The opponent has less direct access to the choking structure while the neck becomes more tightly enclosed.

## Decision Rules

If the opponent attacks the choking wrist:

- maintain elbow height and protect the grip

If the opponent drives forward:

- preserve neck depth and adjust body angle

If the opponent begins rotating:

- follow the head rather than allowing the neck to slip free

If grip depth deteriorates:

- rebuild before increasing force

## Failure Modes

- raising the elbow before establishing neck depth
- leaving space around the neck
- losing control of the head
- relying on arm flexion alone
- sacrificing position to maintain the grip

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** fabric friction may change how the opponent's head and shoulders move.

**No-Gi:** the configuration is particularly valuable because the elevated elbow helps protect the choking structure without fabric grips.

## Synochi Read

The High Elbow Guillotine is a **defensive-access restriction schema**.

The finishing configuration improves not only compression but also the opponent's difficulty accessing the mechanism producing it.

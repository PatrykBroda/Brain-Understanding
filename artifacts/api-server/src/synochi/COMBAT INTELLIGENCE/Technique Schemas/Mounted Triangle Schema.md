# Mounted Triangle Schema

## Definition

Mounted Triangle is a triangle finishing structure created from top Mount in which the opponent's head and one arm are isolated inside the attacker's legs.

The position combines submission threat with continued top control.

## Primary Objective

Isolate the head and arm while maintaining enough positional dominance that the opponent cannot use posture or gravity to relieve the strangulation.

## Core Mechanics

1. Control the opponent's upper-body frames.
2. Isolate one arm across the body.
3. Position one leg around the neck.
4. Bring the second leg into the triangle configuration.
5. Maintain control of the opponent's head and shoulder.
6. Adjust angle and leg position.
7. Remove remaining space.
8. Finish while preserving positional control.

## Key Principle

**Use Mount to build the triangle rather than abandoning Mount to chase it.**

## Decision Rules

If the opponent's arm remains structurally strong:

- improve isolation before committing the legs

If the opponent turns:

- follow their shoulder orientation

If the triangle is structurally secure:

- adjust angle before applying maximal pressure

If the finish deteriorates:

- retain Mount rather than falling into a weaker position unnecessarily

## Failure Modes

- throwing the leg over before controlling the arm
- losing Mount during entry
- poor shoulder positioning
- staying square when angle adjustment is available
- prioritising the submission over positional integrity

## Gi / No-Gi Distinction

**Gi:** grips can assist arm isolation and posture control.

**No-Gi:** pressure, wedges and upper-body control become more important during entry.

## Synochi Read

Mounted Triangle is a **dominant-position-to-submission conversion schema**.

The submission should emerge from accumulated control rather than require sacrificing it.

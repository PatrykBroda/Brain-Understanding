FRAME MMA — ONBOARDING + INITIAL CHAT FLOW

Core principle:

The onboarding should not feel like account creation.
It should feel like athlete calibration.

The goal is:

* low friction
* high signal
* immediate psychological buy-in
* fast entry into the system

Target onboarding completion time:
60–90 seconds maximum.

No long forms.
No tutorials.
No wellness/productivity app feeling.

The athlete should feel:
“This system is already profiling how I train and compete.”

⸻

ONBOARDING FLOW

SCREEN 1 — Identity

Fields:

* Name / Callsign
* Age
* Weight
* Primary Art
* Skill Level
* Training Frequency
* Competition Experience

UI direction:

* clean
* minimal
* dark
* restrained
* mostly dropdowns/toggles
* minimal typing

Suggested header copy:
“Initialise athlete model.”

Subtext:
“This is not a profile. It is the foundation for how FRAME coaches you.”

⸻

SCREEN 2 — Archetype Calibration

Question:

“What style do you naturally fall into under live pressure?”

Options:

* Top pressure
* Guard player
* Wrestling-based
* Scramble-heavy
* Submission hunter
* Control-focused
* Speed / movement
* Defensive / reactive
* Don’t know yet

Purpose:
This gives FRAME a first behavioural signal without over-analysing the athlete.

It allows the system to estimate:

* pacing tendencies
* confidence style
* likely strategic preferences
* energy usage
* probable coaching direction

Important:
Do NOT frame this as a permanent identity.
This is only an initial signal.

⸻

POST-ONBOARDING TRANSITION

After onboarding finishes:

* do NOT show a dashboard first
* do NOT show tutorials
* do NOT show features

The athlete should enter directly into chat.

The first thing visible should be a message from FRAME.

This message is extremely important.

The purpose is to:

* create immediate trust
* reflect back the onboarding signals
* explain how FRAME thinks
* establish coaching tone
* make the athlete feel understood quickly

Tone direction:

* calm
* observant
* direct
* performance-oriented
* restrained
* intelligent
* not robotic
* not motivational
* not therapeutic

⸻

INITIAL CHAT MESSAGE STRUCTURE

1. Welcome
2. Reflect onboarding signals
3. Explain what FRAME currently knows
4. Explain that the model sharpens over time
5. Explain FRAME philosophy briefly
6. Invite interaction

⸻

EXAMPLE INITIAL MESSAGE

“Welcome to FRAME.

You’re a blue belt training 3–4 times per week with competition experience. Your natural tendency under pressure is to create movement and transitions rather than slow exchanges down.

That already tells me a few things:

* you likely perform best when reactions stay instinctive
* your confidence probably rises with momentum
* your game is strongest when you commit early instead of hesitating mid-transition

Right now I only have a few signals.
That’s enough to begin building your athlete model.

As we train together, the model sharpens:

* decision patterns
* pressure responses
* recurring breakdowns
* tactical strengths
* competition behaviour

FRAME is not designed to overload you with information.

The goal is simple:
reduce noise,
increase clarity,
improve execution under pressure.

Start anywhere:

* recent session
* competition prep
* recurring problem
* something that finally clicked”

⸻

IMPORTANT UX PRINCIPLES

1. Fast entry into the system
    The athlete should reach chat almost immediately.
2. No excessive psychological profiling
    Avoid fake precision or overly clinical questioning.
3. No therapist energy
    FRAME should always feel performance-based and grounded.
4. Minimal UI noise
    The onboarding should feel confident through restraint.
5. The athlete should immediately feel:
    “This is different from ChatGPT.”
